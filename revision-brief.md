# Revision Brief — August 2026

**What was changed across the vision documents, why, and what is still outstanding**

Prompted by two observations: that the accessibility and learning design portfolio is more capable than the documents describing it, and that a plan with a twenty-five-year horizon had no stated position on money, security, or optionality.

---

## 1. The diagnosis, in four lines

- **The CPACC was in the wrong phase.** `the-sequence.html` had it in Phase one; `my-vision.html`, `checklist.html`, and `deployment.html` had it in Phase two. That is exactly the cross-file drift the fact-checking standard ranks as risk 8 — and here the drift was hiding a real strategic error, since it is the only credential in the plan that changes what can be earned during the current job search.
- **The landscape was undersold.** Roughly a hundred mapped contributions across five branches were being described as "core income," "the day job," and "the income that funds the credential years."
- **There was no stability thesis.** Money appeared once, as a planning task deferred to Phase three. The largest financial exposure in the plan — a cohort-based master's with several hundred daytime practicum hours — was a single line.
- **The destination was described as deployment, not as choice.** The far-side documents describe the work beautifully. They do not describe the freedom to decline it.

---

## 2. Changes made

### New — `the-strategy-layer.md`

The document the portfolio did not have. It states the principle that stability is a condition of contribution rather than a compromise with it, defines the destination as optionality across four modes (the chooser, the room, the lectern, the studio), reclassifies accessibility and learning design as a load-bearing column, and sets out a financial architecture: three income engines, five money seasons mapped onto the phases, one named exposure, eight ranked leverage moves, and five decision rules. It closes with a verification appendix in the house format, including six unverified items that must be checked before any of it is acted on.

Two things it deliberately does not contain: invented numbers, and anything resembling investment advice. The numbers depend on facts about the life being built that no external source holds; they should be worked out once and reviewed annually.

### Revised — `three-assets.md` (now v2)

Accessible Learning Labs is rewritten from the stream that funds the others into the second column. Its *Ground* section now carries three concrete moves — CPACC now, publish the four written resources, reprice as a specialism — and its later phases carry the licensed-methodology and teaching-stream threads. A sixth phase, **Optionality**, is added to every asset and to the summary table. A third design choice is added: build at least one thing that earns without your presence. The v1 text is preserved as `three-assets-v1-backup.md`.

### Revised — `my-vision.html`

A new **Phase One — Ground** section has been added to the education timeline, which previously jumped from the completed foundations straight to Phase two. The CPACC entry has been moved into it, rebadged *Phase one · now*, and rewritten to say plainly why it moved: it is the only credential on the list that changes what can be earned this year, and the disability–depression thread it keeps visible is real but is not the reason to do it now.

### Revised — `checklist.html`

The CPACC item has been moved out of the Leeds panel and into the Phase one panel as `cl-now-12`, with steps rewritten around confirming the session date, submitting the application, and running the twelve-week study design already drafted. Two dependent sentences were corrected: the Phase two panel intro no longer claims the credential, and the Phase one completion condition now includes it — with "none of these require a credential" rewritten to "none of these require enrolment, permission, or a change in circumstances," which is what was actually meant.

### Revised — `deployment.html`

The CPACC action has been moved from *New During Phase Two* into the *Now* panel and rewritten. It concedes the original observation — the Leeds overlap is real — and explains why the conclusion drawn from it was wrong: overlap argued for pairing, when the asymmetry in speed and cost argued for sequence.

## 3. Still outstanding

Ordered by value, not effort.

**Work out the two numbers.** The annual cost of the life actually wanted, and the size of the Phase three trough (tuition, plus earnings foregone, plus duration). Everything in `the-strategy-layer.md` §5 is structure waiting on these. One page of arithmetic, and it does not need to wait for anything.

**Verify the six items in the strategy layer's §10** before acting on the teaching-stream argument or the part-time-master's proposal. The claim that a BC counselling psychology master's can be taken part-time is currently an unchecked condition inside a projection, and the whole "shallow the trough" option rests on it.

**`node-map.html`.** Node `ck5` still reads "Begin CPACC Exam Preparation · near-term, 6–12 months," and `sk5` reads "CPACC prep underway." Update both to Phase one with the twelve-week design named, and consider adding nodes for the strategy layer: the financial architecture, the licensed methodology, and the teaching-stream route. The map currently has no node representing income at all, which is itself informative.

**`skills.html`.** Two competencies are missing from a document that otherwise maps this work carefully: **pricing and positioning a specialist practice**, and **productizing a methodology** — turning a training system into something licensable, with the intellectual-property and agreement questions that follow. Both are learnable, both are load-bearing for the far side, and neither currently appears.

**`essential-requirements.html`.** Unchanged here, and still carrying the scarcity claim drafted before the AFMC's May 2025 technical standards reform. An hour's revision, and it blocks publication of one of the four resources.

**`an-average-tuesday.md` and `about-the-whole-portfolio.md`.** Both are excellent and both are silent on the thing that makes the Tuesday possible. Two small additions rather than rewrites: in the Tuesday, a line acknowledging that the freedom to give the younger colleague forty minutes instead of twenty is something that was *built* — that the calendar is chosen; and in the About, a sentence in "What it adds up to" naming the learning-systems and accessibility work as one of the two things the career is made of rather than the route by which it arrived.

**A standing review.** The strategy layer should be re-read annually alongside the sequence. Its numbers decay faster than anything else in the portfolio, and the phase-closing conditions are the natural moment to check them.

## 4. One thing worth saying plainly

The rest of this portfolio is unusually honest about its own weaknesses — the thin evidence base, the field's politics, the fragility of the portfolio model, the imprecision of the theory of change. It has been markedly less honest about one thing: how much is already in hand.

A hundred mapped and cited contributions. Three scoped services. Four finished resources awaiting an editing pass. A running program with real participants. Two public writing practices. Fifteen years inside institutions complex enough that most people never learn to move knowledge through them at all.

That is not a runway. The correction these revisions make is not to add ambition to the plan; it is to stop describing a substantial existing practice as preparation for a future one — and to design the money so that the practice can go on choosing its own work for twenty-five years.
