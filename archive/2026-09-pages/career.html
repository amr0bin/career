One specialism, one doctorate, and a ceiling worth naming.

A Registered Clinical Counsellor in British Columbia specialising in disability and depression, working inside a university, holding a practice at Therapeutic Studio, and completing a doctorate — on the way to building the work on disability and mental health that does not yet exist in this province.

This document holds the plan in one hand. Every stage states what it requires to enter, what I build while I am in it, who helps, and the condition that closes it. It is written ceiling first: each stage names what it makes possible before it names what it costs. Where this page and an earlier one disagree, 05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md and 00-portfolio-spine-shared.md (13 September 2026) govern.

The destination

What this could become, stated at full size.

The work I want is clinical work aimed at disability: students and adults whose depression is entangled with a body or a mind that institutions were not built for. A university as the daily setting, because that is where those people are and where the accommodation systems that shape their lives are administered. A practice alongside it. A doctorate that lets me generate the evidence rather than only apply it. And the studio, held seriously throughout, because it is the thing the rest of this is built to protect.

At its ceiling, that becomes something that does not yet exist here: a clinician-researcher who holds the clinical hour, the accommodation file, and the research programme at once, and who uses that position to build the service, the evidence, and the standards that the next generation of disabled students will meet. Grace Wong Sneddon's fractional career — a counselling master's and clinical registration, an interdisciplinary PhD, an adjunct appointment, and a primary career in university equity and academic leadership — is the proof that the pieces can be held together. It is the starting shape, not the ceiling.

The clinical registration

A thesis-based counselling master's, then registration as a clinical counsellor. That is what opens the practice, the practicum supervision of counselling students, and the clinical standing the accessibility work needs. The art therapy diploma remains available as a second registration, taken if it serves the route chosen.

The doctorate

Wanted, and entered funded. Two routes are prepared for together: counselling psychology, which adds the protected title, the psychologist tier, and a clinical-academic career; or a practice-based or art-history doctorate, which opens an art department's lectern. The route is chosen in the master's second year, on evidence that does not exist yet. Most of the preparation serves both.

The specialism

Disability, and I already hold it. Roughly a hundred mapped contributions across accessibility, universal design, and learning systems; the essential-requirements corpus work; accommodation law and clinical placement access. It is what makes a clinician, a researcher, and a teacher each rare rather than one of many.

The ceiling

Founding and leading a university centre where access and mental health are one intake rather than two. Directing a counselling or accessibility service. A funded research programme — a tri-council grant line, and in time a research chair — on disability, accommodation, and depression. Standards seats that rewrite essential requirements for every cohort that follows. And a voice in how depression care scales — nationally and internationally — for the people it currently misses.

What multiplies it

Supervisors who want the question. Mentors and sponsors who nominate. An employer that is also the school. Funding that pays for the research rather than evenings absorbing it. Collaborators and co-authors. Every stage below names who helps, because the ceiling is reached with support, not in spite of its absence.

The record that says it is reachable

This plan is built by someone who has already designed her way through serious barriers more than once, largely without support, and over the past decade produced a body of applied work most specialists never assemble. The plan treats that record as the baseline.

Floor, named once: if a doctorate is never funded, the counselling registration, the practice, the university role, and sessional teaching still deliver most of this life.

The path

Six stages, each closing on a condition met.

Stages close on conditions rather than dates, so the plan moves at whatever pace the work allows and every review asks one answerable question: is this stage finished, and what does the next one open?

Stage one

Ground

Put the existing work on the record, and get inside a university.

This stage needs no enrolment and no permission. It is composed almost entirely of work already begun, and it makes every application that follows stronger.

What it makes possible

A role inside UBC or SFU where the existing expertise is the job description, a public record that hiring panels and supervisors can read, and the relationships that later write references and nominations.

The work

  • CPACC. Self-paced, against a published body of knowledge. It makes a decade of applied accessibility work legible to a hiring panel in a single line.
  • A Visual Communication Design certificate. Part-time, continuing studies — Emily Carr, BCIT, or UBC Extended Learning. It pays in the Groundwork workbooks, accessible document design in the advisor role, and the studio's public face, and it starts a design portfolio.
  • Publish the four accessibility resources. All written, none public. Revise the essential-requirements piece against the AFMC technical standards reform first.
  • Move the role inside UBC or SFU. Accessibility services, student services, or learning design — chosen for whether the employer can also be the school.
  • Outcome measurement in Make Space. A validated depression screen at the start and end of each cycle, which turns a practice into a programme with a defensible claim.
  • Crisis Centre of BC — Crisis Services Responder. Distress line and text support under staff supervision; accredited by the American Association of Suicidology and recognised across BC as relevant experience for counselling admission. It adds supervised one-to-one crisis skills and a reference letter to a record that already shows years of holding people in difficulty. Roughly 110 hours of training plus 32 supervised hours, then 250 service hours at four a week. Cohorts are limited, so the application goes in early.
  • Submit the conceptual paper. No affiliation required.

Who helps

A current mentor and a former employer, told plainly what the plan is. Current UBC and SFU accessibility advisors, met through CACUSS. The start of the nominators file: five named people who receive the record twice a year.

Complete when

The CPACC is held, the four resources are live, the paper is submitted, one Make Space cohort has pre- and post-data, the role is inside a post-secondary institution, and crisis-line shifts are underway.

Stage two

Frame — the disability master's

The degree that can be held alongside a full salary, and the one that finishes the corpus study.

MSc Disability Studies, Rights and Inclusion — University of Leeds, online, part-time over twenty-four months, with intakes in March and September 2027. Leeds has taught disability studies since 1990 and its Centre for Disability Studies was the first university centre in the UK dedicated to the field.

It is placed here because it fits beside full-time work, which makes this stage the accumulation window for everything after it. Leeds permits breaks between modules and allows up to four years, so the crisis-line hours and the counselling prerequisites fit inside the same stage.

What it makes possible

A finished, supervised, credited corpus study; international standing in disability studies and global mental health; a research-literate application to a thesis-based counselling master's; and a partner organisation on record.

Requires

A bachelor's degree with relevant experience — a 2:1 in any subject plus relevant experience, or a 2:2 with at least three years of it. Cleared comfortably.

The work

  • Nine taught modules of fifteen credits, eight weeks each: disability theory, human rights and the CRPD, policy reform, global challenges, research methods, and change-making practice.
  • The forty-five-credit applied project. Done in partnership with an organisation — a disabled people's organisation or the employer — this is where the essential-requirements corpus study is written, supervised, and finished.
  • The five undergraduate prerequisites alongside: interviewing skills, counselling theories, learning or cognitive psychology, abnormal psychology, and statistics — inventoried against the equivalency table first, complete by 31 December in the year of application.
  • Global mental health, formally. The LSHTM Global Mental Health module (GHM202: fifteen credits, online, October to June; the October 2027 intake, since the 2026 window closed on 14 September), with its policy report written on depression and disability. Then, as Leeds closes, the University of Glasgow Postgraduate Certificate in Global Mental Health — sixty credits, about twelve months part-time online, stackable later to a PgDip or MSc — in the year before the counselling application.
  • Research experience for the thesis route. A lab apprenticeship or research assistance with a UBC or SFU counselling faculty member whose work touches disability, and the first conversation with a prospective thesis supervisor.

Fees are payable module by module — around £1,170 per taught module and £2,220 for the project on currently published figures. Confirm the 2027 figure.

Who helps

A Leeds supervisor, who becomes an academic referee. A partner organisation — Disability Alliance BC or BCANDS — for the applied project. A counselling faculty member who has watched the research work.

Complete when

The applied project is submitted, the Global Mental Health PgCert is held, all five prerequisites are held, the 250 service hours are complete with the reference letter received, and a prospective thesis supervisor has agreed to a conversation.

Stage three

The Room — the counselling master's

Clinical formation, an empirical thesis, and the registration that opens the room.

A thesis-based counselling master's at UBC or SFU. The thesis is not optional: it is what keeps the doctorate open, and it is the single most consequential choice in this stage. The thesis tests the question at the centre of the whole plan: does the accommodation process itself shape depression and help-seeking among disabled students, and which parts of it are the levers? Documentation burden, wait times, denials, and unmet need, measured against depressive symptoms and use of campus mental health services. It is the highest-leverage question available: the levers are policies universities already control, they reach every disabled student at once, almost no one is studying them, and very few people hold both the accommodation expertise and the clinical training to do it well. It is also the evidence base for the joined access-and-mental-health centre at the top of this plan. The art route stays open through the studio record, not the thesis.

UBC or SFU from inside the employer is the strongest version of this stage: tuition benefit, flexible hours, and a practicum negotiated around a four-day week. Adler University and Athabasca are checked alongside, with one condition added: a programme without a thesis route closes the counselling doctorate and is weighed with that in view.

What it makes possible

Registration as a clinical counsellor, a first empirical thesis on the question the whole path is organised around, a supervisor who has seen the research, and the first tri-council funding on the record.

Requires

The five prerequisites, a B+ senior average, a criminal record clearance before the supervised placement, and an available thesis supervisor in the declared area — which is why that conversation starts in Stage two.

The work

  • Forty-eight credits of coursework and a six-credit thesis, with the thesis designed from the first term.
  • Practicum placements in post-secondary counselling and disability services.
  • Applications in the first term to SSHRC or CIHR master's awards, the BC Graduate Scholarship, and a Mitacs Accelerate partnership for the thesis.
  • A research assistantship on a project near the thesis — the most reliable route to a co-authored paper before the doctoral application.
  • Registration as a clinical counsellor applied for on conferral.

Who helps

The thesis supervisor. The practicum supervisors, who become clinical referees. Mitacs and a partner organisation, which fund the thesis. The employer, through the tuition benefit and a flexible arrangement set at the start of the stage.

Complete when

The thesis is defended, the degree is conferred, the practicum hours are complete, and the RCC application is submitted. The doctoral route is chosen in this stage's second year.

Stage four

Practice

Registration held, the practice open, and — if the route calls for it — a second registration.

Therapeutic Studio opens as a practice at RCC scope: depression and disability, sliding scale from the first client, with Creative Health Studio kept as the separate peer programme inside it. The Advanced Diploma in Art Therapy at the Vancouver Art Therapy Institute — eighteen months from July, applications by 30 March, a 600-hour placement — is taken here if the art route is chosen or if image-based work becomes part of the clinical offer; its creative project is designed as the first piece of a doctoral portfolio.

What it makes possible

Paid clinical work in the specialism, the first referral relationships, clinical data for the doctoral question, and — with the diploma — registration as a Canadian Art Therapist.

The work

  • The practice opened small and deliberately, with insurance, records, advertising compliance, and a dual-relationship policy set before the first client.
  • The doctoral application prepared: the supervisor, the funding package, the SSHRC or CIHR doctoral application in the autumn before entry.
  • If taken: the diploma, its placement in post-secondary or community mental health with a disability population, and RCAT registration on completion.

Who helps

A clinical supervisor for the early practice. The placement site, which becomes the first referral partner. The doctoral supervisor, already in conversation.

Complete when

Registration is held, the first clients have been seen, and the doctoral application is funded and accepted.

Stage five

The Question — the doctorate

Entered funded, with a supervisor who wants the question.

Counselling psychology or psychology at UBC or SFU: an empirical dissertation that moves from the thesis's levers to an intervention — testing a joined access-and-mental-health intake, or accessible low-intensity provision (creative health among the candidates) built to reach the students the thesis found left out; a CPA-accredited internship, served where possible at a campus counselling centre; the protected title; and eligibility for the psychologist tier and a clinical-academic career. Or a practice-based or art-history doctorate — SFU's Contemporary Arts or Interactive Arts and Technology, or UBC's Art History — where the studio's question is examined and an art department's lectern opens. The criteria for choosing are written in 05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md so the decision is made on evidence rather than on the mood of a single month.

What it makes possible

Research independence and a funded programme of work; the standing to lead services, supervise doctoral students, and sit on the bodies that write standards; and, on the counselling route, the protected title.

Requires

The thesis-based master's, a supervisor who wants the work, a publication record, a teaching dossier begun, and funding — a condition of entry with equal standing to the others. On the counselling route, an approved thesis proposal comes earlier than it seems: internship applications require one.

The work

  • Tri-council doctoral funding, and a nomination for the university's flagship doctoral award, asked for in the first term.
  • UBC's Public Scholars Initiative or SFU's Community-Engaged Research Initiative, with Make Space or a disability organisation as partner.
  • Teaching assistantships and the graduate teaching certificate, building the dossier an academic appointment reads.
  • The practice held small and the registration current; Make Space handed to a trained facilitator, which tests whether the method transfers.

Who helps

The supervisor and committee. The department that nominates. Research collaborators, a statistician, and co-authors. The nominators file, now a decade deep.

Complete when

The dissertation is defended — and on the counselling route, the internship is complete and registration as a psychologist is applied for.

Stage six

Optionality

Work chosen rather than accepted — and some of it built rather than applied for.

The fractional shape holds several seats at once, none of them full-time. Some already exist and are applied for; the most important ones are built, because the role this plan describes does not yet have a posting.

What it makes possible

  • A centre that does not yet exist. A university service or research centre where access and mental health share one intake — proposed, funded, and led.
  • Senior leadership. Director-level seats in accessibility, equity, or counselling services, which set policy on essential requirements for every programme at once.
  • A research programme. Tri-council grants, doctoral students of my own, and in time a research chair on disability, accommodation, and depression.
  • Standards and regulation. Accessibility standards committees, the College's registrant committees, and professional boards.
  • The lectern. Faculty, adjunct, or sessional teaching in a counselling department or an art department, and clinical supervision of students.
  • Therapeutic Studio and the studio. A practice sized by desire, and an art practice shown, funded, and taught from.
  • Global mental health. Advisory, research, and policy roles on how depression care scales, carrying the disability question into the field that sets the global agenda.

Who helps

Sponsors inside the university who carry a proposal to a dean. Funders. A team — clinicians, researchers, facilitators — who deliver the work when I am not in the room.

Complete when

It does not close. This is the practice.

Held throughout

Five practices that run at every stage.

These are the plan's raw material, and each one becomes something larger by being kept. What changes across the stages is their volume; each has a minimum rhythm that is defended.

Make Space

A weekly community creative-health programme with original conceptual tools, run as Creative Health Studio. With the screening instrument in place and a brief note after each session, it becomes an evidence base, then a manualised method that others deliver. Minimum rhythm: one session weekly.

The public writing

Practitioner-facing work on accessibility, disability, and learning design. It builds the readership that will later adopt and cite the research, and it is where each formal paper is first worked out. Minimum rhythm: one substantial piece monthly.

The consulting practice

Priced as a specialism: the scarcity is the intersection of disability studies, accommodation law, essential requirements, and learning design. It holds existing clients during coursework and carries the flexible income through the funded stages.

The depression lens

The WHO names depression a leading cause of disability worldwide. Using the 80,000 Hours framework — scale, neglectedness, tractability, fit — I build the knowledge deliberately: the Global Burden of Disease data, the WHO World Mental Health Report and mhGAP, the Lancet Commission, the scalable interventions (Problem Management Plus, the Healthy Activity Programme, the Friendship Bench), and their cost-effectiveness. Minimum rhythm: an annual depression brief, and 80,000 Hours advising to pressure-test the theory of change.

The studio

Dear Ordinary and the studies, on the standing order: Sunday ten until six for making, Monday evening for documentation, twenty minutes of drawing on the days between. It is a practice held for its own reasons on either doctoral route, and the catalogue never lapses.

What it is for

The specific gap this closes.

Disabled students report depression at higher rates than their non-disabled peers, and then meet a support structure that treats access and mental health as two separate offices with two separate intake forms. The counselling centre addresses the distress. The accessibility office addresses the accommodation. Very few people sit in either room able to see that a student's flattening mood and their unmet access need are the same problem viewed from two angles.

That is the gap, and it is a gap in combined expertise. A clinician who can hold a clinical hour, read a programme's essential requirements and say precisely why it is indefensible, and then produce the evidence that changes it, is close to unique in this province.

The reach is wider than a caseload. A student helped in the counselling room is one person. A professional programme's technical standards rewritten from method to construct changes who can enter that profession at all, for every cohort that follows. A centre that joins the two offices changes what every disabled student meets on arrival. The same expertise does all three, and the doctorate is what lets it generate the evidence rather than only apply it.

The art stream is the third place the gap appears. Art schools admit and assess on essential requirements too, and their students carry the same entanglement of mood and access, in a setting where the work itself is the body.

Working rules

How the plan is run.

  • Ceiling first, at every review. Each stage is reviewed by asking what it now makes possible before asking what it costs. Floors are named once, briefly.
  • Prepare the next stage in detail; keep the ceiling in view always. Detailed preparation is one stage ahead. The large ambitions — the centre, the research programme, the chair — are held in view from now, because the people who later sponsor them are met now.
  • Nothing on this path is a hoop. Abnormal psychology, learning theory, and statistics are the interesting part, arriving early. Statistics is what makes it possible to read a depression trial and judge it, and eventually to run one.
  • Every stage produces something citable and something visible. A published resource, a submitted paper, a thesis, a dataset; a catalogued body of studio work, shown.
  • Every stage names who helps. Supervisors, sponsors, funders, collaborators. The nominators file is kept current and sent the record twice a year.
  • Keep a decision log. One line per decision, dated: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why.

To confirm

What is set by others, and what I have not yet verified.

Each item is checked with the source institution rather than a secondary summary, and ordered by what it unlocks.

Verify first

Thesis routes. For UBC's and SFU's counselling programmes: whether a thesis route exists, whether it is elective or by admission stream, what it requires, and what each department says about doctoral progression. Asked of the graduate advisor directly, with answers recorded and dated.

Verify first

Thesis data and the dual role. Studying the accommodation process at one's own employer creates a dual-role and conflict-of-interest question for the ethics board. Routes to confirm: a study at another institution, recruitment through a student union or disabled students' association, a supervisor's existing dataset, or a national student survey with disability items (check whether the Canadian National College Health Assessment carries them). Settled with the prospective supervisor before the application.

Verify first

Staff study. Whether UBC and SFU staff can take the counselling master's while employed — tuition benefit, flexible hours, and a practicum around a four-day week. Established while there is still a choice of employer.

Verify first

What the doctorate confers. What a counselling psychology doctorate adds in BC beyond RCC registration, and the current requirements for registration as a psychologist.

Verify first

The art doors. Whether SFU's Contemporary Arts and Interactive Arts and Technology doctorates, and UBC's Art History doctorate, admit on a BFA plus professional practice without a master's in the discipline.

Verify first

Doctoral funding. Packages, tri-council eligibility, assistantship rates, and part-time provisions on each route.

Verify first

Global mental health qualifications. LSHTM GHM202: the fee for standalone study and the 2027 application date. University of Glasgow Global Mental Health PgCert: international fee, entry requirements, and how credits carry into the PgDip and MSc. Worth checking whether either counts toward CPACC recertification or the counselling application's research-experience line.

Verify first

Prerequisites. Which of the five the earlier UBC coursework already satisfies, against the published equivalency table, and whether admissions testing is currently required.

Verify first

Registration requirements. RCC with the BC Association of Clinical Counsellors and, if taken, RCAT with the Canadian Art Therapy Association.

Verify first

Crisis Centre application dates and training cost, and the comparable remote routes — the Crisis Centre for Northern BC and Crisis Text Line.

March and September 2027: the Leeds intakes. Confirm the 2027 fee.
