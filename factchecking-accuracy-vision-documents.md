# Fact-Checking & Accuracy Instructions — Vision and Pathway Documents

**Verification standards for `my-vision.html`, `node-map.html`, `checklist.html`, `the-sequence.html`, `deployment.html`, `skills.html`, `landscape.html`, `essential-requirements.html`, and the narrative companions**

Status: standing reference · applies to every pathway document before it is shared, and before it is acted on
Adapted from: `factchecking-accuracy-knowledge-resources.md` (itself adapted from `Fieldwork_Factchecking_Instructions.docx`)
Companion to: the knowledge-resource standard, which continues to govern the published portfolio

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

The knowledge-resource standard was written for artifacts that claim domain authority — pages that say *this is what the field knows*. These documents make a different kind of claim. They say *this is what I will do, and this is the terrain I will do it in.* That is not a lesser claim. It is a claim with a different failure mode.

**What this applies to:**

- Every vision, pathway, and planning document — the interactive HTML files and their linear or markdown companions
- The node map, including all claims embedded in the `NODES` array
- The checklist and sequence documents, which are the operational versions of the same claims
- Any of this material that becomes a personal statement, a supervisor enquiry, a funding application, or a page on the public site

**Why the standard is different here, not lower.** A knowledge resource that gets a citation wrong is embarrassing in front of people who know the literature. A pathway document that gets a programme requirement wrong is *acted on*. The cost is not reputational; it is an application submitted to the wrong programme, a prerequisite discovered too late, a year of supervised hours that do not count toward the registration they were accumulated for, or a course paid for that the receiving institution does not recognise. Errors here are expensive in the two currencies that matter most to this plan: time and money.

There is a second asymmetry. Knowledge-resource facts are mostly stable — Freire published in 1970 and will continue to have done so. Pathway facts decay on an annual cycle and sometimes faster. Programmes restructure, fees rise, admission requirements shift, regulatory colleges amalgamate. A verified fact in this portfolio is verified *as of a date*, and the date is part of the fact.

**The core test, restated for this artifact.** Not only *can an independent reader verify this claim* — but also: *if I acted on this sentence tomorrow, and it were wrong, what would it cost me?* Rank the checking effort accordingly.

## 2. The risk profile of these documents

Ranked by likelihood of error multiplied by cost when wrong. This ordering differs substantially from the knowledge-resource portfolio.

| Rank | Risk area | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Programme, course, and admission facts** | Named institutions and named courses, each with prerequisites, formats, fees, and application cycles. High volume, fast decay, and directly decision-bearing. The AI-drafted sentence here does not merely misinform — it misdirects. |
| 2 | **Regulatory and registration claims** | Titles, colleges, supervised-hour requirements, scope of practice, and what a given credential does and does not entitle. Jurisdiction-specific, actively changing in BC, and the area where being wrong is most costly and least recoverable. |
| 3 | **Plans presented in the grammar of facts** | Not a factual error but an accuracy failure with real consequences. A sequencing intention written as a settled pathway is the most common error in these files. See §4. |
| 4 | **Epidemiological and burden figures** | Treatment-gap percentages, prevalence, coverage rates, DALY claims. Rare in the prose and therefore conspicuous — and these are exactly the numbers a reviewer in global mental health will know cold. |
| 5 | **Framework and scholar attribution** | Patel, Araya, Fancourt & Finn, Cochrane, WHO frameworks, Messick, task-shifting literature. Wrong attribution is the error a prospective supervisor notices fastest. |
| 6 | **Claims about the practice work** | Make Space and any facilitation account. The only place where you are the sole source, and the place where clinical overclaim is easiest. See §7. |
| 7 | **Self-descriptive and biographical claims** | Years of experience, institutional descriptions, scope of past roles. Checkable by anyone who asks a former colleague. |
| 8 | **Cross-file drift** | The same claim stated differently in the vision, the node map, the checklist, and the sequence. Compounded by duplicate files and by claims buried in JavaScript. |

## 3. Programme, course, and admission facts

*The highest-risk area in these documents — a reversal from the knowledge-resource portfolio, where specific figures ranked first.*

Every named programme, course, credential, and institutional requirement is a factual claim about a live institution. Treat each one as unverified until confirmed against the institution's own current page.

**The verification unit.** For each named offering, confirm all of the following against the provider's site, not against a search result summary and never against AI-generated text:

- The course or programme still exists and still carries that name
- The delivery format claimed (online, in-person, hybrid, part-time) is current
- The prerequisites stated are the prerequisites listed
- The application cycle and deadlines, with the year attached
- Whether the credential is recognised by the body you intend it to satisfy — this is a separate question from whether the course is good

**Recognition is the claim most often assumed and least often checked.** A course being excellent, well-taught, and from a respected institution does not make it count toward anything. Wherever a document implies that credential A serves as a prerequisite for programme B, or as evidence toward registration C, that implication must be confirmed *with B or C*, not with A. The provider will always describe its own course favourably; only the receiving body can tell you whether it is accepted.

**Distinguish "leads to" from "helps with."** A short course that strengthens an application is not a course that qualifies you for admission. Both are worth doing. They are different sentences, and the difference determines whether a phase can close.

**Every programme fact carries a capture date.** Record the date checked alongside the claim. Where practical, archive the page — admissions and course pages change without notice and without changelogs, and a plan built on a page that has since been revised is a plan built on nothing recoverable.

**Fees, funding, and duration.** Where these appear, cite the institution's current published figure with the academic year named, and note the currency. Do not carry a fee forward from a previous year's page. Do not state a programme duration that assumes full-time study if the plan assumes part-time.

**Never state that no one has done something without saying how you looked.** The claim that a corpus study has not been done in Canada is a research claim, not a rhetorical one. Where it appears, attach the search that supports it — databases queried, terms used, date run — or soften to "as far as I have been able to establish," which is what the essential-requirements document already does correctly and which should be the house formulation everywhere.

## 4. Facts, plans, and intentions

*This section has no equivalent in the knowledge-resource standard. It is the most important adaptation.*

These documents are mostly about the future, and the future cannot be fact-checked. What can be checked is whether each sentence is honest about which kind of claim it is making. Three categories, and every sentence in these files belongs to exactly one:

**A verified fact.** Something true about the world right now, confirmed against a source, with a date attached. *LSHTM currently offers a short course in epidemiology for public health.* Fact-check normally.

**A stated intention.** Something you have decided to do. It cannot be wrong, only abandoned or revised. *I intend to complete the epidemiology and statistics short courses before applying to the MSc.* No verification needed — but it must be written so that no reader mistakes it for the next category.

**A conditional projection.** A claim that if X happens, Y follows. This is where the errors live, because a projection can be *unwarranted* even when every fact inside it is correct. *Full registration should be achievable by the end of the doctoral programme* is a projection. It depends on hour requirements, supervision availability, and a college's current rules — each of which is a separate factual claim that must hold, and each of which is checkable now.

**The working rule.** Wherever a projection appears, name the conditions it rests on, and check each condition as a fact. Where a condition cannot be confirmed, the projection stays but the grammar changes: "should be achievable" becomes "would be achievable if the current hour requirements hold." The plan survives either way. What does not survive an unmarked projection is your ability, three years from now, to reconstruct what you actually knew when you wrote it.

**A particular watch item.** The phase structure in these documents closes phases on conditions rather than durations — which is a good design and should be preserved. But a condition is only meaningful if it is verifiable. "Complete when the registration requirements are met" is a real condition. "Complete when I am ready" is not. Every phase-closing condition should name something a third party could confirm.

## 5. Regulatory, registration, and title claims

Titles and registration pathways are the highest-consequence factual terrain in these documents, and the BC landscape is actively changing.

**Rules:**

- Name the regulator correctly and currently, and check it before every use. Regulatory bodies in BC have been consolidating; a college named in a document written two years ago may no longer exist under that name. See Appendix A — this has already happened to a body named repeatedly across these files.
- Distinguish protected titles from occupational descriptions. In BC, *psychologist* is a protected title. *Counsellor*, historically, has not been in the same way — but this is exactly the kind of thing that changes, and it must be confirmed against current legislation rather than carried forward. Any document that describes future practice must be precise about which title is being claimed and when it would be held.
- **Never describe a registration pathway in the general.** Requirements for supervised hours, degree accreditation, and examination differ by profession, by province, and by year of application. A pathway sentence needs the regulator named, the profession named, the jurisdiction named, and the date checked.
- Where a document notes that requirements should be confirmed — as several of these correctly do — that note is a placeholder, not a discharge of the obligation. Convert each one into a dated confirmation or an open item in Appendix A.
- **Do not state that a non-clinical degree "may lead to" clinical registration** without naming the specific route and the body that recognises it. This is the single most consequential conditional in the entire plan. If the route does not exist as described, phases built on it do not close.

## 6. Epidemiological figures and burden claims

The house voice in these documents is qualitative and careful. A number therefore stands out, and stands out to precisely the audience — global mental health researchers, prospective supervisors — that will recognise it.

- Every figure about prevalence, treatment coverage, treatment gap, or disease burden is traced to a named source with an edition and a year. WHO reports and Global Burden of Disease estimates are revised; the figure and the edition travel together.
- **Treatment-gap figures are the recurring exposure.** Claims of the form *more than three quarters of people in low-income countries receive no care* are widely repeated and often repeated at the wrong magnitude, for the wrong condition, from the wrong year. Attach the specific source, the population it describes, and the year, or reformulate qualitatively. The argument does not weaken without the number.
- Distinguish *depression* from *mental disorders generally*, and *low-income countries* from *low- and middle-income countries*. Figures are routinely migrated across these boundaries in secondary sources. When you cite one, confirm which population the original measured.
- Never attach a figure to a named researcher unless the figure appears in that researcher's work. This remains the single most damaging error available, and it is checkable in one search by anyone who knows the scholar.
- Figures describing sector scale — programmes operating, coverage rates, workforce numbers — change annually. Cite the current edition of the publishing organisation's own report and diarise a re-check.

## 7. Claims about the practice work

The facilitation work is a first-person account of your own practice. The verification question is not *can a reader find the source* but *would this account survive contact with the people who were there* — and, additionally here, *does it claim a clinical standing not yet held*.

- **Label the practice work for what it is.** A facilitated group is not a clinical intervention, and a reader must never be able to mistake one for the other. Where the plan describes a future in which it becomes an evidenced intervention, the tense must make clear that this has not happened yet.
- **Do not attach outcome statistics to your own work unless the measurement actually happened and the instrument can be named.** Qualitative accounts of what shifted are credible and appropriate. Invented percentages are the fastest way to lose a reader who does this work — and in a mental health context, the fastest way to lose one who is responsible for participants.
- **Do not describe the work in therapeutic terms the credential does not support.** *Facilitation*, *group*, *participants* are accurate. *Treatment*, *therapy*, *clients*, *clinical outcomes* assert a standing that arrives later in the plan, if it arrives. This rule tightens rather than relaxes as the credentials accumulate: at each stage, the language must match the standing actually held on that date.
- Name partner institutions, funders, and community organisations only with permission. Where permission is not held, describe the setting by type.
- Describe participants in terms they would recognise and accept. Accounts involving people experiencing depression carry an obligation that goes beyond accuracy.
- Where a document claims something about an institution's programmes, compliance, or internal processes, that claim needs a source or must be softened.

## 8. Framework and scholar attribution

Same standard as the knowledge-resource portfolio, applied to a different literature.

- Verify the originator of every framework, term, and methodology before the document is shared. Locate the specific publication — do not attribute from general knowledge.
- **Attribution requires the right person, the right work, and the right publication venue.** Getting the authors right and the venue wrong is still an error, and in this field it is a conspicuous one: describing a report as having appeared in a journal it did not appear in signals that the work has not actually been read. See Appendix A for a live instance.
- Where a body of evidence is genuinely collective — task-shifting, creative health, the psychological treatment evidence base — describe it as a literature with named contributors rather than assigning it to the best-known name.
- Where a framework has been revised, cite the version being described, with its issuing body and date.
- **Never cite a source you have not read.** In a document whose purpose is to demonstrate research literacy to future supervisors, this rule is doing double duty.
- Claims about what a literature shows — *the evidence base supports X* — need either a systematic review cited by name or a softer formulation. "Cochrane reviews" as a bare gesture is not a citation.

## 9. Self-descriptive and biographical claims

- State years of experience, role scopes, and institutional relationships in terms a former employer would confirm without hesitation. Round down.
- Describe past roles by what you did, not by the most senior framing the facts will bear.
- Where a description of past work is doing argumentative work in the plan — establishing that a transition is credible, that a skill transfers — that is the description most likely to have drifted upward. Check it first.
- Degrees, institutions, and dates: exact.

## 10. Cross-file consistency

These documents restate the same plan in several registers, and restatement produces drift.

- **Claims embedded in JavaScript are content and are fact-checked as content.** The `NODES` array in `node-map.html` carries substantive factual claims — scholar names, framework descriptions, evidence characterisations — inside a data structure that no proofreading pass naturally reaches. Every node body goes through the same verification as prose. This is currently the least-checked and most claim-dense surface in the portfolio.
- **Resolve duplicate and near-duplicate files.** `deployment.html` / `deployment__1_.html`, `skills.html` / `Skills.html` (a case-only difference that will break on case-sensitive filesystems), `three-assets.md` / `three-assets-ten-years.md`, `an-average-tuesday.md` / `an-average-tuesday-the-whole-portfolio.md`. Determine which is canonical, mark or delete the other, and do it before the next correction pass rather than after.
- **The checklist and the vision must agree.** The checklist is the operational form of the vision's claims; where a phase condition is stated differently in the two, one is wrong. The same applies to the sequence document.
- **Correct every version in the same session.** A fix applied to one file and not its companion produces two conflicting plans, which is worse than one wrong plan because you will not know which you are working from.
- **Maintain a single source and fact register.** One running document listing every cited source, every named programme, every regulatory claim — with verified detail and date checked. Before adding a claim to a new document, check the register. When a claim recurs, copy the entry.
- Alt text and long descriptions are content and are fact-checked as content.

## 11. AI-assisted drafting

AI tools draft structure, argument, and prose well. They do not reliably produce accurate programme details, admission requirements, regulatory facts, citations, figures, or attributions.

Treat all factual content in an AI-generated draft as unverified: institution names, course names, prerequisites, fees, deadlines, registration requirements, scholar attributions, publication venues, and figures.

Failure modes to expect in this specific material:

- **A real institution with a course that does not exist**, or that existed until a recent restructure
- **Plausible but wrong prerequisites** — the requirements a programme of that type usually has, rather than the ones this one lists
- **Conflated registration pathways** — requirements from one province, profession, or year presented as another's
- **A real report attributed to the wrong venue** — the correct authors and year, the wrong journal or publisher
- **A treatment-gap or prevalence figure of roughly the right shape and the wrong magnitude**
- Confident sequencing that assumes a prerequisite relationship no institution has confirmed
- Institutional or biographical claims that cannot be confirmed

**The working rule, restated.** If a fact in a draft is load-bearing — if a phase closes on it, an application depends on it, or a decision follows from it — it is both the fact most likely to be wrong and the one most expensive to get wrong. Check it first.

## 12. Pre-circulation checklist

*Work through every item before any of this material is shared with a supervisor, an admissions committee, a funder, or the public site — and before a phase is treated as closed. All versions, same session.*

- [ ] Every named programme and course confirmed to exist, with current name, format, and prerequisites
- [ ] Every recognition claim confirmed with the *receiving* body, not the providing one
- [ ] Every fee, duration, and deadline carries the year it applies to
- [ ] Every programme fact carries a date checked; pages archived where practical
- [ ] Every regulator named correctly and currently
- [ ] Every registration claim names profession, jurisdiction, regulator, and date checked
- [ ] Every "confirm current requirements" note converted to a dated confirmation or logged as an open item
- [ ] Every sentence identifiable as fact, intention, or projection — and written in the grammar of the right one
- [ ] Every projection names the conditions it depends on, each checked as a fact
- [ ] Every phase-closing condition is something a third party could confirm
- [ ] Every epidemiological figure traced to a named source, edition, year, and population
- [ ] No figure attributed to a named researcher without confirming it appears in their work
- [ ] Every framework attribution has a named originating publication and the correct venue
- [ ] No source cited that has not been read
- [ ] Practice work described in language matching the standing actually held at that date; no invented outcome figures; partner names used only with permission
- [ ] Self-descriptive claims stated conservatively and confirmable by a third party
- [ ] Node map `NODES` array fact-checked as prose
- [ ] Duplicate files resolved
- [ ] Vision, checklist, and sequence agree on every phase condition
- [ ] All URLs resolve, in every version
- [ ] Source and fact register updated with anything new, including dates checked

## Appendix A: Outstanding verification items

*Identified in the current files. Unresolved as of this document's compilation.*

### Portfolio-wide — regulatory

- **"BC College of Psychologists" / "BC College of Psychologists' current requirements."** Appears repeatedly across `my-vision.html` and `checklist.html`, including in phase-closing conditions. **The body no longer exists under that name.** On June 28, 2024, the College of Psychologists of British Columbia amalgamated with six other colleges to form the College of Health and Care Professionals of BC (CHCPBC), which now regulates psychologists along with eight other professions. Two corrections follow: update every reference to the current regulator, and re-verify the registration and supervised-hour requirements themselves against CHCPBC, since amalgamation is exactly the circumstance in which requirements are restated. Note also that the former name was *College of Psychologists of British Columbia*, not *BC College of Psychologists* — the files use a word order that was never correct. **Highest-priority item in the portfolio**, because phase four closes on it.
- **"Clinical licensure can sometimes be pursued separately through a post-doctoral supervised practice pathway in some Canadian provinces."** Two hedges stacked on an unnamed route. Name the provinces, name the regulators, name the pathway — or remove the sentence. As written it cannot be acted on, and it may be carrying more optimism than the regulations support.
- **Route from a non-clinical doctorate to registration as a psychologist in BC.** Referenced as a possibility across the challenge options. Confirm with CHCPBC directly whether, and under what conditions, this route exists. This is the load-bearing assumption of the entire education path and it is currently unverified.

### `node-map.html`

- **"The Fancourt & Finn 2019 Lancet report."** The authors and year are plausible and the work is real, but the venue characterisation should be confirmed before this goes anywhere near a supervisor — the widely cited 2019 Fancourt & Finn evidence synthesis on arts and health was published as a WHO Regional Office for Europe Health Evidence Network synthesis report, not as a Lancet report. Confirm the correct title, series, number, and publisher, and correct every instance. This is the exemplary case of the §8 failure mode: right authors, right year, wrong venue.
- **Task-shifting node.** Attributes the framework area to Patel and Araya. Both are appropriate names, but confirm the specific publications being referenced and describe the literature as collective rather than as two people's work.
- **Every remaining node body.** The `NODES` array has not been through a verification pass. Treat it as unchecked prose.

### `my-vision.html`

- **"More than three quarters of people experiencing it in low-income countries receive no care at all."** Header claim, therefore the most-read sentence in the portfolio. Attach the source, edition, year, and the population the figure actually describes — and confirm whether the original measured depression specifically or mental disorders generally, and low-income or low- and middle-income countries.
- **The four LSHTM short courses and the Harvard programme.** Confirm each still runs, still under that name, still in the format described, and confirm the URLs resolve. Then confirm separately whether LSHTM treats the epidemiology and statistics short courses as recognised preparation for MSc admission, or merely as useful — the document currently implies the former.
- **"May lead to the same clinical registration pathway through supervised practice"** (Option D, interdisciplinary programmes). Same issue as above; confirm or soften.

### `essential-requirements.html`

- **TCPS 2 (2022), Article 2.2.** The reasoning is sound and correctly cautious. Confirm the article number against the current TCPS 2 text and confirm 2022 is the current edition, since the citation carries the study's entire ethics rationale.
- **"As far as I can establish, nobody has analysed them systematically in Canada."** Correctly hedged. Strengthen by recording the search — databases, terms, date — so the claim can be defended and re-run.

### Portfolio-wide — structural

- **Duplicate files.** Resolve `deployment.html` / `deployment__1_.html` and `skills.html` / `Skills.html` before the next correction pass. The case-only filename collision will cause silent problems on any case-insensitive filesystem.
- **Narrative variants.** Determine the relationship between `three-assets.md` / `three-assets-ten-years.md` and `an-average-tuesday.md` / `an-average-tuesday-the-whole-portfolio.md` — successive drafts or parallel pieces — and mark it.
- **Phase conditions.** Reconcile `my-vision.html`, `checklist.html`, and `the-sequence.html` line by line.
