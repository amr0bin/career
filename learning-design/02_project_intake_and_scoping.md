# 02 · Project Intake and Scoping

## Purpose of This Stage

The intake and scoping stage establishes the foundation for everything that follows. Done well, it prevents scope creep, misaligned expectations, and wasted effort. Done poorly, it leads to projects that drift, stall, or require painful correction mid-stream.

**Your goals at intake:**
1. Understand what is being asked and why
2. Identify who the learners are
3. Surface constraints (time, budget, technology, politics)
4. Establish whether this is the right fit for a full design engagement
5. Set expectations about the process, your role, and the SME's role

---

## The Intake Conversation

Before sending a form, start with a brief conversation (15–20 min). Forms capture information; conversations build trust and surface what people don't think to write down.

**Opening questions:**
- "Tell me about this course — what's prompting the request right now?"
- "What does success look like to you at the end of this engagement?"
- "Who are the learners, and what do you know about them?"
- "Has this been taught before? What's worked, and what hasn't?"
- "What's your timeline, and is there any flexibility there?"
- "Who else is involved or needs to be consulted?"

**Listen for:**
- The difference between what they *want* ("I want a video series") and what they *need* ("students aren't retaining the foundational concepts")
- Unstated constraints (a difficult colleague, a political history, a previous failed redesign)
- Signs that the scope is larger or smaller than initially presented
- Whether the SME has worked with a learning designer before

## Intake Form: Key Fields

Use this as a starting point for your intake form. Adapt fields to your institutional context.

### Project Basics
| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Course/program name | Include course code if applicable |
| Institution / faculty / department | |
| Delivery modality | Online / blended / in-person / hybrid / HyFlex |
| Anticipated enrolment | Per offering; per year |
| Target start date | When does the course need to be ready? |
| Pilot or full launch? | Is this a beta or a full cohort? |

### The Request
| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Type of engagement | New course / redesign / review / specific component |
| Driver for the request | Curriculum change / accreditation / low outcomes / new technology / faculty request |
| Is there a previous version of this course? | If yes, what materials exist? |
| What has been tried before? | What worked? What didn't? |
| Are there examples you admire? | Courses, resources, approaches |

### The Learners
| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Who are the learners? | Year of study, program, background |
| What do learners typically know coming in? | Prior knowledge and skills |
| What are common struggles or misconceptions? | Based on past experience |
| What contexts will learners apply this learning in? | Career, further study, civic life |
| Any known accessibility or equity considerations? | |

### Constraints and Logistics
| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Budget | If applicable |
| Technology available | LMS, authoring tools, media tools |
| Technology constraints | What can learners NOT be expected to have? |
| Team / collaborators | Who else will be involved (co-instructors, TAs, etc.)? |
| SME availability | Hours per week they can commit |
| Hard deadlines | Accreditation dates, academic calendar, program milestones |

## Scoping: Defining What's In and What's Out

After the intake conversation, document the scope clearly. This becomes part of your project plan.

### Scope Statement Template

> **This engagement will include:**
> [List the specific deliverables, stages, and support you will provide]
>
> **This engagement will NOT include:**
> [Explicitly name what is out of scope to prevent assumption creep]
>
> **Assumptions:**
> [What are you assuming to be true that, if untrue, would change the scope?]
>
> **Dependencies:**
> [What does this project depend on that is outside your control?]

## Red Flags at Intake

Watch for these signals that a project may need more careful scoping or escalation:

- ❌ "We just need someone to put it online" — often signals underestimation of design work involved
- ❌ "We need it in 2 weeks" for a full course build — timeline is unrealistic; address now
- ❌ "I already know exactly what I want" — may signal resistance to the design process; explore gently
- ❌ "The dean/provost wants this done" — political pressure can distort good design decisions
- ❌ "We've tried this before and it didn't work" — unpack what happened before proceeding
- ❌ No clear learning outcomes or goals — needs assessment is non-negotiable before scoping

## Setting Expectations with SMEs

At the end of intake/scoping, confirm these with your SME explicitly:

- [ ] The SME understands their role in the process (content expert, not sole decision-maker)
- [ ] The SME knows approximately how many hours of their time will be required
- [ ] Communication norms are established (how, how often, response times)
- [ ] The timeline is agreed and realistic
- [ ] The SME has seen examples of what the finished product might look like
- [ ] There is a clear decision-maker identified (especially for multi-stakeholder projects)
- [ ] Both parties have signed off on the scope document or project brief

## Useful Frameworks for Scoping

**MoSCoW Prioritisation** — categorise scope elements as:
- **Must have** — non-negotiable for the project to succeed
- **Should have** — important but not critical
- **Could have** — nice to have if time/resources allow
- **Won't have (this time)** — explicitly deferred to a future phase

**Effort vs. Impact Matrix** — plot potential design elements by effort required vs. learning impact. Focus energy on high-impact, feasible items first.
