# 01 · Workflow Reference: The 20-Stage Learning Design Process

## Overview

This workflow maps the full lifecycle of a learning design project — from initial request through to impact evaluation and case study. Each stage has a defined purpose, key inputs, expected outputs, and primary stakeholders.

Stages are numbered to reflect **logical sequence**, though in Agile/iterative practice some stages overlap, repeat, or run in parallel.

> ⚠️ **Not every project uses all 20 stages.** A small course review might only touch stages 3, 6, 12, and 15. Use judgment to scope appropriately.

## Stage-by-Stage Detail

### 1. FAQs
**Purpose:** A living document of frequently asked questions — useful for onboarding team members, managing stakeholder expectations, and reducing repeated conversations.
**Inputs:** Intake conversations, stakeholder questions over time
**Outputs:** FAQ document (updated throughout the project)
**Owner:** LXD maintains; SME and stakeholders contribute

---

### 2. Backlog
**Purpose:** A running, prioritised list of all tasks, open decisions, and future work. Central to Agile project management.
**Inputs:** All stages contribute items
**Outputs:** Sprint-ready task list, icebox items, decisions log
**Owner:** LXD owns; full team contributes
**Tools:** Trello, Jira, Microsoft Planner, Notion, Asana

---

### 3. New Request Info (Intake Form)
**Purpose:** Capture the initial project request in a structured way. Sets up the needs assessment conversation.
**Inputs:** Faculty/SME request (email, meeting, or form)
**Outputs:** Completed intake form
**Key fields to capture:**
- Course/program name and level
- Delivery modality (online, blended, in-person, hybrid)
- Driver for the request (new course, redesign, accreditation, etc.)
- Key constraints (timeline, budget, technology)
- Learner description (who, how many, context)
- Prior course history (what worked/didn't)

---

### 4. Overhead Plan
**Purpose:** Internal resource and capacity planning — time estimates, team assignments, fit with other active projects.
**Inputs:** Intake form, team capacity, institutional priorities
**Outputs:** Effort estimate, resource allocation, team assignments
**Owner:** LXD + team lead/manager

---

### 5. SME Onboarding
**Purpose:** Orient the subject matter expert to the design process, their role, and what to expect. Establishes norms for collaboration.
**Inputs:** Overhead plan, intake form
**Outputs:** Onboarding document or facilitated session
**Key topics:**
- Overview of the design process and timeline
- What the LXD does vs. what the SME does
- Communication norms (channels, frequency, turnaround expectations)
- Decision-making and sign-off process
- Examples of finished work

---

### 6. Needs Assessment and Analysis
**Purpose:** Deep-dive into the learning context — who learners are, what they need to do, what barriers exist, and what the course must accomplish.
**Inputs:** Intake form, SME conversations, existing course materials, learner data
**Outputs:** Needs assessment summary, learner personas, gap analysis
**Key areas to analyse:**
- Learner characteristics (prior knowledge, motivation, access)
- Current state vs. desired performance gap
- Contextual constraints (modality, time, technology)
- Institutional or program requirements
- Existing resources that can be repurposed

---

### 7. Assessment Report
**Purpose:** Document the assessment strategy — how learning will be measured, what types of assessments will be used, and how they align with outcomes.
**Inputs:** Needs assessment, draft or confirmed learning outcomes
**Outputs:** Assessment design document
**Contents:**
- Assessment philosophy and approach
- Mapping of assessments to outcomes (alignment table)
- Assessment types and rationale (formative, summative, authentic)
- Academic integrity considerations
- Grading and feedback strategy

---

### 8. Research Report
**Purpose:** Surface relevant pedagogical research, disciplinary context, and best practices to inform the design.
**Inputs:** Needs assessment, discipline area, specific design questions
**Outputs:** Research summary with design implications
**May cover:**
- Evidence-based approaches for the discipline or topic
- Learning science (cognitive load, retrieval practice, spaced learning)
- Comparable course designs or institutional case studies
- Technology or tool research

---

### 9. Project Plan
**Purpose:** Formal documentation of scope, timeline, milestones, deliverables, and responsibilities. The governing document for the project.
**Inputs:** All previous stages
**Outputs:** Project plan document
**Contents:**
- Scope statement (what's in / what's out)
- Key milestones and dates
- Deliverables list with owners
- Communication plan
- Risk register
- Approval and sign-off process

---

### 10. Storyboard
**Purpose:** Visual/structural representation of course content — how it flows, what's in each module, what activities and media are planned.
**Inputs:** Learning outcomes, needs assessment, research report
**Outputs:** Storyboard document (slide-by-slide or module-by-module)
**Formats:** PowerPoint outline, Word doc, Figma wireframe, storyboard template
**Owner:** LXD leads; SME reviews and contributes content

---

### 11. Learning Path
**Purpose:** Map the learner's journey through the course — sequence, prerequisite logic, key milestones, and progression.
**Inputs:** Storyboard, learning outcomes, assessment strategy
**Outputs:** Learning path diagram or document
**Owner:** LXD creates; SME and learner representatives review

---

### 12. Content Development
**Purpose:** Build the actual learning materials — written content, activities, media, assessments, and LMS course structure.
**Inputs:** Storyboard, learning path, approved project plan
**Outputs:** Draft course materials, media assets, built LMS course shell
**Note:** This stage runs **iteratively in sprints** — build, review, revise, repeat
**Owner:** LXD coordinates; SME provides content expertise; media/tech specialists contribute

---

### 13. Beta Testing Feedback Session
**Purpose:** Test the course with real or representative learners before full launch. Gather structured feedback on clarity, navigation, engagement, and technical function.
**Inputs:** Draft course (content complete or near-complete)
**Outputs:** Raw feedback data (survey responses, session notes, recordings)
**Owner:** LXD facilitates; beta testers participate; SME observes

---

### 14. Beta Testing Report
**Purpose:** Synthesise beta testing feedback into actionable findings and prioritised recommendations.
**Inputs:** Beta testing session data
**Outputs:** Report with prioritised recommendation list
**Owner:** LXD authors; shared with SME and team

---

### 15. Quality Assurance Report
**Purpose:** Systematic check of the course against a quality rubric — covering pedagogy, accessibility, technical function, and instructional consistency.
**Inputs:** Near-final course
**Outputs:** QA checklist/report with flagged issues
**Common frameworks:** Quality Matters (QM), OLC OSCQR, institutional rubric
**Owner:** LXD + accessibility specialist; independent reviewer preferred

---

### 16. Delivery Prep Plan
**Purpose:** Ensure everything is in place for launch — faculty are prepared, LMS is configured, students can access the course, support is in place.
**Inputs:** QA report, project plan
**Outputs:** Launch checklist; faculty preparation notes
**Owner:** LXD coordinates; LMS admin, faculty, and student services contribute

---

### 17. Evaluation
**Purpose:** Assess whether the course achieved its learning goals, using data from assessments, learner surveys, and faculty reflection.
**Inputs:** Course completion data, learner surveys, assessment results, faculty reflection
**Outputs:** Evaluation summary
**Frameworks:** Kirkpatrick Model (Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, Results); CIPP Model
**Owner:** LXD designs evaluation; faculty and institution provide data

---

### 18. Evaluation-Impact Report
**Purpose:** Document the outcomes of the course and the design intervention — for institutional reporting, continuous improvement, and professional portfolio.
**Inputs:** Evaluation data, course analytics, stakeholder reflections
**Outputs:** Impact report
**Owner:** LXD authors; manager/director reviews

---

### 19. Sustainment Plan
**Purpose:** Ensure the course can be maintained, updated, and improved without requiring a full redesign engagement.
**Inputs:** Evaluation report, faculty capacity, institutional calendar
**Outputs:** Sustainment plan document
**Contents:**
- Recommended update schedule
- Content flagged for regular review
- Faculty responsibilities vs. LXD support level
- Trigger points for formal review (enrolment changes, curriculum shifts, poor outcomes)

---

### 20. Case Study
**Purpose:** Capture and share the story of the project — what was designed, what worked, what was learned, and what the impact was.
**Inputs:** All project documentation
**Outputs:** Written or visual case study (internal or public)
**Owner:** LXD authors; may be shared as institutional knowledge or portfolio piece

---

## Workflow Tips

- **The backlog (Stage 2) is always alive.** It's not a one-time deliverable — it's your daily working list throughout the project.
- **Stages 10–12 are the most iterative.** Plan for multiple rounds of storyboard → content → review → revise.
- **Don't skip Stage 19.** The sustainment plan is most often cut under time pressure and most often wished for later.
- **Stage 20 builds your portfolio.** Treat every project as a case study in progress.
