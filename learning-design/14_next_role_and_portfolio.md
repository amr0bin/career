# 14 · The Next Role and the Portfolio

## The Ceiling for This Role

The next full-time role is a seat inside a post-secondary institution where inclusive learning design and accessibility meet: designing courses and processes that don't need individual accommodation to work, and deciding accommodations well where they still do. Held well, it is a six-figure, pensioned career in its own right, the tuition waiver that funds the prerequisites, the source of logged helping hours, and the internal-candidate route the arc depends on (`../arc/stage-1-access-and-frame.md`).

## The Role Families to Watch

Title wording varies by institution; read the duties, not the title.

| Family | Typical titles | Where it sits | What it uses from this folder |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accessibility advising** (the arc's first target) | Accessibility Advisor, Disability Access Advisor, Accommodation Specialist | Centre for Accessibility (UBC), Centre for Accessible Learning (SFU) | 08, 10, 11; `../structures/ACCOMMODATION_RATIONALE_MEMO.md` |
| **Inclusive learning design** | Learning Designer, Instructional Designer, Educational Developer, UDL Specialist | Teaching and learning centres (e.g. UBC CTLT, SFU Centre for Educational Excellence), BCcampus, faculty learning-design units | All of 00–12 |
| **Accessibility programme roles** | Accessibility Coordinator, Digital Accessibility Specialist | Equity, IT or accessibility-plan offices under the *Accessible BC Act* | 08, 09, 11; `../structures/ACCESSIBILITY_AUDIT_REPORT.md` |

**Which to prioritise:** the arc sets accessibility advising at UBC or SFU as the target, because it carries the thesis question and the internal route into the counselling MA. Inclusive learning design roles in the same institutions also carry the tuition waiver and the internal route, so apply to both families there. Roles outside post-secondary (health-authority learning design) are strong but lack the waiver and the route (`../options/open-forks.md`). Apply to every operational and term role in both units; continuing status is the target.

## The Portfolio: Three Case Studies Carry an Application

Hiring panels in learning design ask to *see* work. Three strong case studies beat twenty screenshots. Use [`templates/case-study.md`](templates/case-study.md) (Stage 20).

| Case study | What it proves | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · A redesign with measurable outcomes** | Alignment, assessment design, beta and QA, evaluation | A past course project, anonymised and with permission |
| **2 · An accessibility or UDL review** | Standards fluency (WCAG 2.2, UDL 3.0), prioritisation, process barriers | An Accessible Learning Labs review, or an audit of your own public resource |
| **3 · An invented accommodation determination** | Adjudication: essential requirements, *Meiorin*, undue hardship, a clear rationale | The casebook — clearly labelled as invented (house rule 5) |

Each case study: context and constraint → the decision that mattered and why → what you made → evidence of effect → what you would change. One page each, plus artefacts. Host on andrearobin.com; the Watershed cases already show the register.

**Never include:** employer artefacts without written permission, real student or client information, or real accommodation cases in any form.

## Applications and Interviews

- **Cover letter:** one paragraph per duty in the posting, each with one piece of evidence from the portfolio.
- **The design task.** Many learning-design interviews include a take-home or live task (redesign a module, write outcomes, critique a page). Practise with the alignment table and the QA checklist as your scaffold: state the learner, the outcome, the assessment, then the activity, and say what you'd test.
- **Advisor interviews** often use scenarios. Answer with the memo's structure: functional impact → essential requirement → options → undue hardship → decision → review date.
- **Questions to ask them:** how accommodation and learning design teams work together; whether UDL is resourced or only encouraged; reduced-hours, study-leave and secondment provisions (ask at offer stage, as the arc says).

## The First 90 Days

| Window | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Learn the policies (UBC LR7 or SFU GP 26, the institution's accessibility plan), the LMS, the team's workflow and templates | A personal map of who decides what |
| Days 31–60 | Take a first project end to end with a senior colleague; start the reflection log from day one (helping hours count) | One delivered piece of work; logged hours |
| Days 61–90 | Propose one small improvement from the review findings — ideally a *process* fix | A short proposal; a case-study draft |

Then set the year: one improvement project, one conference (CACUSS or AHEAD), and the Stage 1 prerequisites on the waiver.
