My Vision — Living Document

Working on the thing that
matters most.

Disabled students report depression at higher rates than their non-disabled peers, and then meet a support structure that treats access and mental health as two separate offices with two separate intake forms. Very few people sit in either room able to see that a flattening mood and an unmet access need are the same problem viewed from two angles. This is the plan for becoming one of them — one qualification, one specialism, five stages — built from creativity, learning science, disability studies, neurobiology, and fifteen years of knowing how to make knowledge travel.

The education path has resolved into one qualification and one specialism. The qualification is a doctorate in counselling psychology — in British Columbia the only route to the protected title, and also the terminal degree the university teaching stream requires, so one credential opens the counselling centre, the private practice, the supervision of students, and the lectern. The specialism is disability, and it is already held. Every item below either serves that or it is not on this list. The stages are not durations. Each one closes when a condition is met rather than when a year elapses, so the path holds at whatever pace the work and the life allow.

None of the three is load-bearing alone; the circuit is. Each property also needs a defined minimum viable state before the busiest year arrives — for the personal site, a current CV and publication list and nothing more; for the practice, a static offer page with the deposited research artefacts; for the studio, the running sessions and nothing new. Defining those minima in advance is what prevents an unplanned collapse into one site during the doctorate.

The gap this closes is not a gap in compassion or in funding. It is a gap in combined expertise, and it is unusually narrow.

I am building toward becoming a registered psychologist whose specialism is disability — able to hold a clinical hour with a student whose mood is flattening, and also to read that student's programme's essential requirements document and say precisely why it is indefensible. The counselling centre addresses the distress. The accessibility office addresses the accommodation. Almost nobody sits in either room able to see that they are the same problem viewed from two angles.

The fields that sit between these pairs are where underserved, high-value work exists. Most researchers and practitioners live in one. This path builds fluency across all of them — in service of one problem.

The Floor Under All of It
How This Work Continues — No Matter What

Three named employers and two competitive admissions gates is a narrow path, and narrowing it was the right call — focus is what makes preparation possible at all. But a plan that only works if every gate opens is a plan that spends a decade being anxious. This section exists so that the answer to what if none of it lands is already written down, in advance, in a calm year.

FloorAlready true
The Work Is Not Downstream of the Job
Four things that continue regardless of who employs you, or whether anyone does

The most important thing to notice is that the work already exists. Every credential on this path makes it easier to be heard, better funded, and more securely paid — but not one of them is what makes it real. That is a genuine structural fact about this particular career, not a consolation.

One
The specialism belongs to you, not to an employer
A hundred mapped contributions, the essential-requirements corpus work, the accommodation-law fluency, fifteen years of learning design. No institution granted it and none can withdraw it. It transfers to professional colleges, licensing bodies, accreditation agencies, health authorities, and any employer with a duty to accommodate — and it transfers across provinces and countries in a way that registration does not.
Two
The published record is permanent
Four resources, the conceptual paper, a preregistered corpus study, a thesis. Once these are in the world they keep working — cited, adopted, argued with — with no reference to where you are employed. This is the strongest argument for publishing early rather than after arrival, and it is the part of the plan least dependent on anyone saying yes.
Three
The products and the method travel
Workbooks, licensed training materials, and eventually the manualised facilitator curriculum are tied to no province, employer, or regulatory regime. They are why the financial plan insists on building them in Stage one — but their second function is this one: they are the version of the work that survives a sector downturn, a College rule change, or a decision to live somewhere else entirely.
Four
The room exists at every tier
Registration as a clinical counsellor after the master's already permits a practice, campus counselling posts at the college and art-school tier, and sessional teaching. The doctorate adds the protected title, the psychologist tier, and the tenure-track stream. Those are real additions — and the version without them is still a working clinical life with this specialism inside it.

Which means the honest ordering is this: the credentials are for reach and for pay, and the work is for its own sake. If a gate closes, what is lost is speed and salary — not the practice, not the research, not the people helped. Worth rereading in any year when an application comes back no.

Each of these is a route already walked by other people. Writing them down now — rather than in the week a decision arrives — is what keeps a setback the size it actually is.

Around twenty-five public post-secondary institutions in BC run accessibility services, and several of the colleges and polytechnics are closer to home and less competitive than the research universities. Failing that, consulting into those same offices produces the sector fluency and the relationships from outside — slower, and it still works.

The programme is not the only accredited route to registration either — comparable master's routes exist across the sector.

This floor is already written into the plan and it holds: a practice, a campus counselling post at the college or art-school tier, and sessional teaching. What is lost is the protected title and the tenure-track stream. It is a smaller life than the full version and it is not a small one.
If the sector contracts
The same expertise, other buyers
Professional colleges, licensing and accreditation bodies, health authorities, employers with accommodation obligations, and publishers all need what this specialism knows. The dissertation question — how requirements are written, and who they exclude — is not a post-secondary question that happens to apply elsewhere. It is a general question that post-secondary is one good place to study.

And a practical guard against the anxiety this section is written for: prepare one stage ahead, never three. Most of the fear in a long path comes from rehearsing gates that are still four years away and cannot be influenced today. The prerequisite inventory can be influenced today. That is the whole list.

The risk in a long path is not failing an examination. It is arriving at each stage having spent the interval on the wrong things, and treating the intervals themselves as dead time to be endured. Prepare one stage ahead, never three. While in Ground, the only forward-looking tasks are the prerequisite inventory and the Leeds application. Keep a decision log rather than a diary — one line per decision, dated, what was chosen and what was rejected and why. It becomes the methods section later, and on the days the work feels like nothing, it is evidence that it is not.

There are exactly two stages in this path that a salary cannot carry, and they are known in advance. Everything financial here is about arriving at them prepared rather than discovering them from inside. This tab is new, and it exists because the plan had been written down six times, one stream at a time, without anyone ever asking what pays for the years that will not pay for themselves.

If I cannot be stable, I cannot contribute at full strength. Stability is not the opposite of impact — it is the precondition for it, and the reason financial structure belongs inside a vision document rather than in a separate file nobody opens.

The shape
Sees clients. Runs studies. Trains others. Writes plainly.
The career shape worth copying is the one that holds all four: clinical hours, a research programme, a training system that transfers the method, and public writing that reaches people outside the field. The setting here is a British Columbia university rather than global health, but the shape is the same.
The test at every step
Does this serve the person in the room?
Every methods skill acquired should trace back to this: does understanding it make me better at helping someone, at training others to do the same, or at rewriting the requirement that excluded them in the first place?
Layer One · Ground
Reading the Evidence — Fluency in the Field's Language

The smallest possible investment that gives enough to read the literature critically, collaborate with researchers, and write work the field takes seriously.

Step 01Stage one
Read the Evidence Base
Self-directed · No prerequisites · Makes methods training legible

Before any formal methods training, read the research that defines the current state of the question — what the evidence says about disability, access, and depression, and where the gaps sit. This is the content knowledge that makes methods training mean something.

Treatment evidence
Cochrane reviews — psychological treatments for depression
Systematic reviews of CBT, behavioural activation, interpersonal therapy, and arts-based approaches. The most rigorous available summaries of what works, for whom, and at what effect size. Free online.
The structural case
Disability studies on higher education
Dolmage on academic ableism, Goffman on stigma, and the literature on technical standards and clinical placement access. This is where the argument comes from that the accommodation system itself is a mental health determinant.
The measurement case
Messick on construct validity
Whether a requirement measures the capability or one bodily route to it. Read alongside the accommodation-law reasoning, this is the intellectual engine of the specialism — and the reason a clinician who can read a standards document is worth more than either specialist alone.
Creative health evidence
Fancourt & Finn — arts and health, 2019
The evidence base Make Space will eventually contribute to. Worth returning to after the statistics course; the regression models on cohort data become legible in a way they were not before, and that is the clearest possible demonstration of what the literacy buys.
Step 02Stage two
Statistics — as an Undergraduate Prerequisite
Required for admission anyway · Where the trial-reading literacy actually comes from · Not becoming a statistician

The plan used to buy this literacy twice — once as an admission requirement and once as a short course at a school of public health. It only needs buying once. The goal is bounded and specific: to read the results section of a trial and understand what the numbers mean.

The course
Undergraduate statistics, evening or online
Descriptive statistics, probability, hypothesis testing, and regression. One of the five prerequisites, completable by 31 December in the year of application, and taken as content rather than as a hoop. It is not a mathematics course; it is a reasoning course.
The payoff
Effect sizes, intervals, and what a model is doing
Enough to judge whether a claimed difference matters, whether an estimate is precise, and what a statistical model is actually claiming. This is the threshold below which collaboration means deferring and above which it means contributing.
Then
Research methods inside the Leeds MSc
The Leeds programme carries research methods modules, and the forty-five-credit applied project is a supervised piece of real research. Between them, this is the study design training the plan previously went to a school of public health for.
What this is not
Training to run analyses independently
Research teams have statisticians. The role is to understand outputs well enough to interpret them, question them, and communicate them. The clinical and access expertise is the contribution; the analytical rigour is theirs.
Layer Two · Frame
Generating Evidence — The Corpus Study

Where research literacy becomes research competence. The Leeds applied project is not a hoop — it is the first substantial original study, and it is designed so that independence is a feature rather than a limitation.

Step 03Stage two
The Essential-Requirements Corpus Study
A queryable population rather than a hand-picked sample · Preregistered · A second coder as co-author

Independence costs three things — data access, credibility, and a second pair of eyes — and each one has an answer that does not require an institution. Designed this way, a preregistered analysis from an independent scholar carries more evidential weight than an unregistered one from a professor.

Data access
Query the population, do not sample by hand
Where eligibility or requirement criteria exist as structured, queryable fields in a public dataset, the population can be queried, the denominator reported, and a justified subset coded — a materially stronger design than forty documents gathered by hand, and one that costs about a weekend of SQL.
Credibility
Preregistration, before looking at the data
A time-stamped, frozen copy of the hypotheses, design, and analysis plan, deposited publicly. No affiliation required. For an independent researcher this is the single highest-leverage act available, because it converts the absence of an institution from a weakness into a non-issue.
The rubric
Mechanism proximity — remote, adjacent, constitutive
The rubric is the study. It has to be written with worked examples and frozen before coding, not judged during it, and piloted on twenty double-coded records. If agreement is poor, that is itself a finding worth printing: a field that cannot agree on what its requirements are for has a validity problem more interesting than any exclusion count.
The second coder
The only obstacle that genuinely needs another person
And the answer is co-authorship — offered to a clinician and to someone who has been on the receiving end of the requirements being studied. It solves the methodological problem and improves the study at the same time.

One prediction is worth registering in advance: that requirements closest to the mechanism are the least likely to carry a justification, because the closer a requirement sits to how something works, the more obviously necessary it feels and the less anyone has been asked to defend it. If it holds, that is the paper. If it inverts, saying so plainly is what preregistration makes credible.

Step 04Stage three
Research Methods Inside the Counselling Master's
Qualitative and quantitative · The thesis as the real output · What the doctoral application is read for

The MA rather than the MEd, precisely because of this. Statistics, research methods, a thesis, and an oral examination — the components a doctoral admissions committee actually reads.

Qualitative methods
Thematic analysis · Framework analysis · Grounded theory
Especially relevant for studying how students experience an accommodation process, and what an unmet access need does to mood over a term. Methods that can be conducted independently and that align with existing practice knowledge.
Mixed methods
Outcome measures alongside in-depth accounts
The strongest work in this area uses both, and designing studies that hold quantitative and qualitative evidence together is a genuine skill that comparatively few researchers have. It is also the natural shape of a Make Space study once the screening measure is in place.
The thesis
Disability and depression, formally treated
The same question the whole path is organised around, given its first formal treatment — and by then the third substantial piece of work in the world, after the conceptual paper and the corpus study.
Documentation as method
Three years of reflective notes
A brief note after each Make Space session, kept from Stage one onward, becomes a qualitative dataset that needs no doctoral affiliation. Fidelity and drift can be coded against a declared core, which is what makes a well-run container distinguishable from a degraded one.
Layer Three · The Question
Research Independence — The Dissertation and the Grant

Doctoral methods training is shaped entirely by the dissertation question. Every method developed should be traceable to something that question specifically requires.

Step 05Stage four
Clinical Research Methods — Chosen for the Question
Feasibility studies · Single-case design · Qualitative deepened · A statistician as collaborator

The right tools for the question rather than the most sophisticated ones available. The question is what makes access and accommodation decisions defensible, and what happens to mood when they are not.

Feasibility studies
Testing an intervention before scaling it
Can this be delivered as intended? Do participants engage? What should a larger study measure? The right scale for a dissertation — rigorous, achievable, and genuinely useful to the field.
Single-case design
Individual response, studied rigorously
Well suited to clinical psychology research on new approaches. Allows rigorous study of whether something is working for a specific person over time, and bridges clinical observation and research evidence.
Qualitative — deepened
Interpretative phenomenological analysis · Narrative inquiry
What is it like to be told that a requirement you cannot meet is essential? These methods answer that, and they draw on the same sensibility as the fine art and facilitation practice. Not lesser methods. Different questions.
Collaboration model
Statistician as collaborator, not dependency
The programme has statistical support. For quantitative components, collaborate rather than attempt to become one. Their expertise is the analytical rigour; the clinical and access knowledge is the contribution. This is how research teams work.
Step 06Stage four
Grant Writing as Applied Research Literacy
Tri-council awards first · CIHR · SSHRC · The skill that funds the work and proves the thinking

Writing a grant is the most demanding test of research literacy: it requires showing expert reviewers that the evidence base is understood, that a genuine gap has been identified, and that the methods will address it. It is also — directly — part of how Stage four gets funded.

First
Graduate awards, during the master's
Tri-council master's and doctoral awards and provincial graduate scholarships. Apply early and practise the genre before it matters — these are also part of the funding package that makes the doctorate enterable at all.
Health framing
CIHR
The primary federal funder of health research in Canada, including psychosocial and arts-based work. The depression and clinical outcomes side of the question sits here.
Social framing
SSHRC
Disability studies, education, and the standards-and-exclusion side of the question. Framing the programme so it is legible to both agencies increases the funding surface area substantially, and the intersection is genuinely eligible for both.
Internal
Departmental and university sources
Faculty research funds, teaching and research assistantships, and internal awards. Less prestigious, considerably easier to obtain, and part of the same package. They also build the teaching record the educational leadership stream is later assessed on.
Layer Four · Throughout
The Outputs — What Research Literacy Actually Produces

Research literacy is not an internal achievement. It is what allows the clinical and creative work to generate knowledge others can build on — and each stage should produce something citable.

Step 07Ongoing
Publication — Starting Now, Growing Over Time
Four resources first · Conceptual paper · Corpus study · Thesis · Dissertation papers

The record builds gradually, and each piece reflects the stage of training it came from. The progression is the story of growing research independence in service of one coherent question.

Now
Four resources, then the conceptual paper
The four accessibility resources are written and unpublished; publishing them costs a revision pass, not a research programme. The conceptual paper needs no affiliation and changes the tenor of every conversation after it.
Stage two
The corpus study
Preregistered, supervised inside the Leeds applied project, credited toward the degree, and publishable as a stand-alone paper. It is the empirical spine of the whole specialism.
Stage three
The master's thesis
Disability and depression, formally treated — and the piece of work the doctoral supervisor actually reads before agreeing to anything.
Throughout
The Substack as public scholarship
Not separate from the research record but its public face. Writing clearly about access, disability, and depression for a practitioner audience is knowledge translation that academic publication cannot do. Both matter. Both compound. Minimum rhythm: one substantial piece monthly.
AnchorAlways
The Test at Every Decision Point
Does this make me more effective — in the room, and at the level of the requirement that put them there?

Research methods training is a means, not an end. At every decision point — which course, which method, which paper — the orienting question is the same.

Clinical
Does this make me better at sitting with someone?
Training that deepens clinical understanding by revealing what the evidence shows serves this directly. Training that pulls attention from the clinical relationship without obvious payoff should be questioned — and, on the current evidence, sometimes removed.
Systems
Does this help rewrite the requirement?
The reach is wider than a caseload. Methods that illuminate how standards are set, justified, and defended are load-bearing, because that is the lever that changes who can enter a profession at all.
Creative
Does this deepen the account of why creative work helps?
Make Space needs a research foundation to travel beyond the room where it happens. Qualitative studies of participant experience, feasibility work, and the documented method all serve that directly.
Portfolio
Does this keep the switching intact?
Research literacy should not collapse the portfolio into a single analytical mode. The test is whether the research work is enriching the creative work or quietly replacing it — and the quarterly studio floor exists to make that answerable rather than felt.

This is not a path toward becoming a methodologist. It is a path toward becoming a registered psychologist who understands one question deeply enough — from the inside of clinical work, from the inside of creative practice, from the inside of fifteen years of designing the systems that exclude people, and from the inside of the research evidence — to contribute something the field genuinely needs and currently lacks. The literacy is real. The identity stays clinical.

Every serious career plan has genuine weaknesses. Naming them honestly — and working with them rather than around them — is what separates a plan that holds over twenty years from one that quietly collapses under its own ambition. These are the real challenges, assessed through the 80,000 Hours lens, with options for turning each one into deeper competence.

The challenges below are not reasons to abandon the path. They are the friction points where the most important decisions will be made. A thoughtful, senior person in this field — a registered psychologist, a disability scholar, an admissions chair, someone who cares about the problem and has no stake in making you feel good — would raise every one of these. They are raised here first, so that the answers can be built into the path rather than discovered too late.

Challenge 01Timeline
The path is long, and pace is the thing most easily got wrong
Sequencing risk · Compounding load · Neither rushing nor drifting is free

80,000 Hours is consistently cautious about long credential sequences, and the caution is worth taking seriously — but it is advice about leverage, not about speed. The honest version of this challenge is not “this takes too long.” It is that a long path can be got wrong in two opposite directions: by rushing it, so the work thins out and the person carrying it does not last; or by drifting, so stages blur and nothing ever closes. The response to both is the same, and it is the reason this plan is now keyed to conditions rather than years — a stage ends when its condition is met, which is answerable, rather than when a date passes, which only generates a standing sense of being behind.

The specific risks
Risk A
Credential obsolescence
Understanding of what works — in accommodation practice, in access policy, and in the treatment of depression — will continue to evolve. The dissertation question identified now may be partially answered by others before it is completed, and the specific gap being trained for may shift under it.
Risk B
Opportunity cost of sequencing
A strict waterfall makes every delay compound. The response is to run what can genuinely run together — the Leeds modules and the undergraduate prerequisites — and to make the two heaviest stages, the counselling master’s and the doctorate, conditional rather than scheduled.
Risk C
The field moves on
Disability studies, creative health, and access research are growing areas now, and the specific intersection may be better populated by the time the later stages arrive. The response is not to hurry but to publish throughout — a contribution established in the open, stage by stage, does not depend on arriving first.
Risk D
Burnout before arrival
Part-time study alongside a career, a consulting practice, and a portfolio of creative projects is a sustained demand however long it runs. The risk is not failure to complete — it is arriving depleted, with the most important work still ahead. This is the argument for naming, in every phase, what is carried and what is set down.
Options — turning this challenge into positioning
Option A
Deploy at every stage, not just at the end
The four resources and the conceptual paper now. The corpus study as the Leeds applied project. The disability and depression study as the master’s thesis. Each stage produces something citable. By the time the PhD is complete, there is already a body of work — not an empty runway followed by a launch. The field knows you before you arrive with the credential.
Option B
Close each stage on a condition, not a date
Each stage carries a stated completion condition. The review happens when the condition is met, not on a calendar — and the question at each review is narrow and answerable: is this stage finished, and is the next one right. The plan is the starting hypothesis; the conditions are where it gets updated with real information.
Option C
Run the modules and the prerequisites together
Leeds is modular and permits breaks between modules, and the five undergraduate prerequisites are evening and online. Running them inside one stage rather than two is now the plan rather than an option — not to compress the path, but so that no stage waits on another for no reason. Where they collide, the prerequisites win: anything with a fixed external date outranks anything without one.
Option D
Treat the learning design career as leverage, not just income
A senior role inside a BC university — accessibility services, student services, or learning design — is not waiting to become relevant. It is already the work, it is already inside the institution the path ends in, and it is where the tuition benefits, the assistantships and the references live. Framing the day job as active deployment, not a holding pattern, changes the psychological experience of the credential runway and produces real systems-level impact throughout.

The discipline is treating every stage as deployment rather than preparation — which is already true, since fifteen years of practice, a running program, and a published body of work are in hand before the first credential begins. A person holding those is not on a runway. The pace at which the remaining pieces arrive changes very little about what the work eventually amounts to.

Challenge 02Evidence
The arts-based depression evidence base is thinner than the enthusiasm suggests
RCT evidence is limited · Effect sizes are modest · The field is promising, not settled

The randomised controlled trial evidence for arts-based interventions specifically for depression is weaker than the enthusiasm in creative health circles suggests. Most of the strong evidence is for CBT, behavioural activation, and interpersonal therapy. Fancourt's epidemiological work is rigorous and important, but it establishes association, not mechanism. A peer reviewer at a top journal, or a grant panel at CIHR, will know this. Building a research career on a thin evidence base is a real risk — the work may amount to confirming that the evidence is limited rather than advancing it.

The specific risks
Risk A
Confirmation bias in the research agenda
Someone who has invested fifteen years in creative practice and facilitation has a strong prior belief that creative approaches work. That prior belief can shape research design, interpretation, and publication choices in ways that are invisible from the inside but obvious to external reviewers.
Risk B
Null results are possible and important
The systematic review thesis may find that the evidence for arts-based approaches to depression is weaker than expected. The feasibility study in the PhD may find that creative interventions don't outperform active control conditions. These are genuine scientific possibilities, not catastrophes — but they require a research agenda that can hold them.
Risk C
The field may consolidate before you arrive
Several well-funded research groups in the UK and US are actively working on arts and health, including depression. By the time the PhD is complete, the systematic review may already exist, the feasibility trials may already be done, and the implementation science questions may be further advanced than anticipated.
Options — turning this challenge into positioning
Option A
Make the thin evidence base the research question, not the assumption
The most credible position is not "arts-based approaches work for depression and I am building the systems to scale them" — it is "the evidence is promising and contested, and my research program will clarify what we actually know and for whom." That is a stronger scientific position and a more honest one.
Option B
Study mechanisms, not just outcomes
The most important gap in creative health research is not whether outcomes improve — it is why, for whom, and under what conditions. Mechanism-focused research (what does creative engagement do to the neural, psychological, and social processes involved in depression?) is less crowded, more fundable, and directly leverages the neurobiology background.
Option C
Anchor in implementation science rather than efficacy
The implementation question — how do you design training systems that allow community practitioners to deliver creative health approaches at scale — does not require the efficacy question to be fully settled. It is a tractable research question regardless of whether the intervention effect sizes are large or modest, and it is where the learning design expertise is most directly applicable.
Option D
Design the systematic review to be genuinely neutral
The MSc thesis systematic review should be designed to find whatever the evidence shows — including evidence that creative approaches don't work as well as hoped. Pre-registering the protocol on PROSPERO before beginning is a public commitment to that neutrality, and a signal to reviewers that the research is rigorous rather than advocacy dressed as science.

The evidence base challenge is actually the strongest argument for this research career. If the evidence were settled, the gap would be filled and the contribution would be marginal. The thinness of the evidence is the opportunity — provided the research is designed to advance it rather than simply confirm prior beliefs.

Challenge 03Concentration
The role does not currently exist, and the specialism is narrow, provincial, and sector-bound
One province · One sector · A job description nobody has written yet

The specialism's strength and its exposure are the same fact. "A registered psychologist who can hold a clinical hour and also read a programme's essential requirements and say precisely why it is indefensible" is close to unique — and nothing about being unique guarantees that an institution has a line item for it. The plan now rests on one province's regulatory regime, one sector's hiring practices, three named employers narrowed by geography, and a combined role that no posting yet describes. The earlier global framing had the opposite problem: enormous reach, no traction. This version has traction and a narrow base, and the narrowness is worth naming rather than admiring.

The specific risks
Risk A
No posting matches the profile
University counselling centres hire counsellors and psychologists; accessibility offices hire advisors and coordinators. Very few postings ask for both, and hiring committees hire against the posting in front of them. The combined role is likely to have to be constructed inside an institution rather than applied for.
Risk B
A regulatory and provincial single point of failure
Registration is provincial, the College's requirements are being actively revised, and psychotherapy becomes a regulated profession in British Columbia in November 2027 with qualifying-programme standards still being set. A plan built entirely around one jurisdiction's rules inherits every change to them.
Risk C
Sector-wide budget exposure
Post-secondary funding in Canada is under sustained pressure, and student services and accessibility offices are rarely the first units protected. Concentrating the salaried role, the practicum setting, the teaching route, and the eventual clinical post inside one sector means one sector's bad decade affects all four at once.
Risk D
The specialism could be absorbed
Universal design maturing, accommodation processes becoming automated, or a professional body issuing definitive technical-standards guidance would each reduce the scarcity value of the intersection. Unlikely to happen quickly. Not impossible over twenty years.
Options — turning this challenge into positioning
Option A
Write the role before applying for it
The published resources, the corpus study, and the public writing together constitute a job description that does not exist yet. Institutions do create posts around demonstrated capability — but only when the capability is visible, documented, and already producing something they can point at. That is what publishing in Stage one is actually for.
Option B
Hold the transferable version alongside the specific one
The same expertise reads onto professional colleges, licensing bodies, health authorities, accreditation agencies, and any employer with a duty to accommodate — and onto roughly twenty-five BC post-secondary institutions rather than three, several of them closer to home than Point Grey. Consulting already reaches those buyers. Keeping that door genuinely open — rather than letting the university frame narrow the offer — is the cheapest available diversification.
Option C
Make the products portable
Workbooks, licensed materials, and a manualised training system are not tied to a province, an employer, or a regulatory regime. They are the part of the portfolio that survives a sector downturn, a College rule change, or a decision to live somewhere else — which is a second argument for building them early, on top of the financial one.
Option D
Verify the symmetry rather than assuming it
Only UBC has actually been checked — for the counselling routes, the practicum and internship training, the teaching-focused faculty stream, and the funding structure. With UVic held back on geography, whether SFU operates comparable routes is no longer a curiosity but a third of the remaining target. This is a week's enquiry, not a research project.

Narrowness is not the same as fragility, and this specialism is genuinely defensible: it takes years to assemble and cannot be acquired quickly by anyone else. The response is not to broaden the specialism — that would destroy the thing that makes it valuable — but to make sure the outputs travel further than the role does.

Challenge 04Portfolio
The portfolio model is epistemologically justified but practically fragile
Switching costs are real · Fragmentation risk · The meaning layer can become the escape hatch

The portfolio career model — learning design career, consulting practice, creative health facilitation, fine art practice, research program, clinical training — is intellectually coherent and practically ambitious. 80,000 Hours would note that each stream requires real time, real energy, and real attention. The switching between modes that is described as epistemological generates real cognitive overhead. And the streams that feel most meaningful are often the ones most likely to consume the time that the highest-leverage work requires.

The specific risks
Risk A
Dear Ordinary gets quietly sacrificed
The fine art practice is the most vulnerable stream. It has no external accountability, no deadline, no professional consequence for stopping. When Leeds coursework, the undergraduate prerequisites, the crisis line hours, the Substack, Make Space, and a full-time job are all running simultaneously, Dear Ordinary is the thing that gets postponed — and postponed again — until it has quietly stopped.
Risk B
The consulting practice competes with the research
Consulting income is immediate and tangible. Research outputs are slow and uncertain. During Stage three and Stage four, when income is constrained and research progress feels invisible, the consulting practice will expand to fill the available space — producing income but consuming the hours the degree requires. This is precisely why the cap is set in advance rather than judged in the moment.
Risk C
Make Space becomes an avoidance mechanism
Creative facilitation is enjoyable, immediately rewarding, and produces visible impact. Doctoral research is slow, frustrating, and its impact is invisible for years. During difficult doctoral stretches, Make Space can expand from a protected portfolio stream into the place where the hours that should be going to the dissertation actually go.
Risk D
The switching generates overhead that isn't accounted for
Moving between the analytical register of research writing and the embodied register of art-making or facilitation takes real time and energy to transition. The portfolio model assumes clean switching. In practice, the transitions consume the space that was supposed to exist between streams.
Options — turning this challenge into positioning
Option A
Protect Dear Ordinary with external accountability
The quarterly sharing structure is good but insufficient. Add a specific commitment — a date, a collaborator, a venue — that makes Dear Ordinary impossible to quietly postpone. The fine art practice is the epistemological foundation of the whole portfolio. It deserves the same structural protection as the Substack deadline or the Leeds assignment due date.
Option B
Set explicit time budgets for each stream and defend them
Not aspiration time — actual protected hours in the week, defended from the streams that expand naturally (consulting, facilitation) at the expense of the streams that don't (research writing, art practice). The budget is the discipline. Review it quarterly and name honestly which stream is being subsidised by which.
Option C
Treat each stream as having a minimum viable rhythm, not a maximum
Make Space at its minimum viable rhythm is one session per week and a monthly post. Dear Ordinary at its minimum viable rhythm is one piece per quarter. The Substack at its minimum viable rhythm is one post per week. Define the minimum for each stream and protect that minimum — rather than letting any stream expand beyond what the others can afford.
Option D
Name the hierarchy explicitly
During Stage two, the hierarchy is: job, coursework and prerequisites, crisis line hours, Substack, Make Space, Dear Ordinary. During Stage three, clinical training comes first. During Stage four, the dissertation comes first. Having a named hierarchy means that when streams compete for the same hours, the decision has already been made.

The portfolio is the strength of the career. But portfolios require more structural discipline than single-track careers, not less. The switching is the mechanism — but switching requires boundaries, and boundaries require named priorities. The portfolio survives only if its structure is protected, not assumed.

Challenge 05Leverage
The theory of change is not yet precise enough to evaluate
How does this career reduce depression burden at scale · The mechanism needs naming · Impact is hard to trace

80,000 Hours asks for a clear theory of change: how, specifically, does this career reduce suffering? The mechanism is now much sharper than it was. A student helped in the counselling room is one person; a professional programme’s technical standards rewritten from method to construct changes who is able to enter that profession at all, for every cohort that follows. That is a real multiplier, and it is available only to someone the institution already recognises as a clinician. What is still missing is the arithmetic. How many programmes, holding how many students, over what period — and what evidence is there that a rewritten standard changes an admission outcome rather than a document? Without that, the impact is asserted rather than designed.

The specific risks
Risk A
Busyness mistaken for impact
A portfolio career with many active streams can feel highly impactful without the impact being traceable to specific outcomes. Publishing papers, training facilitators, running sessions, consulting for institutions — all of this can be genuinely busy while no requirement is actually rewritten and no student who would have been excluded is admitted.
Risk B
The highest-leverage intervention is unclear
Is the most important contribution the clinical practice? The research? The standards work? The training systems? The answer almost certainly differs by career stage — but without naming it explicitly, the tendency is to do what is most immediately rewarding rather than what has the highest expected impact. The consulting practice is lucrative. The facilitation is immediately meaningful. The research is slow and uncertain. These incentive structures need to be named.
Risk C
The multiplier may not multiply as expected
Training one cohort of facilitators is the proof of concept for the multiplier model. But whether trained facilitators actually deliver the Make Space model with fidelity, reach people experiencing depression, and produce meaningful outcomes — rather than delivering a diluted version to people who were already moderately okay — is an empirical question that has not yet been tested.
Options — turning this challenge into positioning
Option A
Write the theory of change explicitly and update it annually
Not a vision statement — a specific causal chain. “I will show that a measurable share of essential requirements are written from method rather than construct; publish a rubric and a corpus study that let a programme audit its own standards; work inside institutions where those standards are set; and treat the resulting admissions and mental health outcomes as the thing to be measured.” Then test each link in that chain as the career progresses.
Option B
Identify the counterfactual contribution at each stage
At each career stage, ask: if I weren't doing this, would someone else do it just as well? For the conceptual paper — possibly. For the corpus study — probably, eventually, by someone with an institution behind them. For the specific combination of clinical registration, disability studies, accommodation law, and fifteen years of learning systems design — almost certainly not. Focus the most energy on the contributions that are most counterfactually significant.
Option C
Build in measurement from the beginning
Make Space with a simple outcome measure — a validated depression screen administered at the beginning and end of each cycle — gives real data about whether the programme does what it claims to. That data is the foundation of the evidence base, the grant application, the licensing case, and the honest answer to whether the theory of change is working. It is also the smallest change with the largest downstream effect anywhere in Stage one.
Option D
Apply for 80,000 Hours advising to pressure-test the theory
Their advisors are specifically trained to help people clarify their theory of change and identify the weakest links. A single advising conversation, with the theory of change written out in advance, is more useful for this purpose than months of self-assessment. Apply this week.

The theory of change challenge is not a reason to doubt the path — it is a reason to make the path more precise. The career that can articulate how it reduces suffering, at what scale, through which mechanisms, and with what evidence, is the career that attracts the best collaborators, the best funding, and the best opportunities. Precision is power here.

Challenge 06Admission
Two competitive admissions gates sit between here and the title, and both are graded on things not yet held
Prerequisites · Supervisor availability · A senior average from a decade ago · A long runway before any of it

The path has become more legible than it was — a counselling psychology master's and doctorate is an ordinary, well-marked route rather than an improvised interdisciplinary one — and that legibility cuts both ways. An ordinary route has ordinary competition, published criteria, and admissions committees who compare like with like. Fifteen years of applied expertise is not an entry criterion. Five prerequisite courses, a B+ senior average, an available supervisor in the declared area, and a criminal record clearance are. Meanwhile the whole sequence to registration is long, and the runway before the first gate is where most of the discouragement lives.

The specific risks
Risk A
Supervisor availability is a hard gate, not a soft one
Admission to the research-thesis master's depends on supervisory expertise being available in the declared research area — meaning an application can be strong and still fail because nobody with the right interests has capacity that year. It is the one admissions factor almost entirely outside the applicant's control, and the one that most rewards early contact.
Risk B
The prerequisite and equivalency trap
Equivalency is no longer adjudicated in advance; departmental and calendar listings disagree about whether admissions testing is required; the requirement set itself changed for the 2026 cycle. Discovering a missing prerequisite late costs a full application year, because the 31 December deadline does not move.
Risk C
The doctorate has a second, stricter gate
Doctoral admission to an accredited counselling psychology programme is competitive on research fit and on a supervisor who wants the work — and now also on funding, which is a condition of entry in this plan rather than a bonus. Three independent conditions must be true simultaneously, and any one of them can defer a year.
Risk D
The runway is where morale is actually lost
The real risk in a long path is not failing an examination. It is spending the intervals on the wrong things, and treating them as dead time to be endured while no institution has yet said anything encouraging.
Options — turning this challenge into positioning
Option A
Start the supervisor conversation two years early
A completed disability studies MSc, a supervised applied project, a published corpus study, and disability named among the department's core research foci make that conversation straightforward rather than presumptuous. The conversation is the application; the form is the paperwork that follows it.
Option B
Do the equivalency inventory before enrolling in anything
Unglamorous, cheap, and it can move the timeline by months in either direction. It is the single highest-value administrative hour in Stage one, and it should be done against the published table and confirmed with the department rather than inferred.
Option C
Let each stage produce something citable
Four published resources, a submitted conceptual paper, a preregistered corpus study, a thesis. By the point of doctoral application there should already be several things in the world carrying my name — which is exactly what converts an unusual profile from a risk into an argument.
Option D
Prepare one stage ahead, never three
While in Ground, the only forward-looking tasks are the prerequisite inventory and the Leeds application. While at Leeds, it is the supervisor conversation. Anything further out is speculation, and holding it costs attention without producing readiness.

The profile is unusual and that is not, on its own, a liability. Fifteen years of applied disability expertise, a disability studies master's, a supervised study, and a publication record read as a research programme already underway rather than a proposal for one. The work is to make sure the application says that plainly, and to have the citable evidence in hand before the form is opened.

Challenge 07Identity
The path may be serving personal meaning as much as social good — and those are not always the same
The honest question underneath all of it · Worth sitting with directly

80,000 Hours would ask this directly: is this career path designed to maximise impact on the problem, or is it designed to maximise personal meaning and fulfilment while also being genuinely useful? These are not the same question, and the answer to which one is primary shapes every resource allocation decision. A path purely optimised for impact would look different — fewer credentials, faster deployment, standards and policy work without the clinical registration, and no studio at all. The path as designed is richer and more whole than that — but it is worth being honest about why.

The honest questions
Question A
Is the doctorate for impact or for authority?
This question has changed shape rather than gone away. The plan no longer separates registration from research authority: one accredited doctorate opens the protected title, the private practice, the supervision of students, and the teaching stream at once, which is a much stronger answer than the earlier two-credential version. But a floor still sits beneath it — a master’s and registration as a clinical counsellor deliver the university post, a practice, and sessional teaching. So the honest question is what the doctorate adds beyond authority: the protected title, the tenure-track stream, and the research training. Those are real. They should still be named as the reason, rather than assumed.
Question B
Is the fine art practice essential or protected?
Dear Ordinary is described as epistemologically necessary — the embodied knowledge that feeds the clinical and research work. That argument is genuine. But it is also worth asking honestly: would the research and clinical work be meaningfully worse without the fine art practice? Or is the fine art practice something that deserves to exist for its own sake, without needing to justify itself through the career? Both answers are legitimate. They just generate different planning decisions.
Question C
Is the portfolio model the most impactful structure or the most sustainable one?
A single-track career — policy and standards work alone, or clinical practice alone — might score higher on a narrow impact metric than the portfolio model. The portfolio model may be the right choice anyway — because it is sustainable, because it produces better knowledge, because it is honest about who you are. But the choice should be made consciously, not assumed.
The response that holds
Position A
Acknowledge the dual purpose honestly
This career is designed to do genuine good in the world AND to be a life worth living. 80,000 Hours ultimately accepts this — they note that personal fit, sustainability, and genuine engagement produce more impact over a career than a poorly-fitted person on a "higher-priority" path. The dual purpose is not a failure of rigor. It is honest.
Position B
Make the meaning/impact trade-offs explicit at each decision point
When the counselling master’s and the doctorate are both on the table, and the question is whether the second is primarily for the research credential, the protected title, or the personal formation — name that honestly, once, rather than deferring it. When a consulting contract is accepted at the cost of research hours, name that honestly. The discipline is not eliminating meaning from the calculus. It is making the trade-offs visible rather than invisible.
Position C
Trust that full presence produces better work
The strongest argument for the portfolio model, the fine art practice, and the clinical formation is empirical: people who are fully present in their work — who bring their whole self rather than a version optimised for impact metrics — tend to produce richer, more original, more durable contributions. The creativity, the embodied knowledge, and the personal meaning are not decorative. They are constitutive of the specific contribution only this person can make.

This challenge does not resolve. It is a question worth returning to at every two-year review, every major decision point, every time a new opportunity appears. The answer will change as the career develops and as the relative costs and benefits of each stream become clearer. The discipline is not to resolve it once but to hold it honestly throughout.

These challenges are not reasons to doubt the path. They are the friction points where the most important decisions will be made — and where the most important growth will happen. A career that cannot name its own weaknesses is a career that has not thought carefully enough. A career that names them, and builds the responses into the plan, is one that is more likely to hold over twenty years — and more likely to produce the contribution that the problem actually needs.
