Essential Requirements — Working Document

The work available now,
without authority or resources.

Everything in this document can be done from a laptop, with no affiliation, no funding, no ethics approval, and no permission from anyone. It is ordered rather than scheduled — a sensible sequence, not a set of deadlines.

The thesis, borrowed from the map because it says it better. Much of what people need in order to act is not missing but unusable — built without them, for conditions that are not theirs. Essential requirements are the case in point. They are published, public, and unexamined: documents that decide who may enter a profession, written for a body nobody has ever had to name.

The scarcity, stated once. Four bodies of knowledge rarely sit in one person: construct validity from educational measurement, the duty to accommodate from human rights law, disability studies as critique, and professional programme design. Each has a separate faculty, literature, credential, and career ladder, and no institutional role requires more than two — which is why the problems needing all four go unaddressed.

The mapped practice now tests this properly rather than illustrating it. Ninety-four contributions, individually sourced, across a trunk of shared craft and five branches. No single contribution cites all four families. The essential-requirements and clinical-placement contributions reach Messick, Meiorin, and backward design; the disability studies sources — Oliver, Dolmage, Titchkosky, Kafer — are cited by a different set of contributions entirely. The combination is still in the practitioner and still in no artifact, and ninety-four sourced contributions is a far better test of that than the earlier map could give.

The map corrects the framing in one direction, though. Four literatures understates what is actually held. Implementation science, knowledge mobilization, plain language, research ethics, privacy law, behaviour change, assistive technology frameworks, and disability prevalence measurement are all in the practice and all sourced. The four are simply the ones that bear on whether a requirement is valid. The others bear on whether a valid requirement is ever adopted — which turns out to be the second gap, and the more immediately useful one.

What is true elsewhere, checked rather than assumed. In May 2025 the Association of Faculties of Medicine of Canada published Re-envisioning Technical Standards in Undergraduate Medical Education in Canada. It distinguishes organic standards, which specify motor and sensory methods, from functional standards, which specify the ability required with or without accommodation — the construct/method distinction under other names. It cites the bona fide requirement test, the Accessible Canada Act, and BC case law; it draws on the critical disability literature on ableism in medical education; it had legal counsel and disabled physicians on the working group. Three and a half of the four literatures, in one artifact, with a date on it.

Two absences, not one. The half it lacks is the measurement apparatus: the bibliography contains no validity theory at all — no Messick, no validity argument, no construct-irrelevant variance — so the distinction is reached by analogy and held as a policy preference rather than as a property that can be tested. The second absence is a mechanism. The document invites other health professions to adopt parallel approaches and supplies no means of doing so, and the implementation literature that would supply one — CFIR, EPIS, PARIHS — appears in it no more than Messick does. Two demonstrable absences from a single bibliography is a sharper, more collaborative, and far more provable claim than the assertion that nobody holds the combination.

Four tracks

What can be built from here.

Four kinds of work, in rough order of how much they establish. They interleave — the study feeds the writing, the writing feeds the network, the network shapes the study — so none of them waits on another to finish.

Track A

The corpus study — original research from public documents.

Professional programmes publish their essential requirements. Nursing, social work, paramedicine, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, dental hygiene, pharmacy, midwifery, and counselling programmes almost all post an essential skills and abilities or technical standards document on a public admissions page. No systematic analysis of these documents in Canada has been found. This is the centrepiece: a real empirical contribution requiring no permission at all.

Medicine sits outside the corpus, deliberately. Canadian medical technical standards are already under national reform, so a descriptive study there would document a system mid-change, and would need the AFMC working group's involvement rather than proceeding around it. Every other health and human service profession is both un-analysed and un-reformed — and the AFMC document explicitly invites those professions to adopt parallel approaches while providing no mechanism for doing so. The corpus is that mechanism's evidence base. Medicine can be carried as a reformed comparator: the one profession whose documents show what a revision looks like.

The research question

How do Canadian professional programmes state their essential requirements — as the constructs a standard genuinely measures, or as particular bodily methods of performing them? And what does the difference imply for who can enter the profession?

Why no ethics approval is needed

TCPS 2 (2022), Article 2.2: research relying exclusively on publicly available information does not require REB review where the information is in the public domain and there is no reasonable expectation of privacy. Published institutional admissions documents sit squarely inside that. No individuals are studied, no personal data is collected, and no one is interacted with.

Two cautions. If the study later adds interviews with programme staff or students, that portion does require review and a host institution to provide it. And an REB does not certify its own exemptions in advance for unaffiliated researchers — so state the reasoning in the methods section rather than claiming an approval you do not hold.

One thing the map improves here. The trunk holds a contribution on ethics and consent in learner research, grounded in TCPS 2 and the OCAP® principles. Publishing it before the study means the methods section cites a standing position rather than reasoning from first principles in a footnote — which is worth a surprising amount when the author has no institution behind them.

Building the corpus

  1. Frame the sample deliberately. Stratify by profession, province, and institution type — research university, teaching university, college, polytechnic. Forty to sixty documents is a defensible corpus for a first paper; there is no need for completeness, only for a sampling logic you can state.
  2. Capture, do not just cite. Save a PDF of every document and submit each URL to the Internet Archive at the moment of capture. Admissions pages change without notice, and a study of documents that have since been revised is worthless without the archived versions.
  3. Record provenance in the same row as the text. Institution, programme, document title, URL, archive link, date accessed, and where in the admissions process the applicant encounters it. That last field matters more than it looks: a requirement met before application is a different instrument from one met after admission.
  4. Keep the working file boring. A single spreadsheet, one row per document, one column per code. Nothing about this needs software beyond that.

The coding frame

Seven core codes, each traceable to one of the four literatures, and four the widened map justifies adding. The first is the highest-yield and would carry a paper on its own.

CodeWhat it asks, and what to look forLiterature
1. Construct or method Is the requirement written as the capability being assessed, or as one bodily way of performing it? Assess a patient's respiratory status is a construct. Must be able to auscultate breath sounds is a method. Code every requirement; the ratio is the study's headline finding. Construct validity (Messick)
2. Locus of authority Does the document ground the requirement in a regulatory standard, an accreditation requirement, or nothing at all? Uncited requirements are inherited convention until shown otherwise — and that is the whole argument of the essential-requirements contribution. Duty to accommodate
3. Accommodation framing Absent · available · limited · framed as evidence of unsuitability. The document's own account of what happens when a person cannot meet a requirement as written. Law
4. Bodily specification Count requirements phrased in sensory, motor, or stature terms — vision, hearing, standing, lifting, manual dexterity, speech. This is where the implied default body becomes visible and countable. Disability studies
5. Temporal assumptions Continuous availability, shift length, attendance minima, no-absence clauses, fixed progression. The structural layer that excludes episodic and fluctuating conditions before any accommodation conversation begins. Episodic disability
6. Revisability Does the document state when it was last reviewed, by whom, and through what process? Expect almost none to. A standard with no revision record is a standard nobody has been asked to defend. Programme design
7. Voice Any evidence that disabled practitioners, students, or disability-led organisations were consulted in drafting. Also expect near-zero, which is itself the finding. Disability justice
8. Unaided performance Added. Count requirements phrased as performance without something — unaided, independently, without assistance, without adaptive equipment. This is the purest form of method stated as construct, because it names no capability at all: it names the absence of a tool. Likely to be a substantial share of code 1's method cases, and worth separating so it can be reported on its own. Assistive technology (SETT · HAAT)
9. Disclosure architecture Added; split out of code 3. Where in the admissions process the applicant is asked to self-assess or self-identify against the standard, and to whom. A requirement met before application is a disclosure event; one met after admission is an accommodation conversation. Distinct enough from accommodation framing to be counted separately, and it carries its own literature. Disclosure burden (Goffman · Dolmage)
10. Reading load Added. Run a readability measure over every document and record it beside the codes. A standard the applicant cannot read is a barrier operating before any accommodation question arises. Trivially computable, defensible against a published standard, and — as far as can be established — never done on this corpus. Likely the cheapest genuinely new finding in the study. Plain language (ISO 24495-1)
11. Locus of capacity Added; lowest confidence. Does the requirement place the demand in the person, or in the environment the person will be placed in? Must be able to work in a noisy environment and must be able to work in the environments the programme provides are different instruments. Keep it in the pilot; drop it if it will not code reliably. Universal design (Connell et al.)

Three methodological decisions worth making early

Pilot all eleven, publish on the ones that survive. Eleven codes is too many for one coder to carry across sixty documents, and that is fine: the pilot exists to kill some. Codes 1, 2, 4 and 10 are the ones most likely to hold and most likely to carry a paper between them. Report the frame in full in the coding manual and the findings on the subset that coded reliably.

Reliability. Single-coder document analysis is publishable but weaker. Coding a subsample of ten documents with a second reader — an accommodation professional, a programme director, a disabled practitioner — and reporting agreement raises the paper's standing considerably and costs only a few conversations.

Attribution. Report at aggregate level with unattributed illustrative quotations rather than naming institutions. It is defensible methodologically, it avoids putting programmes on the defensive, and it makes it far more likely that a programme will later invite you in to help. Naming institutions buys a sharper paper and closes the consulting door behind it.

What it produces

  • A paper — a systematic account of how Canadian professional programmes outside medicine write their essential requirements, and the first to code them against a measurement standard rather than a policy preference.
  • An open dataset and coding manual, deposited free on the Open Science Framework. Infrastructure other people cite, which is a faster route into a field than a single article.
  • A ready dissertation. Leeds can absorb and extend this directly, which means starting now is not work done ahead of the credential — it is the credential arriving to find the work underway.
  • The evidence base for the consulting offer, since a programme asking “are our standards defensible?” can now be answered with comparative data rather than assertion.
Track B

Writing that needs no data at all.

Conceptual and comparative work requires no affiliation, no approval, and no fieldwork. Three pieces are already most of the way written inside the existing contributions, and the map shows a fourth that was not visible before.

  • The four-literature framework. The piece that welds construct validity, the duty to accommodate, disability studies, and programme design into one argument. Reform documents now combine three and a half of these; what none of them supply is the measurement half — a way to say whether a requirement is valid rather than merely preferable, and what evidence would settle it. That is the contribution, and it is a narrower and more defensible claim than origination. The essential-requirements contribution is most of the way there; what it still lacks is the disability studies layer held separately in the contributions on models of disability and disability studies for learning designers — the part that asks not only whether a requirement is valid and lawful, but whose body it was written for.
  • The jurisdictional comparison — four layers, not three. Previously framed as Canada, the UK, and the United States side by side. The map holds a drafted contribution on the international and regional rights instruments — the CRPD and its General Comments, the Marrakesh Treaty, the EU Charter, the African Charter, the Inter-American Convention — which argues that accessibility obligations converge across jurisdictions rather than arising independently in each. That is the better frame: a convergent international layer, three domestic implementations nested inside it, and the UK's statutory carve-out for competence standards as the interesting deviation rather than one of three equals. Sharper argument, more publishable, and most of it is already drafted.
  • The clinical placement piece. The narrower, higher-demand version: how a programme protects genuine safety requirements while examining the ones it has never had reason to question. This is the one health professions faculties will recognise immediately.
  • The adoption piece. New. Why a reformed standard does or does not get taken up — the second absence in the AFMC bibliography, argued from CFIR, EPIS and PARIHS. This is the piece that turns an invitation into a programme of work, and it is the one nobody in the disability literature is positioned to write, because implementation science is not in that field's toolkit. Shorter than the framework piece and considerably less contested.

Where these go

Journal of Postsecondary Education and Disability

AHEAD · quarterly · masked review

Practitioner-facing and the natural home for the corpus study. Every article must include practical implications for disability resource professionals, which suits this material exactly. Submissions run through Scholastica; author guidelines are on the AHEAD site.

Canadian Journal of Disability Studies

Open access · no fees · CDSA

Hosted at the University of Waterloo for the Canadian Disability Studies Association. Six thousand words maximum, forty references maximum, no article processing charge, and welcoming to independent scholars. Roughly thirty weeks to publication.

Disability & Society

The Leeds tradition

Where the critique-facing version of the argument belongs, and the journal whose readership the Leeds faculty share. Slower and more competitive; worth the framework piece rather than a first attempt.

Health professions education journals

Clinical placement version

Advances in Health Sciences Education and Academic Medicine for the placement and technical-standards argument. Commentary and perspective formats are shorter and substantially faster than full articles.

Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education

Validity version

For the construct-validity framing aimed at assessment specialists rather than disability professionals — the audience least likely to encounter this argument and most equipped to act on it.

Implementation Science Communications

Adoption version · new

Open access, shorter formats, and the natural home for the argument about why a reformed standard is or is not adopted. An audience that has never encountered this problem and holds exactly the frameworks it needs.

Preprint, in every case

EdArXiv · SSRN

Post anything under review. Timestamped, citable, freely readable, and no gatekeeper. It establishes the ground publicly while peer review runs at its own pace.

Verify before acting

Journal scopes, word limits, submission systems, and fees change. Everything above was checked recently, but confirm current author guidelines on each journal's own site before writing to length.

Track C

Publish into a backlog, in the right order.

Still the lowest-cost move available and still the one that makes every other track legible. What has changed is the reason for it. This track used to rest on an asymmetry that the full map shows does not exist.

The correction

The previous version of this document said that every other area of the practice was largely published and live, and that the accessibility branch was the one with nothing public in it. The map's own count says otherwise: the trunk is 0 of 28 published, knowledge mobilization 3 of 10, learning experience design 3 of 10, universal design for learning 4 of 10, learning systems design 7 of 14, accessibility 4 of 28. Roughly twenty-one published against ninety-four. Nothing is largely published and live. The backlog is general.

The four accessibility pieces are also not the unpublished ones. They are four of twenty-two in development on that branch, and twenty-eight more sit unpublished in the trunk. So the case for doing them first has to be made on different grounds than closing a gap — because the gap is everywhere.

The grounds that survive

These four are what Track A cites. Publishing them is not tidying the map; it is putting the study's own foundations in public before the study asks anyone to accept them. That is a sequencing argument rather than a completeness argument, and it is the stronger one.

  • Why universal design never eliminates individual accommodation — the anchor, and the most connected accessibility contribution in the map.
  • Essential requirements and the limits of flexibility — three of the four literatures already, and the direct companion to the corpus study. Codes 1, 2 and 6 rest on it.
  • Designing for episodic and fluctuating conditions — the structural argument that underwrites code 5.
  • Access in clinical and placement education — the rarest of the four and the one with the clearest employer audience.

And two from the trunk, in the same batch

  • Ethics and consent in learner research — because Track A's methods section reasons about TCPS 2, and a published standing position is worth more than a paragraph of first-principles argument from an unaffiliated researcher.
  • Plain language and writing for learning — because it is the citable warrant for code 10, and because the applicant-facing guide in the companion document cannot be written without it.

Six pieces, all drafted, all editing rather than authorship. That is the whole of Track C. The other eighty-eight contributions are a real publishing programme and a genuine question about pace, but they are not this document's problem and should not be allowed to become it.

And one serialised thread

A running series on essential requirements — one public technical-standards document examined per piece, using the corpus material as it accumulates. Public scholarship that establishes the ground while peer review is slow, and it builds the dataset as a by-product rather than competing with it.

Track D

The five ladders, and where they are climbable.

Conferences accept abstracts, not qualifications. That is the permeable point in every one of these communities, and none of them require you to be anything before you submit. There were four; the residual gap names a fifth.

  • AHEAD — the accommodation professionals' association and the publisher of JPED. Practitioner proposals are normal; this is the community whose daily work the essential-requirements question actually is.
  • CACUSS and the Canadian disability-services community — the domestic counterpart, and the network of the people you would be working alongside in an accessibility advisor role.
  • The Canadian Disability Studies Association — meets at Congress, publishes CJDS, and explicitly accommodates independent scholars.
  • The Docs With Disabilities network — the community around the health professions education work that grounds the clinical placement contribution. Unusually welcoming to people entering from outside the discipline.
  • Health professions education associations — CASN for nursing, AFMC and CAME for medicine. This is no longer a live and uncomfortable topic so much as an active national programme of work: AFMC ran the technical standards reform, consulted the regulators and accreditors, and asked other health professions to follow. Approaching them with a corpus is answering a standing invitation rather than making an unsolicited case.
  • The educational measurement community. The fifth, and the one the residual gap actually names. NCME, AERA Division D, and the NCEO accommodations work. The measurement literature that formalises construct-irrelevant variance has never been pointed at professional entry standards and contains no disability studies at all; the two clusters do not cite each other. It is the least crowded room, the place the argument is most novel, and the only one of the five where nobody will already agree with you before you start — which is worth more than it sounds.

Two things that cost nothing and compound

  • Volunteer as a peer reviewer. Small journals are chronically short of reviewers, and JPED and CJDS will take one with this background. It teaches the genre from the inside and puts you in an editor's address book.
  • Write to people. A short, specific email to an author about their actual argument costs nothing and is how five separate ladders become one network. Most academics answer.

An order, not a schedule

What to do first, and what waits for it.

Each move is sized to be finishable and produces something the next one uses. There are no dates here. If a move takes three months or nine, the order still holds.

Before anything

Run the outstanding verification checks

Roughly two hours, listed in the companion document: Meeks, Jain & Laird's Equal Access and its technical standards chapter; the OIA competence standards checklist and Advance HE's guidance; one search for existing accommodation-decision record schemas; and one search for implementation guidance published since the AFMC document. Every novelty claim in either document is only as good as the search behind it, and the failure this pair of documents exists to correct was generated by reasoning from the inside without one.

First

Publish the six contributions Track A depends on

Editing rather than authorship. Not to close an asymmetry — there isn't one — but so that the study's foundations are public before the study asks anyone to accept them, and so every subsequent conversation has something to point at.

Alongside

Build the first twenty documents and pilot the eleven codes

Twenty is enough to discover which codes work, and several will not. Expect code 1 to need sub-categories, code 3 to need a fifth value, and code 11 to be dropped outright. Revise the frame before scaling, and record what was cut and why — the coding manual is a deliverable, and its discards are part of it.

Then

Draft the jurisdictional comparison

Pure desk work, no data dependency, and mostly drafted already once the international-instruments contribution is folded in. It also forces the UK competence-standards reading that tells you whether the ground is as empty as it looks. Preprint on completion.

Then

Complete the corpus and submit the study

JPED first for the practitioner audience. Deposit the dataset and coding manual openly at the same time, so the infrastructure is available whatever the review outcome.

Then

Write the adoption piece

Short, uncontested, and aimed at a field that has never seen this problem. It is the cheapest way to make the second absence in the AFMC bibliography a matter of public record rather than a private observation — and it is the piece that makes the translation kit in the companion document look like a natural next step rather than a consulting pitch.

Once there is something to present

Submit one conference abstract

One, not five. The findings from the corpus are the abstract, and a single well-placed presentation opens more than scattered attendance at several. If the reading-load finding holds, the measurement ladder is the least obvious and most interesting place to take it.

When invited

Facilitate one essential-requirements session, free

With a single professional programme, through a contact, written up as a case study with permission. This converts the framework from argument into evidence and is the bridge from research to the consulting offer.

Honesty about the premise

How you would know if this is wrong.

“Almost nobody does this” is a claim that flatters and is hard to disprove from inside. Six ways it could be overstated. Two have been partly tested; the results are recorded here rather than in memory. Two are new, and both arrived with the widened map.

  • A national body may move first. Partly answered, and it did. This risk was not anticipated in the first version of this document, and it is the one that materialised. AFMC reformed Canadian medical technical standards in May 2025, reaching three and a half of the four literatures. The lesson generalises: the claim is not that reform is unthinkable but that it is unevenly distributed, and it needs re-checking per profession rather than once. Before fixing the corpus scope, search each candidate profession's national association for a comparable document.
  • The UK may have got there first. Partly answered: on doctrine yes, on evidence no. The Equality Act exempts competence standards from the reasonable adjustment duty while still requiring adjustment to how a standard is assessed, and the EHRC, the OIA and Advance HE have all issued guidance. What has not been found is empirical document analysis. So the concept is borrowed rather than coined — which is why competence standards is the right scholarly term — and the evidence gap remains open.
  • The knowledge may exist unpublished. Unrun, and still the most valuable hour available. Accommodation professionals almost certainly reason this way daily without writing it down; practitioner knowledge is systematically under-published. The test is conversations, not literature searches — ask three experienced advisors how they handle an essential-requirements dispute and listen for whether the four literatures are already in the room.
  • Absence of competition can mean absence of a market. Unrun. The commercial test is not whether anyone writes this, but whether anyone buys it. Look at postings for essential-requirements and technical-standards work, and at whether health professions faculties handle this internally. If they do, the contribution is scholarly rather than commercial — which is a fine answer, but it should be known rather than assumed.
  • The mechanism may already exist. New, and unrun. The second absence — that AFMC invited other professions and supplied no means of proceeding — is asserted from a single document read once. Implementation guidance may have been published since, by AFMC itself, by the AAMC, or by the Coalition for Disability Access. One search settles it, and it should happen before the adoption piece is written rather than after. Note that this is the same failure mode as the first item on this list, arriving in a new place.
  • The four-literature framing may be the wrong unit. New, and unrun. With ninety-four individually sourced contributions across a trunk and five branches, the distinctive thing may not be a four-way combination inside one head at all — it may be that a practice of this size is mapped and cited at that granularity in the first place. That is a claim about infrastructure rather than about combination, and it may be the stronger one. The test is the same shape as the others: look for anyone else publishing a practice map with per-contribution sources like this. If nobody does, the corpus study stops being the whole argument and becomes the first demonstration of a method — which is a larger claim reached honestly rather than a smaller one defended.

Why this section stays

It is still the only part of this document that survives new information without needing rewriting, because it was written to be falsified rather than to persuade. Two of its tests came back and the document changed accordingly; the widened map added two more and corrected a claim in Track C that no test had been attached to. That is the pattern. Whatever else is edited later, this section earns its place — and any new claim of scarcity added anywhere above arrives with its own test attached, or it does not arrive.

None of this requires a degree, a role, a grant, or registration. Two moves — publishing the six pieces the study stands on, and starting the corpus — would produce, within a year and at whatever pace suits, a public record in the cluster and an original dataset nobody else holds. The rest follows from those, and follows at its own speed.
