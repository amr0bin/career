# Living as Your Future Self

> **Purpose:** the method of living now as the person who already holds the next role, and the requirements map that keeps it honest · **Governs:** rooms, reading, rhythms and markers at every stage · **Carried out in Notion:** {{notion:Requisites}}

The stage plans say what to get onto the record. This method says how to live in the meantime: **as if the change has already happened**. It adds rooms, reading and rhythms to existing moves; it never adds a commitment (see the rule of two in `excellence-and-working-rules.md`).

---

## The principle: act at the level of the role, in its rooms, with its texts

Three things, in practice, at every stage:

1. **Be in the rooms that role is in** — its conferences, webinars, associations and local events — so the vocabulary, the current debates and the people are familiar before the interview.
2. **Do the role's thinking weekly** — read what that person reads, and reason through their decisions on paper.
3. **Carry the role's markers** — memberships, a bookshelf, a practice log, a way of introducing yourself — so others start to place you in the role too.

**The design rule:** everything either *feeds an existing move* or *makes it visible*.

| Prevention question | Promotion question |
|---|---|
| Is it worth attending the field's rooms before holding the role? | What would it make possible to already know, and have presented to, the people who will sit on the panel? |
| Is reading at the role's level premature? | What would it take to walk into week one already fluent? |
| Will this stretch the commitment rule? | Which rooms and rhythms turn existing commitments into visible, connected expertise rather than separate CV lines? |

## Across the three stages

### Stage 1 — the advisor

**Who she is becoming:** an Accessibility Advisor at UBC or SFU who makes accommodation decisions with confidence because she has read documentation, case law and policy like an advisor for two years before the title arrived; known beyond her office, presenting on what BC universities ask of the people who decide accommodation; on a statutory accessibility committee; writing Leeds from practice rather than towards it.

- **Rooms:** AHEAD (one flagship in person), CACUSS or the Canadian Disability Studies Association (one Canadian room), CADSPPE and assistive-technology conferences virtually, every local accessibility event.
- **Thinking:** *the advisor's hour* — weekly, one case, one policy clause, one hypothetical; a half-page determination (request, documentation, functional limitation, essential requirement at stake, accommodation, reasoning), **invented cases only, marked as invented**. Forty by month twelve, and the interview scenario has been answered forty times.
- **Texts:** the university accommodation policies, the BC Human Rights Code, the *Accessible British Columbia Act*, *Meiorin* (1999) and *Moore* (2012) read as decisions, not summaries; then Burgstahler, Meyer–Rose–Gordon, Dolmage, Price, Wong, Piepzna-Samarasinha, Kafer. One short note per book: *what this changes about how I would decide an accommodation.*
- **Markers:** the professional memberships, the headline, the printed and annotated policies on the desk. *"I work in accessibility and inclusive learning design, focused on accommodation and essential requirements in post-secondary."* True now.

### Stage 2 — the counsellor

**Who she is becoming:** a counsellor in a campus service hired for what nobody else in the room can do — hold the access lens and the mood lens at once — already known to the service from training inside it.

- **Rooms:** the College's consultations on psychotherapy standards; counselling and creative-health conferences; the counselling services' public events.
- **Thinking:** the fortnightly spoken piece as rehearsal for the recorded mock session; supervised crisis-line work; reflective practice in the register of a clinician, kept private.
- **Texts:** the BCACC Code of Ethics and art-therapy standards, held to voluntarily now; stepped-care and low-intensity intervention literature; the evidence on outcome monitoring and rupture repair.
- **Markers:** credited interviews with registered practitioners; a standing reader who is one.
- **Boundary:** living as the counsellor never means *acting* as one before registration. `house-rules.md` governs words and acts.

### Stage 3 — the scholar-practitioner

**Who she is becoming:** a clinician-researcher who owns the throughline question in public and leads the service that answers it.

- **Rooms:** the research conferences where the review and thesis will be presented; departmental talks; standards committees.
- **Thinking:** the yearly question paragraph (`ledgers-and-review.md`); the scoping review moved one or two stages a year; preregistration as the deadline an institution would otherwise supply.
- **Texts:** the faculty whose work sits nearest the question, read before writing to them.
- **Markers:** ORCID, DOIs, a dated trail of answers on the front door.

## Requirements mapping

Living as the future self is only honest if it is anchored to what the role actually requires. The method, in four moves (a Benstoem-style requirements map):

1. **Map the actual requirements.** Take a real posting or admission page, not a summary or a memory of one. Break it into lines: minimums, preferences, duties, knowledge areas. Record the source and the date read.
2. **Mark each line green or red.** Green: evidence exists on the record today, and you could point to it. Red: not yet shown — even if it has been done. A line that is true but undocumented is red.
3. **Work backwards from the red.** For each red line, the smallest move that would turn it green, and the date it needs to be green by (the application deadline, not the start date).
4. **Prioritise what moves you forward.** Sort the red lines by how much each one changes the reader's decision. The one doubt a panel will have — for the advisor role, *has she decided accommodations?* — comes first. Lines that are nice to have and cost months come last or not at all.

**Plus one column: who can speed this up?** For every red line, name the role (not the person, in public) who could shorten it: a mentor inside a unit, a co-presenter, a supervisor, an employer's development fund, a peer cohort trading cases. This is rule 11 of the protocol made operational: plan for support, not just survival.

| Requirement (source, date) | Green / red | Smallest move to green | By when | Who can speed this up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *one line per requirement* | | | | |

The map itself lives in {{notion:Requisites}} and is redone each time a new posting or programme page appears. The reasoning stays here.

---

## How to know it's working

People in the field recognise her name before they see her CV. When the next posting appears, the application reads as a colleague's, not an outsider's. And when she reads the requirements map, the red column is shorter than it was last November.

**Fallback, once:** if travel or funding for a flagship room does not come through, the virtual and local rooms still deliver the knowledge and most of the visibility, and the flagship moves one cycle with a proposal already in.
