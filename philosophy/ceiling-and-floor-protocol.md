# The Ceiling and Floor Protocol

> **Purpose:** the rules for how any plan, review or conversation about this path is held · **Governs:** every file in this repo that discusses the future, and every review run in Notion · **Carried out in Notion:** {{notion:Weekly Review}}

This protocol exists because how a plan is discussed shapes what the plan becomes. A path that is always described from its risks drifts smaller every year without anyone deciding it should. These eleven rules keep the ambition at full size and keep the honesty intact. They apply to people, documents and tools alike.

---

## The eleven rules

1. **Ceiling first.** Describe the most ambitious realistic outcome before any risk or fallback.
2. **Pair every prevention question with a promotion question.** "What could go wrong?" or "Is this necessary?" is always answered alongside "What could this make possible?" and "What would it take?"
3. **No unrequested downgrades.** Do not suggest a smaller or safer alternative unless it is asked for or there is a hard barrier, and when there is, say what the barrier is.
4. **Barriers are tasks.** A barrier is written as a next step, not as a reason to reconsider.
5. **Past hardship is context, not evidence about capacity.** It is never used to lower expectations.
6. **The confident-applicant test.** Before any answer stands, ask: would this be the answer given to a confident applicant with no history of hardship? If not, revise it.
7. **Fallbacks once, briefly, after the ceiling.** One sentence, in its place, and then the plan returns to the upside.
8. **"Ceiling check" means redo the last answer from the upside.** Balance, not cheerleading: real barriers are named honestly, and the upside is given equal rigour.
9. **The track record is the baseline.** Serious barriers have been overcome repeatedly, often without support. Any suggestion to aim lower needs specific evidence, not general caution.
10. **Apologies for ambition are adaptations to others' bias.** If an ambition arrives pre-apologised, or a doubt is pre-empted, do not comment on it. Answer at full ambition.
11. **Plan for support, not just survival.** Ask what people, mentors, funding and structures would multiply what can be done.

## How it is applied in planning

### Ceiling first, at every level

Every stage file in this repo opens with what the stage makes possible, then what it requires, then who helps, then the condition that closes it. Costs come after. The same order runs in reviews: the first question about any stage is *what does this now make possible?*, and only then *what does it cost?*

A useful habit: if a paragraph opens with a risk, move the risk down and write the sentence that should have come first.

### Prevention paired with promotion

The pairing is written down, not assumed. Where a file lists barriers, it uses three columns:

| Prevention question | Promotion question | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Advisor roles are mostly filled from inside. Is an outside application wasted? | What would being the internal candidate make possible? | Apply to every operational and term role in both units; be in the rooms where the unit's staff are |
| The practicum year is incompatible with full-time work. | What would a planned reduced appointment make possible? | Ask about reduced hours and leave at the offer stage, not mid-placement |
| The psychotherapy education standard is not yet set. | What would it take to choose a programme that certainly meets it? | Read the consultation; confirm with each programme |

A prevention question left unpaired is treated as unfinished thinking.

### Barriers as tasks

A barrier earns its place in a file only when it carries a next step and, where possible, a date. "Funding is uncertain" is not a barrier entry. "Confirm the staff tuition waiver's waiting period before accepting an offer" is. Tasks themselves live in Notion; the file holds the reasoning for why the task exists.

### The fallback, named once

Each major file states its fallback in a single line near the end of the ceiling section. Example, from the arc as a whole: *if the doctorate is not taken, registration as a clinical counsellor plus a continuing advisor or leadership role inside a university is a complete, secure career in its own right.* That sentence is written once and not repeated in every section.

### The ceiling check

"Ceiling check" is a standing instruction that can be invoked on any answer, review or draft. It means: redo it from the upside. In practice:

1. Find the ceiling the answer skipped or understated.
2. Restate it at full size, with the evidence for why it is realistic.
3. Re-pair any unpaired prevention questions.
4. Rewrite any barrier that reads as a reason into a task.
5. Check rule 11: who or what would multiply this?

### Support as part of the design

Every stage names who helps: supervisors, mentors, sponsors, nominators, funders, collaborators, co-authors. Support is planned as deliberately as coursework, because the ceiling is reached with support rather than in spite of its absence. The people slots for each stage are tracked by role in {{notion:People}}.

## What this protocol is not

It is not permission to ignore real constraints. Fixed external dates, regulatory requirements and funding conditions are named plainly and early. The protocol governs the *order* and *framing* of honest information, not whether it is said. A plan that cannot survive an honest barrier is not a good plan; a plan that is never allowed to state its ceiling is not a plan at all.
