# My method: from requirements to impact

> **Purpose:** the planning method I run across the whole arc. It is a requirements-mapping method from academic career strategy, adapted for competitive doors, several institutions, and work meant to matter beyond my own advancement. · **Governs:** how requirements, tasks and priorities are set. · **Carried out in Notion:** [Requirements Map](https://app.notion.com/p/40bd55c9671b81408248cb6f158dd7c9), [Compass](https://app.notion.com/p/40bd55c9671b81c58bf3f67134e9c3fb)

## The four steps it starts from

1. **Find the actual requirements**, from the source, not from what I assume.
2. **Put every requirement in one place**: green for held, red for still missing.
3. **Work backwards**: for each red cell, ask what exactly turns it green, and what the fastest reasonable route is.
4. **Stop treating everything as equally important.** Institutions will hand you endless work, and most of it doesn't move you forward.

The core insight holds for me: careers are mostly strategic planning, and knowing exactly what comes next beats working harder.

## What I add, and why

| Addition | Why my path needs it |
|---|---|
| **One map per door** | My next doors are an advisor role, an MSc, an MA, registration, an art therapy diploma, a PhD and a postdoc, at different institutions. Each has its own requirements, and many requirements serve several doors at once. |
| **Level: minimum, preferred, edge** | My doors are competitive. Meeting the minimum gets me considered. The **edge** gets me chosen: pilot data, a publication, a supervisor who has seen the design. I map both. |
| **Critical path** | Step 4 needs a test. A red cell is on the critical path if it blocks the next door. Those get worked first. |
| **Who can speed this up / who knows** | A green cell nobody has seen doesn't help a file. Each requirement names the person who can shorten the route or vouch for the result. |
| **Every task turns something green** | A task must turn a cell green or meet a gate. If it does neither, it is set down (*Not doing*), however worthy it is. |
| **The question as compass** | When two routes turn the same cell green, choose the one that also advances the thesis line (`arc/the-thesis-line.md`). One piece of work, counted twice. |
| **The impact check** | At every fork, the impact lens (`impact-lens.md`) asks whether this is where my effort does the most good, not only whether it advances me. |
| **Express, don't just achieve** | Requirements are met *in public*. Each green cell becomes something others can use or read: a resource, a paper, a page. The Second Brain (`second-brain.md`) turns notes into those outputs. |

## The loop

- **Weekly (Tuesday and Wednesday blocks):** work the Focus tasks; capture notes; log what moved.
- **Monthly:** mark new greens; verify the next 60 days of dates at source; make sure every critical-path red has a task linked to it.
- **Quarterly:** move gates; choose next quarter's Focus from the critical path; set down any task that turns nothing green; send the record to nominators (twice a year).
- **Yearly (November):** re-read the arc; write the dated question paragraph; run the impact lens on the next two years.

## Fallback

If a door closes, the map shows which green cells transfer to the next-best door. Most of them do, because they were chosen to serve the question, not just the door.
