# The two tracks: the craft and the game

> **Purpose:** the second skill this arc needs alongside excellent work — knowing how each gate actually decides — and the six practices that build it. · **Governs:** how every gate is read, how meetings are recorded, how outputs are planned and how the plan is accelerated. · **Carried out in Notion:** [Gates](https://app.notion.com/p/d4969021f8da4529a41eafec0c323ca4) (columns *Unwritten criteria*, *Who to ask*, *Earliest credible date*), [Meeting Log](https://app.notion.com/p/7483ce88980d41c2856eea1eb15a1c7b), [People](https://app.notion.com/p/25338760610441a8b59216af286b9070), [Tasks](https://app.notion.com/p/1dca49d1884242e28a2f2d21f1216be9)

## The idea

Doing excellent work and knowing how institutions reward work are two different skills. Most people train only the first and learn the second by accident, years late. This arc trains both on purpose, from now.

- **The craft track:** research methods, writing, accessibility expertise, counselling and art therapy skills. Most of this repo already serves it (`structures/`, `knowledge/`, `roles/`, `learning-design/`).
- **The game track:** who decides at each gate, what they actually weigh, how funding moves, which opportunities lead somewhere, and how relationships form before they are needed. This file serves it.

Acceleration comes from the game track. Fast careers are rarely harder-working careers. They are careers where every piece of work was aimed at the criteria of the next position, so nothing had to be redone or reframed later.

## Practice 1 · Reverse-engineer every gate

For each gate in Notion, hold four things side by side:

| Column | What goes in it | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Done when** (existing) | The published requirement | `facts/requirements-by-gate.md` |
| **Unwritten criteria** | What the decision-makers weigh that no page says | People who passed the gate recently; people who sit on the committee or hiring panel |
| **Evidence** (existing) | What already meets it | Requisites, the five ledgers |
| **Earliest credible date** | The soonest the evidence could honestly be ready, not the date in the plan | The quarterly acceleration review |

Questions that surface unwritten criteria, asked of someone who has passed the gate:

1. What made the strongest file you've seen stand out?
2. What do applicants usually get wrong that the page doesn't warn about?
3. Who actually reads the file, and what do they read first?
4. What would you do differently if you were applying again?
5. Is there anyone else I should talk to?

One conversation per gate per year is the target. The answers go into *Unwritten criteria*, with the date and the role of the person (names stay in Notion's People database).

## Practice 2 · Leave a written record after every meeting

After every meeting with a supervisor, admissions office, employer, partner organisation or mentor, send a short note the same day:

> Thank you for your time today. Let me quickly summarise what we agreed, so we both have it for next time:
>
> 1. …
> 2. …
>
> Next steps: I'll … by [date]; you mentioned you'd … .
> If I've missed or misread anything, please let me know.

**Why:** it protects against shifting requirements, turns verbal advice into an agreed record, and shows the professionalism that referees later describe. It is courteous, not defensive.

**Where:** each meeting gets a row in the Meeting Log with the summary, the agreed actions and a *Follow-up sent* checkbox. Supervision of clinical work stays in the private Supervision Log.

## Practice 3 · Build a research profile, not a list of projects

Every piece of work is planned as an output before it starts: what it becomes, where it goes, who sees it. The profile is one line of work, visible from the outside as one researcher's programme. See `knowledge/research-profile.md`.

## Practice 4 · Write playbooks as you go

Each first attempt at a kind of work (a scoping review, an ethics application, a partner agreement, a journal submission) ends with a short playbook: what worked, the order of steps, the templates, what to skip next time. The second attempt then runs faster than the first. Playbooks live in `structures/` (as additions to the matching guide) and become teaching material for the adjunct role.

## Practice 5 · Decide yes and no deliberately

Each opportunity is screened against one question: **which gate does this move, and which row of that gate?** An opportunity that moves no gate is a no, unless it is chosen for rest, joy or the studio, and recorded as such. Notable yeses and noes go into Notion's Decisions database with one line of reasoning. What is deliberately not done is part of the strategy.

## Practice 6 · The quarterly acceleration review

Thirty minutes on the first Wednesday of February, May and August. (November is the full November review in `ledgers-and-review.md`, which absorbs this one.)

For each gate in the current stage:

1. **Prevention:** is the target date still realistic? What could slip, and what is the task that prevents it?
2. **Promotion:** what is the earliest credible date? What would it take to get there, and what would arriving early make possible?
3. Did any unwritten criterion change what the evidence should be?
4. Which one task, if done this quarter, would move the earliest credible date forward?

That task gets *Focus* in Notion.

## Support that multiplies the game track

- **People who passed each gate recently**: the cheapest and most accurate source of unwritten criteria.
- **A mentor one stage ahead**: a current counselling psychology MA student or recent graduate at UBC or SFU.
- **A senior sponsor**: someone who can say your name in a room you are not in. Built through the relational ledger, not requested.
- **Peers on the same road**: a writing or accountability group from the Leeds cohort.
