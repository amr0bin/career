## What excellence means

**Excellence here is not more things. It is fewer things finished, dated, public, and made with disabled people rather than about them.**

The obvious answer — more training, more modalities, more hours — is close to wrong. Four layers, in every stage:

1. **Excellence as a practitioner is measured, not felt.** The field's own research finds that therapist outcomes do not reliably improve with years of experience; experience produces confidence more reliably than competence. What improves outcomes is **outcome measurement and deliberate practice**: routine outcome monitoring, and structured work on specific skills with someone who watches you do them. Rupture repair in particular, since the alliance is the most robust predictor of outcome. Cultural humility, a stance, over cultural competence, which pretends to be a body of knowledge. The same loop applies to advising: a casebook of reasoned determinations and a named reader.
2. **Excellence as a scholar-practitioner is asking a question the field has not asked, from inside the population.** The standpoint is the reason the question is well formed.
3. **Excellence in an institution is changing what the service offers, not only staffing a chair in it.** In the advisor role, that is design work that reduces the number of individual accommodations needed — without ever treating individual accommodation as lesser. *Individual accommodation is a right and a skilled professional practice.*
4. **Excellence is lasting.** The modal failure in helping professions is attrition, and the second is the quiet erosion of the practitioner into someone who has stopped noticing. **Accumulation, not intensity** is the clinical version of the same rule: a year in which all five ledgers gained one small entry and you are still recognisably yourself is a year on the trajectory.

At the level of the field, the people others cite **own a question in public** with a dated trail of answers, **have resources other institutions use**, **sit where accommodation meets design**, are **visibly accountable to disabled people** (a contributor log, not a values statement), and are **in the professional rooms**. One of each is enough to be known.

The form of excellence available here and almost nowhere else: **being credible in the studio, the accommodation office and the clinic at once.**

## Working rules

How the plan is run (from `career.html`, which governs plan over older pages):

- **Ceiling first, at every review.** Each stage is reviewed by asking what it now makes possible before asking what it costs. Floors are named once, briefly.
- **Prepare the next stage in detail; keep the ceiling in view always.** Detailed preparation runs one stage ahead. The large ambitions — the centre, the research programme, the chair — stay in view from now, because the people who later sponsor them are met now.
- **Nothing on this path is a hoop.** Abnormal psychology, learning theory and statistics are the interesting part arriving early. Statistics is what makes it possible to read a depression trial and judge it, and eventually to run one.
- **Every stage produces something citable and something visible.** A published resource, a submitted paper, a thesis, a dataset; a catalogued body of studio work, shown.
- **Every stage names who helps.** Supervisors, sponsors, funders, collaborators. The nominators file is kept current and sent the record twice a year.
- **Keep a decision log.** One line per decision, dated: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why. ({{notion:Decision Log}})
- **Anything with a fixed external date outranks anything without one, every time.**
- **No stage that reduces income is entered before it has been sized and funded.**

### The consulting test

For every consulting enquiry: **would I take this if I didn't need the money?** Consulting expands to fill whatever room it is given, and the stage most in need of income is the stage least able to afford the hours. Hence a cap, set in advance, and a specialism rate that makes fewer hours viable. The same test, passed comfortably years from now, is how to tell the income structure worked.

## The rule of two commitments

**Two commitments beside the job.** Not a sixth. A new idea is sequenced into an existing commitment or it waits; strategy adds nothing, it only sequences. Each new room, rhythm or reading must either feed an existing move or make it visible.

## The weekly rhythm (design)

| Day | Block | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Tuesday** | Access | CPACC, resources, postings and people, the casework gap, calls for proposals; from 2027 the Leeds anchor |
| **Wednesday** | Practice | Notebook, crisis-line preparation, the counselling-side ledgers, monthly resource and relational checks |
| **Sunday + Monday evening** | Studio | Sunday ten until six for making; Monday evening for documentation; twenty minutes of drawing on the days between |

Each block has a monthly pattern inside it (Access: postings, ship, landscape, casework, calls), and each drops to its floor when a degree runs. The rhythm changes by season, not by mood: **expect a season and it is a season; discover it and it feels like failure.**

### The tension, stated honestly

Three protected blocks beside a full-time job is **three commitments, not two**. The two-commitment rule and the three-block week cannot both be true as written. The tension is real, and it sharpens in 2027–29, when Leeds asks about twenty hours a week while a module runs — roughly twelve more than a Tuesday holds.

Readings that could resolve it, none yet chosen:

- **The studio is not a commitment but the thing the rest is built to protect** — a standing order, like sleep, outside the count. Then Access and Practice are the two.
- **Access and Practice are one commitment in two days** — both serve the same throughline question, and their ledgers are already merged. Then the Studio and the merged work are the two.
- **The count is per season, not per week** — only one property runs at full intensity at a time; the others sit at their floors, and a floor is not a commitment in the rule's sense.
- **The rule governs new additions, not the existing architecture** — it was written to stop a sixth item, not to dismantle three that predate it.

> **Open question for her, not a decision:** which reading does she choose, and does the Leeds expansion (about twelve extra hours a week, or a paced programme with breaks between modules) come out of Tuesday, Wednesday, or the evenings? This belongs in the decision log before the Leeds application is submitted, not after the offer.
