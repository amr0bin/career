# The Advisory Council

> **Purpose:** a standing circle of figures, real and fictional, each holding a question this career will keep facing · **Governs:** how hard decisions are framed before options are weighed · **Carried out in Notion:** [Decisions](https://app.notion.com/p/3cdae687f6934e57a7e1d3510e2cf898)

Assembled 29 August 2026. A seat is not a virtue and not a biography. It is **a question this career will face repeatedly**, assigned to someone who has already faced its equivalent. Consulting a member does not mean asking what they would do — they were solving a different problem. It means recalling how they held the question when it was hardest.

---

## Admission

The standard requires both **contribution** and **character under pressure**, and the second is harder to establish.

- **Seated** — the character claim holds on what is known.
- **Nominated** — the contribution fits; the character record is real but incomplete or contested.
- **Vacant** — the question is live and the right occupant has not been found.

**Seats outlast members.** If a member fails the standard on closer reading, the chair empties and the question stays.

---

## The seated (fiction)

Fiction hands over the interior life directly, so the standard can be assessed against the whole person.

| Seat | Member | The question | Consult when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Translator** | Louise Banks, *Arrival* (from Ted Chiang's *Story of Your Life*) | What do you do when the knowledge is sound and it cannot cross? | The room cannot hear a true finding; a deadline pushes the big question before the shared vocabulary exists; the temptation is to blame the audience |
| **The Long Walk** | Aragorn, *The Lord of the Rings* | How do you keep working through the years before the work is legible? | The work is real and the CV is not; before accepting a credential, to test whether it is wanted for the capability or the authority; when something offered would give leverage that should not be held |
| **The Giver-Away** | Shevek, *The Dispossessed* (Le Guin) | What travels free, and what is fenced? | Pricing, licensing or publication decisions; when work built for reach is held back for revenue, or work that should fund the practice is given away out of discomfort with charging |

Louise's method is the part worth keeping: she refuses the large question until the vocabulary exists to hold the answer. Aragorn is authenticated by healing rather than conquest, and he refuses the Ring — he knows what he cannot hold.

## The nominated (real people)

Held at nomination because the character standard is hers to apply, and a member seen clearly teaches more than one only admired.

| Seat | Member | The question | Consult when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Multiplier** | Dixon Chibanda, founder of the Friendship Bench | Can you give away the part of the work you most love doing? | Facilitation is going well and scaling means handing it to people who will do it differently; when "only I can hold this room" starts to sound like a fact |
| **The Long Defeat** | Paul Farmer, co-founder of Partners In Health | How do you continue when the evidence is good and the system does not move? | A finding is published and nothing changes; when cost-effectiveness is used to justify not helping the person in front of you — and when it is ignored because that person is easier to see than the hundred you will not meet |

Chibanda's model originates in the setting it serves, its evidence base was built deliberately, and he holds academic chairs alongside the practice. Farmer stays nominated because of a documented, unsettled tension over resources spent on individuals versus scale; the seat is more useful with that visible.

**Verified** 29 August 2026 against the WHO Bulletin interview (2018), the Friendship Bench site and Ashoka (Chibanda); Tolkien's Letter 195 and Tracy Kidder's *Mountains Beyond Mountains* (Farmer). **Open:** at least one critical source on Farmer read directly before any change of status.

## The vacant seats

Left empty on purpose. **Vacancies are findings**: each maps to a question the vision leaves unresolved.

| Seat | Wanted | The question |
|---|---|---|
| **The Ordinary Room** | Care as daily practice for decades, nothing scaled, nothing published | What holds the work on a Tuesday, when nothing about it announces itself as significant? |
| **The Unjustified Making** | Art that never had to earn its place through the career | Does the studio work need to be epistemologically necessary to be allowed? |
| **What You Put Down** | Someone who gave something real up permanently, and was right to | Which item gives, and under what conditions, named in advance? |

The Ordinary Room exists so the council's criterion does not quietly become *wait for something dramatic enough to be worthy of you*. What You Put Down is the only seat whose occupant must have subtracted rather than accumulated.

## Outside the council: the standing check

**Saruman** has no seat and no vote, and is present in the minutes of every meeting. He held the highest chair on a council like this one, and it did not notice him going. His reasoning was not a monster's: good intentions without leverage are useless, so acquire leverage. His instrument was persuasion so skilled that nothing distinguishes it from good facilitation. The tell is never the words. It is what the room is like afterwards.

**The standing check**, asked after any session, course, piece of public writing or decision:

> **Does the person leave more capable of their own judgment, or more reliant on mine?**

And for decisions: *would this feel any different from the inside if I were already halfway there?* He also checks the council itself. If advice from any chair starts to sound like his, that is the signal — and it will sound reasonable when it happens.

## How to use it

1. **Name the seat before naming the options.** Most of the difficulty in a hard decision is not knowing which problem you are having.
2. **Seats outlast members.** This keeps the council from becoming a defence of people you have grown attached to.
3. **Vacancies are findings.** Fill a seat after resolving its question, never as a substitute for resolving it.
4. **Admission requires both tests.** The character test excludes some people whose careers map onto this one precisely; that exclusion is the point.
5. **Symbol and method stay separate.** Moving toward what is not your own is sound as an inner orientation and hazardous as a fieldwork principle. Louise is flown to an empty site; real fieldwork arrives where people already live and already know things.

Decisions that invoke a seat are logged with the seat named, so the pattern of which questions keep returning becomes visible over the years.
