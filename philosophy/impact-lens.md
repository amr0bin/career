## The problem I'm working on

**Disabled people's access to mental health care, and the institutional processes that decide it.**

- **Scale.** Depression is among the leading causes of disability worldwide, and disabled people experience it at higher rates. Every campus runs an accommodation process that touches thousands of students a year. Globally, treatment gaps for depression are widest in low-resource settings.
- **Neglectedness.** The accommodation process is rarely studied as a cause of distress in its own right; at home, no local researcher holds the question. Disability inclusion within global mental health is thinner still.
- **Tractability.** Institutions control these processes and rewrite them on a schedule. The thesis line is built to find the specific levers, which is the tractable part.

The rarity is the point. Each stage adds a capacity that makes the others more valuable.

## Theory of change, by leverage

| Level | How the work helps | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| One person | Fairer accommodation decisions; skilled counselling | Frame, The Room, Practice |
| A model that scales | Make Space and Creative Health manualised and run by trained facilitators | Practice |
| An institution | Evidence on which process features harm wellbeing; a redesigned intake | The Room, The Question |
| A standard | A documentation standard; seats on accessibility standards committees | Optionality |
| Systems worldwide | Disability-inclusive access adapted for global mental health settings | Global (postdoc), Optionality |

The arc climbs this ladder on purpose. Later levels reach more people, and each one is built on credibility earned at the level below.

## Career capital to build at each stage

- **Skills:** adjudication, research methods, clinical work.
- **Connections:** advisors, faculty, practicum supervisors, international collaborators.
- **Credentials:** CPACC, MSc, MA, RCC, PhD, registration.
- **Reputation:** published resources, papers, a known specialism.
- **Runway:** a salaried role with a pension from Stage 1. Security comes early, so ambition can last.

## Questions at every fork

1. Which option reaches more people, or matters more to the people it reaches?
2. Which is more neglected? Who else would do it if I don't?
3. Which can actually move something in the next one to three years?
4. Which uses what I'm unusually good at?
5. Which builds the most transferable career capital?
6. Which keeps more doors open (option value)?

**Tie-breaker:** choose the option that also advances the thesis line.
**Guardrail:** never trade the floors (health, studio, nature, security) for marginal impact. A career that lasts does more good than one that burns out.
