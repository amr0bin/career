Project Plans — Working Document

Two projects, one sequence,
and no permission needed for either.

The conceptual paper and the corpus study, each broken into stages that end on a condition a third party could confirm rather than on a date. Effort figures are estimates, marked as such. Read the interlock first — these are not two parallel projects, and running them as though they were is the main way this goes slowly.

What each one is, in one sentence.

The conceptual paper argues that current reform of professional entry standards has adopted a construct-versus-method argument without the measurement apparatus that would let anyone say whether a particular requirement is valid rather than merely preferable — and supplies that apparatus, together with a statement of what evidence would settle a disputed requirement.

The corpus study asks how Canadian professional programmes outside medicine actually write their essential requirements, and codes a stratified sample of published admissions documents against that standard.

I have taken the conceptual paper to mean the requirement-validity framework piece rather than the three-jurisdiction comparison. The comparison is smaller and sits inside this paper as its authority section; if you meant it as a standalone, the plan below splits cleanly at stage four.

The interlock

Where each project hands work to the other.

Three handoffs. They are the reason the recommended order is not both at once but capture, then write, then code — each project is cheapest at the point where the other has just finished something.

Corpus → Paper

Worked examples. The paper needs three or four real requirements, quoted from real documents, to demonstrate the instrument on. That needs ten captured documents, not fifty. Capturing ten is a weekend of mechanical work and it makes the paper concrete instead of illustrative.

Paper → Corpus

Decision rules. Coding is only defensible if you can say, in advance, what makes something a construct rather than a method — and that definition is the paper's central work. Coding fifty documents before writing it means coding to an intuition and then reverse-engineering the justification, which a reviewer will find.

Paper → Corpus

A citable frame. If the paper is preprinted before the corpus is submitted, the corpus paper cites a public frame rather than introducing one. Two papers where the second rests on the first read as a programme of work; two papers each inventing their own apparatus read as two attempts.

The premise that already moved once

AFMC reformed Canadian medical technical standards in May 2025, reaching three and a half of the four literatures. That correction is already in the essential requirements document, and both plans below are written after it: the paper's claim is the measurement half is missing, not nobody has thought about this, and the corpus deliberately excludes medicine and carries it as a reformed comparator. The lesson generalises — reform is unevenly distributed, so the check runs per profession rather than once. It is stage one of the corpus plan for that reason.

Plan One

The conceptual paper — eight stages, no data dependency.

Desk work throughout. The expensive stage is the reading, and the discipline that keeps it finite is writing a one-page statement at the end of each block rather than reading until you feel ready. Estimated total: 100 to 140 hours of focused work.

The stages

01
Fix the claim and the venue before reading anything

Write the abstract first — two hundred words, from what you already know, before a single new source. It will be wrong in interesting ways, and keeping the dated original to compare against the final version is the cheapest record you will ever have of what the reading actually changed.

How Choose the venue at the same time, because the word budget determines the structure and there is no point drafting to eight thousand words for a journal that takes six. The measurement contribution wants an audience that already holds construct validity: Advances in Health Sciences Education or Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education. Keep JPED for the corpus study — do not put both papers in front of the same editor.

Done when A dated abstract and a named target journal exist in a file, with that journal's current word limit and article type written next to them.

Cost Two to three hours.

02
Read the reform documents first, not last

The natural instinct is to read your own four literatures and then check what has been said. Invert it. Read what already exists before you build anything, because the version of this paper that restates AFMC with citations added is a real risk and it is only avoidable early.

How AFMC 2025; Curry, Meeks & Iezzoni 2020; Kezar and colleagues 2019; the Moreland 2022 document analysis; Meeks' technical standards chapter; Reichgott 1996 for the origin of the counter-argument. As you read, keep one running note answering a single question: what can this document not tell a programme that is genuinely unsure about one requirement?

Done when That note names, for each document, one specific question it leaves unanswerable.

Cost Twelve to eighteen hours.

03
Three more reading blocks, each closed by a written page

Measurement. Messick on validity as a unified concept; Kane on argument-based validity, which is the structure the paper actually borrows; the AERA/APA/NCME Standards fairness chapter, which is the authority for treating entry requirements as a measurement instrument at all; Sireci on accommodations and score comparability; van der Vleuten and Schuwirth, which is the most likely route to making a measurement argument audible inside a health professions faculty.

Law. The Meiorin three-part test and the reasoning that a standard must be shown to be adopted honestly and to be reasonably necessary; the CHRC guidance on bona fide requirements; the OHRC accessible education policy. Then the UK: the Equality Act's competence-standards carve-out, and the EHRC, OIA and Advance HE guidance sitting on it. The UK is where the doctrine is developed and the evidence is absent, which is exactly the shape of the opportunity.

Disability studies. Dolmage on academic ableism; Stergiopoulos on the discourse of the able doctor; Battalova on epistemic connection; Bulk on the invisible labour of co-created access; the episodic disability framework. This block is the one most likely to be under-read, and it is the one whose absence a Disability & Society reviewer will spot in a sentence.

How Each block closes with one page: what this literature contributes to a decision about a single disputed requirement. Not a summary — a contribution to a decision. If the page cannot be written, the block is not finished; if it can, the block is finished regardless of how much remains unread.

Done when Three pages exist, and each names something the other two cannot supply.

Cost Thirty-five to fifty hours, and this is the estimate most likely to be optimistic.

04
Build the gap audit

A table with one row per reform document and columns for the four lenses, plus a column recording whether the document cites any measurement source at all. This is the paper's evidence that it is necessary, and its virtue is that it is demonstrable from bibliographies rather than asserted from inside your own head — which is precisely how the premise went wrong last time.

How Build it from the reference lists, not from impressions. Where a document does cite measurement work, say so plainly and narrow the claim accordingly; a smaller true claim is worth more than a large one a reviewer can puncture.

Done when The table is complete and you can state the residual gap in one sentence that survives someone reading the same bibliographies.

Cost Six to eight hours.

05
Build the instrument, and test it by deletion

The four-stage analysis is the core of the paper: extract the construct, trace the authority, audit the default body and its temporality, trace the system consequence. Each stage needs a decision rule and a statement of what evidence would settle it — that second half is the contribution, because it is what turns a preference into a claim someone can be wrong about.

How Run the deletion test on the draft. Remove one lens and ask whether the output changes or only the framing. If only the framing changes, that lens is decorative and the paper is an argument wearing a tool's clothes. Stage four is the usual failure point: a framework that tells a faculty its standards are indefensible without telling it what breaks when they change is one it will thank you for and ignore.

Done when All four deletions change the output, and you can say how.

Cost Ten to fifteen hours.

06
Demonstrate it on real requirements

Three or four requirements taken verbatim from captured documents, each run through the instrument to a stated conclusion — including at least one that comes out genuinely construct-central. A framework that never validates a requirement reads as advocacy; the one that occasionally says this one holds, and here is the evidence is the one a dean believes.

How This is handoff one. It needs ten captured documents from the corpus plan, which is a weekend, and it needs them archived so the quoted wording is verifiable later. Report the examples unattributed.

Done when Four worked examples exist, at least one upholding the requirement and at least one where the construct itself is the problem rather than the method.

Cost Five to seven hours, plus the capture.

07
Draft in reverse order

Instrument first, then worked examples, then the gap audit, then implications, and the introduction last. Introductions written first commit you to a claim before you know which one you can support, and rewriting them is where conceptual papers go to die.

How Name the framework in the abstract and define it once. Call it a proposed framework, untested — that costs nothing now and protects the second version's standing. Use competence standards where you are speaking to the field and essential requirements where you are describing the Canadian documents; the terminological split across jurisdictions is itself a finding and worth a paragraph.

Done when A complete draft exists at the target journal's word limit, not above it.

Cost Twenty-five to thirty-five hours.

08
Three readers, then preprint and submit

The three readers are chosen for what each can break. A measurement specialist answers the question that would be most expensive to discover in review: does requirement validity read as a category error to someone who holds construct validity precisely? An experienced accommodation professional answers whether stage four is actionable or aspirational. A disabled practitioner or disability studies reader answers the question the CJDS audience will ask first — whether this produces institutional tools only.

How Approach each with the specific question rather than would you read this. A short, specific letter about someone's actual argument is the same move that builds the network, so this stage does double duty. Preprint on submission.

Done when Three responses are in hand, the draft has been revised against them, the preprint is posted, and the submission is acknowledged.

Cost Two to four hours of your time; four to eight weeks of elapsed time. Plan the corpus around the wait rather than sitting in it.

What would tell you to change course

  • A measurement specialist says the coinage is a category error. Then the concept survives and the name does not. Rename to the property being claimed and keep the instrument — the relocation from accommodation to requirement is the novelty, not the label.
  • The UK literature turns out to hold the framework already. Then this becomes translation and adaptation, which is still publishable and is a different first sentence. Read the UK material at stage three, not after drafting, so this is discoverable while the paper can still absorb it.
  • The gap audit comes back thin. If two or three reform documents do cite measurement work, the paper narrows to the one thing that remains absent — most likely a stated evidentiary standard — and becomes shorter and better.
Plan Two

The corpus study — ten stages, front-loaded with checks.

The first three stages are unglamorous and they are where the study is won or lost. Coding is the visible work and the smallest risk. Estimated total: 130 to 180 hours, of which roughly half is mechanical and can be done tired.

The stages

01
Run three pre-checks and log them

Each of these can change the design, and one of them has already changed it once.

Per-profession reform scan. For every candidate profession — nursing, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, social work, paramedicine, midwifery, dental hygiene, pharmacy, counselling — search the national association and accreditor for an AFMC-equivalent document. A profession that has reformed is not lost to the study; it becomes a comparator, which is a better paper. What would be fatal is finding out after coding.

The Jarus SSHRC national study. Establish which professions and which document types it covers before fixing scope. Overlap is worth knowing about deliberately — as collaboration, or as a reason to move.

The Moreland and Haywood US review. If their coding frame is published, align to it where you sensibly can. Two comparable datasets across two countries is an asset; two incomparable ones is a wasted opportunity you cannot recover later.

How Record each search — databases, terms, date run — in the same file as the results. This is the file that lets you write as far as I have been able to establish and mean it.

Done when A dated search log exists with an explicit decision recorded against each of the three.

Cost Six to ten hours.

02
Write the protocol, and settle the unit of analysis

Most of the protocol is already written across the existing documents. The one thing that is not, and that will cause more trouble than everything else combined, is what counts as a single requirement. A bullet? A sentence? A clause? A document listing sufficient vision, hearing and tactile sensation to assess a patient is either one requirement or three, and the construct-to-method ratio — your headline finding — moves depending on which you choose.

How Decide it on ten documents, write the rule with two boundary examples, and never change it silently afterwards. Also settle: professions in and out, provinces, institution types, inclusion and exclusion rules, target N of forty to sixty, and the treatment of programmes with no public document — which is itself a finding and should be recorded rather than skipped.

Done when A protocol document exists that a stranger could follow to the same sample.

Cost Eight to twelve hours.

03
Register the protocol publicly before coding anything

Free, quick, and it does two things at once. It timestamps the design, which matters because the corpus is otherwise trivially replicable by anyone with the same idea. And it forecloses the objection that the frame was fitted to the data, which is the standard objection to single-coder document analysis and the one you cannot answer after the fact.

How Deposit on the Open Science Framework with the codebook at version one. Amend openly after the pilot rather than editing quietly — a visible amendment with a stated reason is a strength, not an admission.

Done when A registration exists with a public URL and a date.

Cost Two to three hours. Verify current OSF registration types before choosing one.

04
Build the sampling frame from accreditor lists

Not from search results. Accreditor and regulator programme registries give a defensible population to sample from, which is the difference between a stratified sample of Canadian programmes and the documents I could find. Sampling logic is the methodological claim here; completeness is not.

Done when One spreadsheet holds every programme in the population, with its stratification cells filled, and the sample is drawn and marked.

Cost Eight to twelve hours.

05
Capture the whole corpus before coding any of it

PDF of every document, every URL submitted to the Internet Archive at the moment of capture, provenance recorded in the same row as the text — institution, programme, document title, URL, archive link, date accessed, and where in the admissions process the applicant meets it. Capturing everything first means the corpus is a snapshot with a stated date range rather than a smear across whatever months the coding took.

How This is the mechanical half of the project and it does not require you to be sharp. Batch it into sessions of ten. Take the first ten early, because the paper needs them.

Done when Every sampled document is saved, archived, and has a complete provenance row; the capture window is recorded as two dates.

Cost Roughly two to four hours per ten documents.

06
Pilot on twenty, then rewrite the codebook

Expect the frame to fail in specific ways. Code one will need sub-categories — a requirement can be a construct with a method appended, which is neither. Code three will need a fifth value. Code two will need a rule for requirements that gesture at authority without naming it.

How Version two of the codebook is not a tidier version one; it carries decision rules and boundary examples for every code, written from the cases that were genuinely hard. That document is publishable infrastructure in its own right.

Done when Twenty documents are coded, the codebook is at version two, and the OSF registration carries a dated amendment saying what changed and why.

Cost Ten to fifteen hours.

07
Bring in a second coder for ten documents

This is the highest return per hour in the whole project. Single-coder document analysis is publishable and weak; a reported agreement statistic on a subsample moves the paper up a tier for the cost of a few conversations.

How An accommodation professional, a programme director, or a disabled practitioner. Send the codebook, not the argument, and code independently before comparing. Where you disagree, the disagreement itself is worth a paragraph in the paper — the codes that are hard to apply are the requirements that are hard to defend.

Done when Ten documents are double-coded, agreement is calculated, and disagreements are resolved with the resolution recorded.

Cost Two to three hours of yours, plus the recruiting.

08
Code the remainder

Once the frame is stable this is steady work at roughly thirty to forty-five minutes a document. Code in profession blocks rather than randomly — it is faster, and drift within a block is easier to spot and correct than drift across the whole corpus.

How Re-code five of the pilot twenty at the end, blind to your first pass, as an internal consistency check. If those five have moved, say so in the paper and say by how much.

Done when Every document is coded and the five-document re-code is complete.

Cost Twenty-five to thirty-five hours.

09
Analyse plainly, and resist the small cells

The headline is the construct-to-method ratio. The second finding is how many requirements cite no authority at all. The third is how few documents carry a revision record. All three are counts, and the analysis needs nothing beyond a spreadsheet.

How Cross-tabulate by profession and institution type, and then be disciplined about what the cells can bear: with fifty documents across eight professions, a difference between two professions is an observation, not a result. Say observed and not significant, and the paper gets stronger rather than weaker.

Done when Every claim you intend to make has a count behind it that you could reproduce from the spreadsheet in front of a reviewer.

Cost Eight to twelve hours.

10
Deposit, preprint, submit — and harvest the by-products

Dataset and codebook to OSF under CC-BY at the moment of submission, so the infrastructure is public whatever review decides. Preprint alongside. JPED first, and the practical-implications requirement is a feature here rather than a hurdle.

How Two artifacts fall out of the coding as sorting work rather than new research: the reframing bank, which is the code-one output re-sorted by recurring requirement, and the defensibility file schema, which is what code six's absence implies. Both are citable infrastructure and both are finished in hours, not weeks. Do them before the paper clears review, not after.

Done when Dataset, codebook, preprint and submission are all public or acknowledged, and the two by-products are deposited.

Cost Thirty to forty hours for the paper; six to eight for the by-products.

What would tell you to change course

  • Three or more professions have already reformed. The study becomes a comparison of reformed and unreformed documents, which is a stronger paper with a clearer implication. It changes the sampling, so it must be discovered at stage one.
  • The SSHRC study covers your professions. Move the corpus to the ones it does not, or write to the group. Duplicating a funded national study quietly is the worst of the available options.
  • Most programmes publish nothing. If the documents are largely absent or unfindable, that is the finding — a study of what applicants can actually locate before they apply, which is a different and quite publishable paper. Do not treat it as the study failing.

Verify before acting

Journal word limits, article types, submission systems and fees change; confirm current author guidelines on each journal's own site before drafting to length. Confirm the current OSF registration types before depositing. And state the TCPS 2 reasoning for why no review is required in the methods section rather than claiming an exemption you have not been granted — a research ethics board does not certify its own exemptions in advance for unaffiliated researchers.

The first month, concretely

Four weeks that commit you to nothing you cannot revise.

Sized for a few hours a week alongside everything else. Nothing here requires the plan to be right — each week produces something that survives a change of direction.

Week one

The three pre-checks

Corpus stage one, and the highest-value hours available anywhere in either plan. It is also the week most likely to be skipped, because it produces no visible progress and it is the only work whose entire purpose is to find out that something else was wrong.

Week two

The abstract, the venue, and the first ten documents

Paper stage one in an evening. Then start capturing — ten documents, archived, with provenance rows. Those ten are the paper's worked examples and the pilot's opening batch at the same time, which is the interlock doing its job.

Week three

Read the reform documents

Paper stage two. AFMC first, then Curry, Meeks and Iezzoni, then Kezar. Keep the single running note: what can this not tell a programme that is genuinely unsure about one requirement?

Week four

Build the gap audit table, and fix the corpus scope

Paper stage four, brought forward because the reading for it is done and the table is what tells you whether the paper's claim survives. In the same week, use the week-one log to settle which professions are in, and draft the protocol.

After that

The long reading, with capture running underneath it

Paper stage three is the largest single block in either project and it wants continuous attention. Capture is the opposite — mechanical, interruptible, doable on a bad evening. Running them together is what keeps the corpus moving during the months the paper is absorbing everything.

Neither plan asks anyone for anything until stage seven of one and stage seven of the other — the three readers, and the second coder. Everything before those points is a laptop, a spreadsheet, and the willingness to write down what you searched for and when. The order matters more than the pace. If the paper takes nine months instead of four, the corpus is still better for having been coded against a frame that was published first.
