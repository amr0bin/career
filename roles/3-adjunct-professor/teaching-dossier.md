# The teaching dossier, started now

> **Purpose:** the plan for a teaching dossier built from today, so the record a teaching-stream or adjunct appointment reads has fifteen years of evidence behind it. · **Governs:** what goes into the dossier, how each piece is built, and where it lives. `decisions/2026-11-05-ubc-psychology-lecturer-benchmark.md` > this file. · **Carried out in Notion:** [Role Kits](https://app.notion.com/p/40dd55c9671b81d6a18ce91cccd7847d) (the private dossier store holds the evidence itself)

## The ceiling

By the final PhD year: a dossier with a decade and a half of delivered workshops and courses, evaluations and peer reviews from every stage, four anticipatory courses each in several dated versions, one of them built on her own research, and a teaching philosophy with an example behind every belief. Few lecturer applicants arrive with any of that; most assemble a dossier in their last year.

## What the committee asks for (UBC Psychology Lecturer, read 5 Nov 2026)

Five documents, each started now as a living draft:

| Document | Start now with |
|---|---|
| **Letter of application** (with three referees) | The future-self draft: written as the 2040 applicant, to show what is missing. Revised yearly. *Final application letters are hers alone (house rule 4).* |
| **Academic CV** | Academic format, kept separate from the industry résumé: teaching, research, presentations, service, awards |
| **Teaching philosophy** | One page from the learning-design practice; revised every year with a new example per belief |
| **Equity and inclusion statement** (one page) | A running, dated log of accessibility work, Accessible Learning Labs, SOGI workshops, peer support and Make Space, Butterflyway community work |
| **Evidence of teaching effectiveness** | This dossier: syllabi, evaluations, peer reviews, evidence-based assessments |

## Where things live

- **Here (repo):** the plan, the course designs and their reasoning, the source logs. Course exemplars are products and can go public later (andrearobin.com).

## Part 1 · Workshops already delivered

| Workshop | What it shows | Roles it supports |
|---|---|---|
| **Pronouns 101 / SOGI** (one core design, adapted for several organisations) | Tailoring one curriculum to different audiences; facilitating sensitive identity topics; equity leadership | Lecturer equity statement and dossier; Accessibility Advisor (inclusion facilitation, intersectional practice); counsellor (2SLGBTQIA+-affirming practice, see Role 2) |
| **Intro to Psychological Health and Safety** | Teaching psychology-adjacent content to non-specialists; workplace mental health; links to open-curriculum strand 9 (the National Standard, CAN/CSA-Z1003) | Lecturer (closest existing teaching of psychology content); embedded counsellor (staff training, consultation); Accessibility Advisor (accommodation, return to work) |

**Show the SOGI adaptations side by side:** the core design, then each variant with what changed and why. That is direct evidence of "a variety of tools and techniques".

### Each workshop entry

Title · dates · host (named only with permission, otherwise e.g. "a provincial non-profit, 40 staff") · audience, size, format, length · learning outcomes · design approach (activities, UDL choices, how sensitive moments were held) · adaptations and why · evidence (evaluations, feedback, testimonials, outcomes, repeat invitations) · reflection (what worked, what would change, link to the teaching philosophy) · roles it supports.

### Recovering evidence from past workshops

1. Gather slides, facilitator notes, handouts, agendas.
2. Search email for thank-yous, feedback, repeat bookings; these also fix the dates.
3. Ask each host now for two or three sentences on what was delivered and its effect. A repeat booking is evidence in itself.
4. Write a reflection for each, dated honestly as written in November 2026.

### Boundaries

- De-identify all participant feedback; never include disclosures made in a session. This matters most for SOGI and mental health content.
- Describe the psychological health and safety workshop as **education about workplace wellbeing**, not clinical work or a mental health service (house rule 1). Name the National Standard if it was the framework.

Venues: Accessible Learning Labs clients, the current employer (with its outside-activities policy read first, house rule 3), andrearobinstudio.com, community organisations, then a proposal to UBC CTLT once there is a track record. ## Part 3 · Four anticipatory courses (design exemplars)

Together: one teaching identity, **creative health, ethics and inclusion**.

| Course | Grounded in | Supports |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Ethics in Creative Practice** | BFA: cultural theory and non-western art history | Art therapy and creative health ethics; equity statement; Therapeutic Studio |
| **2 · Learning, Engagement and Health** | Neurobiology studies at Dalhousie | Health psychology subdiscipline; lecturer; counsellor |
| **3 · Disability, Accommodation and Wellbeing: A Social Model Approach** | The Applied Project, MA thesis and dissertation | Accessibility Advisor; PhD; upper-level psychology |
| **4 · Creative Health: Arts, Learning and Wellbeing** (later capstone) | Courses 1–3 together | Therapeutic Studio; lecturer; Creative Health |

### 1 · Ethics in Creative Practice

Threads: cultural appropriation and borrowing; who tells which stories; decolonising art history; ethics of art made in therapeutic and community settings (consent, ownership, display); AI and creative authorship.
**Task:** Indigenous cultural protocols belong here, and UBC Psychology names Indigenous priorities. Centre Indigenous scholars and sources, cite them directly, and seek Indigenous review rather than speaking for those communities.

### 2 · Learning, Engagement and Health

Threads: education as a social determinant of health; arts engagement and health outcomes; learning, neuroplasticity and cognitive reserve; stress, learning and wellbeing.
Anchor: the WHO Regional Office for Europe scoping review on the arts in health and wellbeing (Fancourt and Finn, 2019; **verify** citation, item 59).
**Task:** a module on spotting neuromyths (learning styles, left-brain/right-brain). It shows rigour and research literacy and keeps the course from overclaiming.

### 3 · Disability, Accommodation and Wellbeing (grows with the research)

| Version | When | Adds |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | Now → 2027 (runway) | Syllabus on published disability studies and accommodation research; the project's question as a module |
| v2 | Leeds Applied Project | Pilot findings and methods as a worked case; a "how this study was built" methods module |
| v3 | Counselling MA | Thesis findings on accommodation burden and fluctuation; a guest lecture or TA delivery |
| v4 | PhD | Dissertation-based upper-level course, taught as a sessional if possible |

Draft arc for v1: models of disability → how psychology has studied disability → accommodation as a process (burden, negotiation, fluctuation) → fluctuating and invisible conditions over time → reading the research critically (methods bridge) → universal design in education and work → wellbeing, community and creative health → from findings to action.
Design features: methods as content; accessibility built into every assessment; a module on turning findings into policy and practice.
**Task:** teach from published or approved results only, never raw data or identifiable stories. Check that Leeds and later UBC ethics approvals allow teaching use of findings, and write that into consent wording from the start (item 60).
Later: the course itself as a scholarship-of-teaching study in the PhD.

### 4 · Creative Health capstone

Designed once courses 1–3 have at least one version each.

### What each exemplar produces for the dossier

Full syllabus with access statement · outcomes mapped to assessments (`../../learning-design/templates/alignment-table.md`) · UDL rationale · one fully built sample module · an accessible assessment design · a source log · a reflection linking it to the teaching philosophy · a short peer review from an academic in the field.

### Sourcing method

- **Source log** per course: full citation, DOI or stable link, source type, one line on why it is credible.
- **Evidence hierarchy:** systematic reviews, meta-analyses, peer-reviewed journals, university presses first; each finding marked *established*, *emerging* or *contested*.
- **Verify independently:** confirm each source exists and says what it is cited for; read past the abstract for anything central.
- **Label honestly:** "course design exemplar"; her role stated as designer; what her own coursework contributes listed.
- **Expert review:** one academic per course (former instructors at Dalhousie and the BFA institution are natural first asks; each is also a possible mentor or referee).

### Boundaries for all exemplars

- Labelled as exemplars or proposed courses, never as courses taught.
- No clinical or therapeutic skills content before training and registration (house rule 1).
- Expressive arts appear as learning activities, never as therapy; "art therapy" is not used before the VATI diploma.
- Teaching examples are published cases or composites, never client-derived (house rule 2).
- Lived experience appears only where and when she chooses (house rule 6).

## Support that multiplies this

- **Mentors:** teaching-stream faculty in UBC Psychology, especially any who came from applied or counselling backgrounds; the department's Associate Head, EDI, when the equity statement is ready to discuss.
- **Structures:** UBC CTLT programmes and its scholarship-of-teaching community; Psychology TA training (**verify** item 58).
- **Funding:** SSHRC doctoral awards; teaching and learning enhancement grants (funded pedagogy projects are CV evidence).
- **Early relationships:** accessibility reviews or course redesign offered to UBC instructors through Accessible Learning Labs, years before applying.

**Fallback, once:** if no lecturer post is open at the end of the PhD, sessional and adjunct contracts are the bridge and build the next application.

## Yearly rhythm

Each autumn: revise the teaching philosophy; add the year's deliveries and evaluations; version any anticipatory course that moved; update the referee tracker; reread the benchmark posting and any new ones against the dossier.
