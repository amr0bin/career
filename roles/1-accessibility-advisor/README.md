# Role 1 · Accessibility Advisor (UBC or SFU)

> **When:** Stage 1, held from 2027 (target) through the MA practicum at a negotiated fraction. **Where:** UBC Centre for Accessibility; SFU Centre for Accessible Learning. **Governing instruments:** UBC Policy LR7 (*Accommodation for Students with Disabilities*); SFU Policy GP 26 (*Accommodations for Students with Disabilities*); BC *Human Rights Code*; *Meiorin* and *Moore*; the *Accessible British Columbia Act*.

## The ceiling for this role

Advisor decisions that are fast, minimal in what they ask students to prove, legally sound and kind, together with a steady record of **moving barriers out of the process**: a documentation requirement narrowed, a re-application cycle removed, a programme's essential requirements rewritten as constructs. The role becomes the field site and the evidence for the thesis question, and it is a six-figure, pensioned career in its own right.

---

## Competencies

**Framework:** AHEAD *Professional Competencies* and *Program Domains, Standards and Performance Indicators*; the IAAP **CPACC** body of knowledge; the OHRC *Policy on Accessible Education for Students with Disabilities* (2018) as the most detailed Canadian guidance.

| Competency | What good looks like | How to build it before the role | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Disability knowledge and models** | Explains functional impact through the social model; knows episodic and fluctuating conditions | CPACC; Leeds *Understanding Disability* | CPACC; module essay |
| **Human rights law and policy** | Applies *Meiorin*, *Moore*, the BC Code, LR7 / GP 26 and undue hardship correctly | Read the instruments in full; one BC Human Rights Tribunal decision a month | Casebook memos |
| **Adjudication and determination writing** | Decides on documentation of functional impact, not diagnosis; writes rationales a student and a tribunal can follow | One invented determination a week | `../../structures/ACCOMMODATION_RATIONALE_MEMO.md` |
| **Essential requirements consultation** | Helps faculty separate the construct from the method; proposes alternatives that keep the standard | Assessment design practice (learning-design 05); corpus study coding | A worked consultation (invented) |
| **Intake and interviewing** | Warm, efficient, trauma-informed intake that asks only what is needed | Crisis-line training; intake template practice | Supervisor feedback |
| **Universal design and digital accessibility** | Recommends course-level changes that remove the need for individual accommodations | Learning-design kit; WCAG 2.2; UDL 3.0 | Audit case study |
| **Case management** | Holds a caseload with clear notes, review dates and follow-up | Practise the caseload tracker on invented cases | — |
| **Exam and assistive technology operations** | Knows how exam accommodations, alternate formats and AT are delivered | Shadowing; AHEAD and CACUSS sessions | — |
| **Referral and collaboration** | Knows the campus map: counselling, health, advising, Indigenous student services, equity office | Build the referral map in the first 30 days | Referral map |
| **Confidentiality and privacy** | FIPPA-literate; shares only what the instructor needs (the accommodation, never the diagnosis) | Read FIPPA basics and the unit's privacy policy | — |
| **Cultural safety and equity** | Recognises intersecting barriers; refers to culturally safe supports | Indigenous cultural safety training (e.g. San'yas) | Certificate |

## Systems

| Rhythm | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Caseload tracker updated; notes written the same day; letters issued within the service standard |
| **Weekly** | Review dates checked; one consultation with a colleague on a hard case; reflection log entry (helping hours) |
| **Monthly** | One tribunal decision read; pattern review: which *process* features generated repeat contacts or appeals (anonymised, aggregate) |
| **Termly** | Exam-period readiness; faculty outreach on UDL; one improvement proposal |
| **Yearly** | Accessibility plan contribution under the *Accessible BC Act*; CACUSS or AHEAD |

**Records:** institutional case-management system only. Personal reflection is anonymised and kept in Notion Private. Nothing about a real student leaves the office.

## Tools

The unit's accommodation case-management system (find out which one at hire; Clockwork and Symplicity Accommodate are common in Canada) · exam scheduling and alternate-format workflows · assistive technology (screen readers, text-to-speech, speech-to-text, note-taking tools) to understand, not to administer · WCAG and PDF checkers (see `../../learning-design/08_accessibility_and_inclusivity.md`) · Teams / Zoom with captions.

See `../../learning-design/14_next_role_and_portfolio.md` for applications, the scenario interview and the 90-day plan. The advisor-specific addition: in days 1–30, build the **referral map** and read every unit procedure; in days 61–90, propose one process fix from the process-barrier log.

## Boundaries

- **Never a real case** in any public piece or portfolio, even de-identified (house rule 5).
- **Never research your own caseload.** The thesis recruits through a DPO or NEADS, not the office (house rule 3).
- **No dual roles:** students you advise are not Make Space or peer-programme participants while you advise them.
- **Freelance:** decline any Accessible Learning Labs engagement involving your employer's processes or students.

## What it hands forward

The adjudication record; staff tuition waiver for the prerequisites; helping hours; referees; an insider's view of the process the thesis studies; the practicum route into campus counselling.
