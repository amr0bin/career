Training · Free and low-cost

The open curriculum

Everything reputable that can be studied without enrolment, permission, or tuition — sequenced against the CPACC, the Leeds MSc, the five counselling prerequisites, and the studio days that prepare the doctoral application. Revised September 2026 to follow the six-stage plan in career.html. Cost and availability are stated plainly, including where something is not actually free.

How to read this. Nothing here substitutes for the credentials already in the plan. What free training does is different: it closes specific knowledge gaps ahead of paid study, it produces certificates that make a CV legible to hiring managers who cannot assess fifteen years of learning design at a glance, and — in two cases below — it puts a person in a supervised room with people in distress, which no course can do. Verify enrolment windows before planning around any single item; several of these run on annual or seasonal intakes.

Two tracks are timed rather than optional. Track six is the five undergraduate prerequisites, placed against the fixed deadlines of the counselling master's application; Track seven is the studio days — every Sunday and Monday evening — that prepare the application to a doctorate in Contemporary Arts, Interactive Arts and Technology, or Art History. Neither is free training in the usual sense; both are here because this is the document that holds the calendar.

Tracks eight and nine are keyed to two named postings. Track eight is the MHPSS coordination layer that a Canadian Red Cross MHPSS Specialist role screens for, above the first-aid layer in Track three; Track nine is workplace psychological health and safety, the field a PHSA Specialist, Psychological Health & Safety role sits in. Both were added in September 2026 from a reading of those postings, and both are options rather than commitments: the credential plan's rules still hold, and the only item in either track that carries real weight is a volunteer role, not a course.

The last two sections are not courses. Tracks one to five, eight and nine are things to study. What follows is what studying does not solve: two decisions that will cost more the later they are made, and a set of rooms to be in rather than certificates to hold. They are here because the catalogue above would otherwise read as though the whole gap were a knowledge gap, and it is not.

Track two

Accessibility standards and the built environment

CPACC Domains II and III. The digital side is already a fifteen-year strength; the honest gaps are the built environment, procurement, and the legal frameworks. That is where these should be pointed.

Digital Accessibility Foundations

The authoritative introduction, built on the W3C WAI open curricula and taught by trainers from Deque, Intopia, Knowbility, TPG, Infoaxia, and Web Key IT. Covers what web accessibility is, people and digital technology, the business case, and first steps in applying WCAG and WAI-ARIA.

Track three

Psychological first aid and the humanitarian model

This is the Red Cross track, and it is the closest available approximation of the training stack that makes those practitioners so credible. It is also the honest, self-limiting answer to the counselling question: PFA is not therapy, and every one of these courses says so. What it builds is the capacity to be useful in a room where someone is in distress, which is the substrate the clinical training will later sit on.

IFRC Psychological First Aid e-learning
International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies, with the Danish Red Cross

Ninety minutes, self-paced. How to comfort someone in distress and help them feel safe and calm; common patterns of reaction to crisis; connecting people to further support; and self-care. Developed under the international Psychological First Aid for All pledge, which means it is the version the Red Cross network itself uses.

Hosted on the IFRC Learning Platform. Registration and use of that platform are completely open and free to the general public, not just Red Cross volunteers, and courses can be downloaded for offline study.

Free90 minThe Red Cross model
IFRC Learning Platform and Health & Care Training Platform
IFRC

Hundreds of short, free, multilingual courses with communities of practice attached, plus a dedicated health hub covering migration and health, community-based health and first aid, climate and health, and MHPSS. Free and open to anyone, not only to network volunteers.

Treat this as the browse-and-build source. Three or four completed courses here plus the PFA module is a coherent humanitarian-health block on a CV, and it is the closest a person can get to the Red Cross training culture without joining a National Society.

Introducing Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) in Emergencies
World Health Organization, on OpenWHO

Around seven hours, taken all at once or across sittings, with a test-based certificate and a shareable digital badge. That last section is the task-shifting evidence base stated in WHO's own words. It is the direct precursor to the Global Mental Health certificate in Stage two and, further out, to the training manual for Make Space.

Track four

Supervised practice — the part no course can replace

This is the track that matters most and the only one with a real deadline. Counselling psychology programs, and the people who write reference letters for them, do not weigh certificates the way they weigh documented hours in a supervised helping role. Everything above is preparation for this.

Crisis Services Volunteer
Crisis Intervention and Suicide Prevention Centre of BC — Vancouver

110 hours of basic training including two to four hours a week of online modules, then 32 hours of supervised call-taking, then a commitment of 250 credited hours of direct service. The Centre describes its training and ongoing supervision as well recognised by universities and helping agencies across the province — which is the whole point of doing it.

Applications for the next group are not currently open. There is a notification form for the November 2026 training group; filling it in does not hold a place. Training carries a fee on a sliding scale, discussed at interview. This is the one item on this page with a clock attached.

Track five

Research literacy and ethics

Small track, high leverage. The qualitative paper from Make Space needs an ethics footing, and the Leeds application is stronger for arriving with one.

TCPS 2: CORE — Course on Research Ethics
Interagency Advisory Panel on Research Ethics — CIHR, NSERC, and SSHRC

Nine modules, about four hours, self-paced, free, with a certificate of completion. It is the Canadian standard: every graduate student conducting human-participant research submits this certificate with their ethics application, and most Canadian institutions require it.

Do it before the Make Space paper rather than after. It costs an afternoon and it changes how the consent, privacy, and participant-welfare sections of that paper get written — which is precisely where a paper submitted without a doctoral affiliation is most likely to be challenged.

Track six

The five prerequisites, timed

UBC's counselling psychology master's — MA or MEd — requires five senior undergraduate courses, completed by 31 December of the year of application: basic interviewing or helping skills, theories of counselling, learning theory or cognitive psychology, abnormal psychology, and statistics. Career counselling was removed from the requirement from the 2026 cycle. The application portal opens around 20 September and closes 1 December, with references due 15 December; there is one intake, in September; and the department no longer adjudicates equivalency in advance, so courses are checked against its published equivalency table before enrolling. The master's may be taken part-time and must be completed within five years, which is what makes a working student's version of Stage three possible.

The anchor dates
Leeds intake September 2027 · Counselling application 1 December 2029 · Prerequisites complete 31 December 2029 · Entry September 2030

The Leeds MSc runs twenty-four months part-time from a September 2027 start, closing on the applied project in the autumn of 2029. The counselling application is made in the same window — 1 December 2029 — so that Stage two closes and Stage three opens without a gap year. Five courses fit between January 2027 and December 2029 at one per term, with no course in the two terms that carry the first Leeds module and the crisis line hours. A March 2027 Leeds start moves nothing in this table.

If the master's is taken at SFU, Adler, or Athabasca instead, the prerequisite lists differ. Check each against this one so that a single set of five courses serves whichever programme is chosen; the UBC list is the most demanding and is used here as the reference.

Track seven

Studio days — the doctoral application, prepared weekly

Every Sunday from ten until six, and every Monday evening, as set out at the studio-day site. These are not rest days between the curriculum's tracks; they are the track that prepares the application to a PhD in Contemporary Arts at SFU, in Interactive Arts and Technology at SIAT, or in Art History at UBC. Each of the three reads a different record, and all three records are built on the same standing order. The application itself is years away; the record cannot be assembled retroactively, which is why it starts now.

What each door reads
SFU School for the Contemporary Arts · SFU School of Interactive Arts and Technology · UBC Art History, Visual Art and Theory

Contemporary Arts reads a body of studio work, a shown record, and a written proposal for a practice-based or scholarly thesis; a supervisor is matched on admission and applications open on 1 October for the following September. Interactive Arts and Technology admits on academic preparation, fit with a research faculty member, and a supervisory agreement made before applying — roughly one applicant in ten is offered a place — and reads a letter of intent that sets out the research question and a portfolio of design or research work. Art History reads a writing sample and a research proposal, and its stated norm at entry is a master's in art history; whether it admits on this file is a verify item in career.html. The Sunday builds the first and second records; the Monday evening builds the third.

Track eight

MHPSS coordination and the humanitarian system

Track three builds the capacity to be useful in a room with someone in distress. This track is the layer above it, and it is what the Canadian Red Cross MHPSS Specialist posting actually screens for: the verbs there are deploy, assess, oversee, coordinate referrals, brief personnel, maintain operational trackers, write end-of-mission reports. That is a technical-advisor and coordination role inside an emergency operations structure, and the training that makes those practitioners credible is the IASC and IFRC coordination stack plus emergency-management literacy. Added September 2026 after reading the CRC posting against this page. Almost all of it is free; the item that matters most is not a course.

IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings
Inter-Agency Standing Committee — Reference Group on MHPSS

The reference document for the whole field. It gives the intervention pyramid, the multi-sectoral minimum-response matrix, and the coordination architecture that every humanitarian MHPSS role is written against. The CRC posting's assessment and service-plan duties are lifted from it almost verbatim. Read the Guidelines, then the Reference Group's Assessment Guide and its Common Monitoring and Evaluation Framework, which are the two documents a Technical Advisor is expected to be able to apply on arrival.

Reading, not a course, and the equivalent of what the CPACC body of knowledge is for the accessibility work: the thing to read cover to cover before anything else in this track.

IASC MHPSS MSP (WHO, UNICEF, UNHCR, IOM and partners)

A regularly updated database of online and offline MHPSS courses from UN agencies, NGOs and universities, filterable by which MSP activity they serve, by modality, duration, language, and whether they are free. It is the closest thing that exists to a syllabus for what MHPSS specialists actually study, and it is the browse-and-build source for this track in the way the IFRC platform is for Track three.

Track nine

Workplace psychological health and safety

A different layer again, and the one the PHSA Specialist, Psychological Health & Safety posting is written against. The verbs there are assess readiness, apply tools to analyse current state, present findings to leadership, recommend an implementation plan — all measured against the thirteen factors of the National Standard of Canada for Psychological Health and Safety in the Workplace. That is a management-system role inside human resources, closer to the learning-design portfolio than to the humanitarian thread, and it names motivational interviewing, coaching, trauma-informed practice, and the Indigenous cultural safety foundational documents explicitly. Added September 2026. Nearly everything here is free and measured in hours; the one legible credential is employer-funded or not at all.

The National Standard (CAN/CSA-Z1003-13) and Assembling the Pieces
CSA Group, Bureau de normalisation du Québec, and the Mental Health Commission of Canada

A voluntary standard specifying requirements for a documented, systematic approach to a psychologically healthy and safe workplace, built on a plan-do-check-act cycle and thirteen psychosocial factors. The PHSA posting's duties are the Standard's cycle written out as a job description. Both the Standard and the implementation handbook, Assembling the Pieces, are available to read or download at no cost from CSA.

Read cover to cover. It is to this track what the CPACC body of knowledge is to Track two and the IASC Guidelines are to Track eight: the document that the interview questions will be drawn from.

FreeReadingThe reference document
CCOHS free courses — psychological health and safety block
Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety, with the Mental Health Commission of Canada

Four short courses, together about half a day: Psychological Health and Safety Awareness (twenty minutes, the six categories of psychosocial factors, no certificate); Reducing Mental Health Stigma in the Workplace (thirty minutes); Being a Mindful Employee: An Orientation to Psychological Health and Safety (led by the MHCC, with a printable certificate on passing the exam); and the Assembling the Pieces Toolkit course, which walks through implementing the Standard. CCOHS content is reviewed by labour, employer and government representatives, which is what gives these lines their credibility on a health-authority CV.

Guarding Minds at Work
Workplace Strategies for Mental Health (Canada Life), distributed with CCOHS

This is the tool to assess and analyse current state that the posting means. Free to any employer, it provides an eight-step audit process with surveys, worksheets and reports on the psychosocial risk factors, benchmarked against a national sample, and is designed to support implementation of the Standard. The gap

What the advisor postings actually ask for

Worth stating directly, because it changes what to study. UBC's Accessibility Advisor postings ask for an undergraduate degree, five to six years of related experience or an equivalent combination, and — the operative line — considerable training and experience in disability, universal design, and duty to accommodate in a post-secondary setting. A graduate degree in a relevant discipline is preferred. The work is described as developing organisational change initiatives, policies, and educational programs, determining and facilitating accommodations, managing complex accommodation cases, and consulting with faculty on how policy affects access.

Read against that, the accessibility knowledge is not the bottleneck. Fifteen years of learning design plus the CPACC covers universal design and standards comfortably. Two things are missing, and neither is a course problem in the way it first appears.

Duty to accommodate as a legal practice. Undue hardship, the interactive process, medical documentation, and the BC Human Rights Code as it applies to educational services. This is human-rights law applied to individual cases, and it is what an Accessibility Advisor does most days. Provincial human rights commission policy guidance and tribunal decisions are free and are the actual source material; the Ontario Human Rights Commission's policy on disability and the duty to accommodate is the most cited plain-language document in the country, useful comparatively even though BC has its own Code. Free webinars from the Government of Canada accessibility hub and the CSPS video series on disability inclusion cover the federal framing.

The post-secondary setting itself. This one is closed by proximity, not study. CACUSS — the Canadian Association of College and University Student Services — runs the accessibility community of practice where these advisors actually gather, and NEADS is the national student-led disability organisation. Membership, conference attendance, and one or two informational conversations with a current UBC or SFU advisor will do more for a candidacy than another certificate.

The Make Space paper has an affiliation problem, not only an ethics problem
Stage one · before a word of it is drafted

TCPS 2: CORE teaches the Canadian standard. It does not provide a Research Ethics Board, and peer-reviewed journals will ask which REB approved the study. Retrospective approval for data already collected is difficult everywhere and effectively unavailable without an institutional affiliation.

There are three real exits. Carry it into Leeds and run it under the university's ethics approval as part of the forty-five-credit applied project — safest, and later than currently planned. Reframe the genre as practice reflection, program evaluation, or autoethnography, which sit outside human-subjects review but land in different journals with different conventions. Pay an independent REB — expensive, and awkward for unfunded community work.

The choice changes the study design, the consent language, and the target journal, which is why it has to be made first. What does not work is writing it as research and hoping the question is not asked.

The method already exists in public. Fieldwork on Accessible Learning Labs is a 24-part inquiry into research methods for inclusive learning design, with its Foundations phase published and its literature, Investigation, and Synthesis phases to follow. The cleanest exit is to let the series carry the method to the point where it needs a setting and an ethics board, and to let the Leeds applied project be that setting. The public series and the supervised study are then one piece of work at two stages rather than two problems.

Patient partner with the BC SUPPORT Unit
Michael Smith Health Research BC, under Canada's Strategy for Patient-Oriented Research

The Unit exists to bring people with lived experience into health research as partners rather than participants — setting priorities, shaping study design, sitting on review panels. Its scientific director has described patient involvement as the feedback loop that makes research translate, and the Unit has supported patient partners who had never written an academic paper to be authors on research about their own experience. Partner voices have also been brought in as reviewers on Health Research BC funding competitions.

Free, provincial, with training provided. It supplies the one thing the plan currently has no route to: a named role on a real research team, alongside researchers who can eventually write an academic reference. Regional centres sit inside Fraser Health and Vancouver Coastal among others.

A seat on an accessibility committee
Required under the Accessible British Columbia Act (2021)

Since September 2023, prescribed public bodies in BC — post-secondary institutions, school districts, municipalities, health authorities, regional districts — have been legally required to establish an accessibility committee, publish an accessibility plan, and run a public feedback mechanism. The committee's job is to identify barriers and advise the organisation on removing and preventing them.

The composition rules are the opening. The Act directs that, so far as possible, at least half the members be disabled people or people from organisations that support disabled people, that the membership reflect the diversity of disabled people in BC, and that at least one member be an Indigenous person. These committees recruit publicly and meet a few times a year.

It is a seat inside the institutions the advisor role sits in, without needing to be hired first — and it is governance experience against real policy, which is exactly what the postings mean by organisational change. UBC, SFU, KPU, Douglas, the City of Surrey, and Fraser Health all have one.

A working relationship with a disabled people's organisation
Disability Alliance BC · BC Aboriginal Network on Disability Society

The Leeds tradition — Oliver, Barnes, Finkelstein, UPIAS — holds that disability research should be accountable to organisations controlled by disabled people rather than conducted about them by professionals. A portfolio strong on professional credentials and thin on organised disability community reads differently at Leeds than it does to a Canadian hiring manager.

Disability Alliance BC co-designed the Accessibility Toolkit the province's public sector used to build the committees above, including templates for recruitment, terms of reference, and feedback mechanisms — which makes it both the obvious partner and a free, well-made reference for how this work is actually structured. BCANDS holds the Indigenous disability file.

The direct payoff: the Leeds applied project may be run in partnership with an organisation. A DPO is a stronger partner for that project than an employer, given what the programme values.

One note on sequence. Stage one holds a full-time role, the CPACC, four public resources, a submitted paper, a Make Space cohort with pre- and post-data, a move into post-secondary, the crisis training and weekly shifts, the design certificate, and the studio days — and this page adds a committee seat, a research partnership, and an ethics decision. The plan is stronger for deciding in advance what the order is than for deciding it in month seven. The order here: the studio days and the crisis line are fixed; the prerequisites are dated; the certificates and the committee seat take the terms that are held clear; and the Make Space paper waits for Leeds, where it has a supervisor, a deadline, and an ethics board — which is the same conclusion the affiliation problem reaches on separate grounds.
