## 4 · The conceptual paper's framing · *pending*

**Question.** Which argument does the first conceptual paper make?
**The four candidate framings.**
1. **The four-literature framework.** An essential requirement is defensible only if it survives construct validity, the duty to accommodate (*Meiorin*), disability studies' *whose body?*, and programme design. No institutional role holds more than two, which is why failing requirements go unexamined. Target: CJDS. *(Recommended 7 Sep 2026 as the frame the corpus codes come from.)*
2. **The three-pillars hypothesis.** A learning environment needs accessibility, disability justice and emergent learning at once; where policy and experience diverge, one is missing. This is Fieldwork's frame, later the frame for the Leeds interview study.
3. **The depression-care workforce ("whose mind?").** Entry requirements for the professions that treat depression ("emotional stability", coping and resilience, full-time attendance) may screen out applicants with lived experience. Same four tests, with the hardest case.
4. **Training systems for depression care** (the June 2026 framing). Designing the learning systems that train practitioners to deliver depression interventions matters as much as designing the interventions (task-shifting lens).

**What decides it.** The UK competence-standards reading (Equality Act 2010), which settles whether framing 1 is origination or translation; whether framing 3's clauses hold up across a pilot of ten Canadian documents; and which framing the corpus study and Leeds most need first.
**By when.** Before the corpus pilot is coded; the paper is targeted for submission in 2027.
