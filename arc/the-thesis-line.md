## The question

**Does the university accommodation process act as a disabling barrier in its own right, and through which of its design features does it shape disabled students' wellbeing and help-seeking?**

The question takes the social model to heart. It does not sort students by diagnosis or condition. It studies the process: what the process asks students to prove, how long it makes them wait, how often it makes them prove it again, and what it assumes about stability and continuous availability. Fluctuation enters as an assumption built into the process, not as a category of person. A process that expects a condition to look the same in October as in March disables anyone whose life does not, whatever the diagnosis.

The theoretical anchor is the Leeds tradition itself. Carol Thomas's *psycho-emotional disablism* and Donna Reeve's work on it describe barriers that act on wellbeing and on who a person can be, not only on what they can do. The accommodation process is a place where that happens inside a single institution, on a schedule the institution controls. That is why it can be changed.

The outcome measures (wellbeing, depressive symptoms, help-seeking) are read as effects of the barrier, not as properties of the student.

## The Applied Project as pilot

- **Who takes part:** disabled students and recent graduates who have gone through, abandoned or decided against a university accommodation process. They are recruited by experience of the process, not by diagnosis.
- **Where they come from:** not her own workplace or caseload. Recruitment runs through a disabled people's organisation or a national student organisation (for example NEADS), or another institution, which settles the dual-role question cleanly.
- **Design:** mixed methods, small and deliberate.
  - A short survey of process features (documentation asked for, waits, re-proving, fit with fluctuation) alongside validated wellbeing, depressive-symptom and help-seeking measures.
  - Interviews with a subset, to explain the pattern.
- **Who designs it:** in the emancipatory tradition. A paid disabled co-researcher and a small advisory group help shape the questions and read the findings.
- **What "pilot" means here:** it tests recruitment, the acceptability of the measures, completion rates and the process-feature instrument. The findings are preliminary patterns, not proof.
- **Alongside it:** the corpus study (stages 5–8) is finished in module breaks and credited within the project. Its sampling frame of programmes and their requirements helps target recruitment.

## Conditions that make it work for both programmes

1. **Two write-ups from one study.** The Leeds report leads with the social model, emancipatory design and what the process does to people. The UBC proposal leads with measurement, help-seeking and campus mental health services. The data and the question are the same; the framing is written for each reader.
2. **Measures read as effects of the barrier.** Disability studies examiners may challenge clinical symptom scales as individualising. Say plainly that they measure what the barrier costs, place them inside psycho-emotional disablism, and give the qualitative strand equal weight.
3. **Consent for future use.** The Leeds consent form and data management plan must allow the anonymised pilot data and instruments to inform later research, including the MA thesis. Without this, the pilot can shape the design but its data cannot be reused.
4. **Confirm with Leeds first.** Before Year 2, check that the Applied Project supports primary data collection with participants outside the UK, and how long Leeds ethics review takes.
5. **Hold the design loosely for the supervisor.** A UBC supervisor will want the MA to fit their lab. The pilot proves readiness and gives a starting design, not a fixed one.

## What the supervisor sees, and when

1. **2029:** Applied Project report, plus a one-page note fitting the question to one UBC lab's programme.
2. **2030:** a conversation about the full design, with the pilot's instruments and patterns in hand.
3. **Autumn 2030:** a draft MA thesis proposal, which doubles as the CGRS M research proposal (CGRS M replaced CGS-M in 2025). One document serves the application, the scholarship and the thesis.

## Support that multiplies

- The Leeds supervisor becomes a research referee.
- The DPO partner becomes a standing advisory group that continues through the MA and the PhD.
- The co-researcher can become a co-author.
- The pilot data makes CGRS M, BC Graduate Scholarship and Mitacs applications concrete.

**Fallback, once:** if recruitment for the pilot is thin, the Applied Project reports on the instrument's development and a smaller interview set. That is still a tested design for the MA.
