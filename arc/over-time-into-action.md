# Over time, and into action

> **Purpose:** the "over time" doctoral route (how process burden and fluctuation interact across a term) designed so that what it finds is acted on: during the study, every term, after the study and beyond one institution. · **Governs:** the action layer of the doctoral design and what the MA and Leeds work must prepare for it. The question and sequence are governed by `the-thesis-line.md`; the MA design by `ma-thesis-design.md`. · **Status:** her leading interest among the doctoral routes (4 Nov 2026); a design to bring to the doctoral supervisor, not a decision. · **Carried out in Notion:** [Gates](https://app.notion.com/p/d4969021f8da4529a41eafec0c323ca4) (view: *Thesis line*), [Tasks](https://app.notion.com/p/1dca49d1884242e28a2f2d21f1216be9)

## The ceiling

A dissertation that follows disabled students week by week through a term at two or three partner institutions, and is wired to act from the first day:

- The institutions' access and counselling services agree **in advance** what they are prepared to change, depending on what the study finds.
- Students in the study are **looked after while it runs**, under a safety protocol designed with counselling services.
- Services get **aggregate results back every term**, so the process starts improving before the dissertation is finished.
- The findings drive a **co-designed, timed response** (support that arrives at the moments the process becomes most burdensome), tested in the same institutions.
- What works travels **beyond them**: into accessibility standards, counsellor training and the joined access-and-mental-health service at the end of the arc.

**The principle:** knowing is the first half of a feedback loop. The study is built as integrated knowledge translation, with the people who own the levers involved from the design stage, not told at the end.

## What "over time" can find that a snapshot can't

- **When burden spikes:** at submission, during waits, at renewal deadlines, when documentation is re-requested, around exams.
- **Whether burden comes before low mood, or follows it:** the order matters for where to intervene.
- **How fluctuation meets the process:** whether a process that assumes stability does most harm in the weeks a person's capacity changes.
- **When help-seeking happens or stalls:** whether a burdensome step is followed by less counselling use, not more.

Each of these points to a *timed* lever, which is what makes the findings actionable rather than general.

## The study

- **Who and where:** disabled students at two or three partner institutions (not her own employer), recruited by experience of the process, with a national arm through the partner organisation.
- **Design:** an intensive longitudinal study across one term (about 12–14 weeks).
  - **Weekly, under three minutes:** a short form of the MA's process-burden instrument, process events that week (submitted, waiting, re-proving requested, approved, denied), capacity and its variability, a brief mood check, and help-seeking actions.
  - **Start and end of term:** the full instrument and full outcome measures.
- **Analysis:** multilevel and dynamic models; event-centred analyses around process steps; preregistered.
- **The study practises what it studies:** flexible response windows, accessible formats, payment per response, and analyses built to handle missing weeks. A study about processes that assume constant availability must not assume it either.

## Four layers of action

### 1 · During the study: care

- **A safety protocol** written with the partner counselling services and approved by ethics: what the mood check can trigger, who responds, how fast, and where people are referred.
- **Every participant gets a resource sheet** for access and counselling services at their institution, and a plain-language summary of findings at the end.
- **Participants as experts:** a paid student advisory group meets each month of the study to interpret patterns as they emerge.

### 2 · Each term: the feedback loop

- **An aggregate report to each partner service every term** (audit and feedback): which steps carried the most burden, where waits clustered, how often re-proving was asked for. Descriptive and aggregated only, agreed in the ethics application, and kept separate from the confirmatory analysis.
- **A standing meeting** with the named decision-maker at each service to read it together. Each meeting is logged, with the summary sent back (`philosophy/the-two-tracks.md`, Practice 2).

### 3 · After the study: redesign and test

- **The lever map** (started in the MA) says which process feature each finding points to, who owns it, and how hard it is to change.
- **Co-design** with students, access advisors and counsellors: timed responses such as proactive contact before a renewal deadline, documentation that does not expire for conditions expected to fluctuate, or a warm handoff to counselling when a request is denied.
- **The test**, chosen with the supervisor:
  - a **micro-randomised trial**, which tests which in-the-moment supports help at which moments, or
  - a **stepped-wedge rollout** across partner institutions, which tests a process redesign while every site eventually receives it.
- This is where the "over time" route joins the "redesign and test" route. One dissertation can hold both.

### 4 · Beyond one institution: policy and practice

- **A brief for process owners**, written with the partner, for every finding.
- **Standards:** submissions or a committee seat where accessibility standards for post-secondary education are being written (federal and provincial routes; **verify** current processes).
- **Counsellor training:** a module on accommodation burden as a risk factor, for campus counselling teams and the adjunct teaching role.
- **Practice:** the joined access-and-mental-health service in Stage 3, and the peer and creative-health programmes at Therapeutic Studio, built from the findings.

## The action agreement

Before data collection, each partner institution signs a short agreement, a preregistration for action:

1. The **named decision-maker** in the access service, and one in counselling services.
2. **The levers they own** and are willing to consider changing (from the lever map).
3. **How findings come back** (termly report, meeting) and what is shared.
4. **What they commit to report back:** which changes they considered, made or declined, and why.
5. **Safety protocol** roles and referral pathways.

Without a named decision-maker and listed levers, a site is a recruitment site, not a partner.

## Acting before the dissertation

Some action does not need to wait for doctoral evidence:

- **In the advisor role:** apply what the published literature, the corpus study and the pilot already show, within policy (for example, documentation timelines that fit fluctuation), and keep a de-identified practice log of process barriers (`roles/1-accessibility-advisor/templates/process-barrier-log.md`).
- **In counselling:** ask about accommodation experience at intake and treat it as relevant context.
- **In public:** each paper ships with a one-page brief for process owners.

The research never studies her own caseload or institution. The findings apply anywhere, including where she works.

## Barriers, as tasks

| Barrier | Task |
|---|---|
| Institutions may not commit to act | Start with one champion service; frame the partnership as service improvement with research support; offer termly reports as value up front; Mitacs or a partnership grant to share costs |
| Weekly measurement may burden participants | The short form, three-minute check-ins, flexible windows, payment per response, co-designed with the student advisory group |
| Mood monitoring carries a duty of care | A safety protocol written with counselling services before ethics submission |
| Termly reporting could bias the analysis | Preregister the confirmatory analysis; share aggregate descriptives only |
| Doctoral timelines are long; services want answers now | The termly loop delivers value in year one of data collection |

## Support that multiplies

- **An implementation scientist** on the doctoral committee, beside the counselling psychology supervisor.
- **Champion decision-makers** in two or three access and counselling services, met during the MA.
- **The student advisory group and the co-researcher**, carried from Leeds and paid.
- **Partnership funding** (for example Mitacs, or tri-council partnership streams; **verify** current programmes) so institutions co-invest.
- **The national partner organisation** for reach and for the policy channel.

**Fallback, once:** if partner institutions will not sign an action agreement, the longitudinal study runs through the national partner, and action moves to public briefs, standards submissions and the redesign proposal that a later service partner can adopt.
