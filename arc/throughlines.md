# Throughlines

> **Purpose:** the threads that run through all three stages and make them one arc: the research line, the four properties, art into clinical work, the streams at each phase, and what pays · **Governs:** how stages hand work to each other (figures are as given in `facts/stability-and-figures.md`, which governs them) · **Carried out in Notion:** [Gates](https://app.notion.com/p/d4969021f8da4529a41eafec0c323ca4) · [Tasks](https://app.notion.com/p/1dca49d1884242e28a2f2d21f1216be9)

## 1 · The question and the research line

**The question:** *does the accommodation process itself drive depression in disabled students, and which parts of it are the levers?* The levers are documentation burden, wait times, denials, interim measures and unmet need. They are policies universities already control, they reach every disabled student at once, and no one at UBC or SFU was found working on them directly (`10-stability` §6, checked 6 Oct 2026). That neglect is the opportunity: the supervisor will be someone adjacent who wants the question.

Each piece of research is designed to be the next one's foundation:

| Stage | Output | What it hands on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Ground | Four accessibility resources; **four-literature framework paper** (CJDS); corpus-study design; Make Space documentation under a decided ethics route | The frame the corpus codes trace to; a public record; first DOIs and an ORCID |
| 1 · Frame | **Leeds Applied Project = the empirical pilot of the MA thesis** (survey + interviews, recruited by experience of the process, Leeds ethics); the corpus study finished alongside and credited within; the corpus paper (JPED first choice); RA work in a UBC or SFU lab | A supervised, credited study; the sampling logic and coding skill the thesis needs; a research referee |
| 2 · The Room | **MA thesis**: the accommodation process against depressive symptoms and help-seeking; CGRS M / Mitacs funded | The first empirical answer on the levers; a supervisor who has seen the research |
| 2 · Practice | Clinical observation in the campus service; a second publication | The dissertation's setting and its data access |
| 3 · The Question | **Dissertation**: from levers to an intervention (a joined intake, or accessible low-intensity care including Creative Health) | The evidence base for the centre and the documentation standard |
| 3 · Optionality | Tri-council operating grants, doctoral students, a chair | A research programme that outlives any single seat |

**Literacy runs alongside:** statistics (the first prerequisite) → Leeds methods → MA qualitative and mixed methods → feasibility, single-case and IPA in the PhD. Grant-writing starts with the master's award and grows into operating grants. Two rules hold throughout. The prevalence claim gets a citation before any public use. And nothing client-derived appears on any public surface, at any stage.

## 2 · The properties circuit

Four properties, one job each. A property that tries to be two things becomes two half-things. Each has a minimum state for the hardest phases.

| Property | Job | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **andrearobin.com** | The record panels, editors and supervisors read: CV, publications, the Watershed overview | CV in the postings' vocabulary; resources and papers listed; ORCID; a *current inquiry* section | Thesis, Mitacs report, clinical writing (never client-derived) | Publications, teaching dossier, standards work: the record of a fractional career |
| **Accessible Learning Labs** | The specialism: consulting priced as one, Fieldwork, the Groundwork workbooks, the corpus study's public face | Front door for the question; first workbook ships; consulting priced as a specialism; the OSF project | Becomes the flexible income; one retainer carries the placement months; no new scope | Capped; products earn without hours; the licensed training system |
| **Therapeutic Studio** | The house: the notebook and the question now, the clinical practice on registration | One page saying what will open and when; the notebook; Make Space documented | Opens at RCC scope in Phase 4, sliding scale; Make Space kept visibly separate as Creative Health peer work | Psychologist scope; individual clients plus the Creative Health peer programme, delivered by trained facilitators |
| **Andrea Robin Studio** | The art practice: Dear Ordinary, the catalogue, Studio Day | Standing order (Sunday making, Monday documentation); catalogue never lapses; quarterly showings | Kept Sunday; VATI's creative project is studio work; first serious showing | Shown, funded and taught from; Canada Council and BC Arts Council project grants |

**The circuit:** the writing on andrearobin.com introduces the practice; the practice supplies the clinical observation the writing needs; Labs supplies the method and the income that doesn't need hours; the studio supplies the embodied knowing all three draw on. None is load-bearing alone, and none is allowed to collapse into another.

## 3 · Art practice → art therapy → Creative Health

- **Now:** the BFA as method, Dear Ordinary on its standing order, and Make Space as a weekly peer creative-health programme with its own conceptual tools (Other Bodies). It is documented now, and measured only once an ethics route exists.
- **Stage 2:** the VATI Advanced Diploma after the MA brings the BFA into clinical service. Its placement is requested with a disability population, and its creative project is studio work. RCAT is a second registration, and image-based work becomes part of the clinical offer.
- **Peer support line:** Peer Support Canada certification in Phase 2 makes Make Space a programme run by a certified practitioner; mentor certification in Stage 2 prepares the hand-over to trained facilitators (see `decisions/2026-11-01-peer-support-line.md`).
- **Stage 3:** Creative Health is one of the candidate interventions the dissertation tests for students the thesis found left out. Make Space is manualised and handed to trained facilitators; the training system (Accessible Learning Labs' method applied to Creative Health's programme) becomes the licensed asset.
- **Optionality:** a Creative Health peer programme inside Therapeutic Studio, and possibly a charity partner for Vancouver Foundation participatory research.

The rule that never moves: the peer register and the clinical offer never share a page.

**The income troughs, named in advance.** (1) *The Room*: shallow and long; the practicum year is the deepest point. (2) *Practice*: short, with VATI fees and placement days against a full or reduced salary; RCC fee income starts here. (3) *The Question*: the long one, funded or not entered. Psychologists and counselling therapists are both WorkBC high-opportunity occupations for 2025–2035.

**Four sources carry a trough, built in this order and each started only once the one before is running:**
1. **One product line.** The Groundwork foundation workbook, small enough to ship in weeks. Products take 18–24 months to earn, so they start in Ground or they're too late for the troughs.
2. **One retained relationship.** Named scope, monthly, at specialism rate, secured before Stage 2.
3. **One buffer**, built in Stage 1 while income is at its peak relative to demands, with a floor that isn't spent whatever the opportunity.
4. **Institutional money:** waivers, awards, assistantships, Mitacs and nominations. It's the largest lever, and the reason the first employer is a university.

**Still blank on purpose:** the annual cost of the life wanted, and the size of the Room buffer. Both go to a fee-only planner. The rule that makes the rest work: no phase that reduces income is entered before it has been sized and funded.
