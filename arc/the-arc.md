## The ceiling

A scholar-practitioner in disability-affirming mental health care for people with episodic and fluctuating conditions: a psychologist registered in BC who can hold a clinical hour with a student whose mood is flattening, read that student's programme's essential requirements and say exactly why they don't hold up, and produce the evidence that changes them. She works inside a university, keeps a practice at Therapeutic Studio, and runs a funded research programme on one question: **does the accommodation process itself drive depression in disabled students, and which parts of it are the levers?**

The seats it opens, none of them full-time and several of them built rather than applied for:

- **A joined access-and-mental-health centre** at UBC or SFU, where access and mental health share one intake. Proposed, funded and led.
- **Director-level leadership** in accessibility, equity or counselling services, setting essential-requirements policy for every programme at once.
- **The documentation gate redesigned.** A registered psychologist in BC may diagnose, and UBC accepts learning-disability documentation only from a registered psychologist or certified school psychologist. Diagnostic authority plus the accommodation thesis means an accessible, lower-cost assessment service and a published standard for what documentation should and should not be asked for.
- **A research programme**: tri-council grants, doctoral students, and in time a research chair on disability, accommodation and depression.
- **Standards and regulatory seats**: accessibility technical committees and the College's registrant committees.
- **The lectern**: adjunct teaching in a counselling department and clinical supervision of the next cohort.
- **Therapeutic Studio**, sized by desire: individual work plus the Creative Health peer programme, with art therapy as a second registration.
- **A national and international voice** on how depression care scales to the people it currently misses.

The record behind it: over the past decade she has built a body of applied accessibility and learning-design work that most specialists never put together, and she has designed her way through serious barriers more than once. The plan treats that record as the baseline.

**Floor, named once:** if a later stage never happens, an RCC plus a university role (the advisor post, or a counsellor post in a campus service) is a complete, secure, pensioned career that still delivers most of this life.

## How each stage compounds into the next

- **Stage 1 → Stage 2.** The advisor role is a funding instrument as much as a job: a staff tuition waiver covers the undergraduate prerequisites at either university, SFU's covers its own graduate programmes, and UBC's covers part of a master's. The role also logs helping experience from day one and puts her among the people who later supervise practicum and sit on hiring panels. The Leeds applied project *is* the corpus study, which turns a practitioner's application into a practitioner-researcher's. Crisis-line hours give the counselling file its supervised one-to-one evidence and its professional referee.
- **Stage 2 → Stage 3.** UBC's PhD admits only from a thesis-based master's, so the MA thesis is what keeps the doctorate open. The practicum seat inside a campus counselling service is where "the training pipeline is the hiring pipeline" becomes literal: the counsellor post that follows is the clinical setting where the dissertation's question lives. RCC means practice income keeps running through the doctorate.
- **Stage 3 → the ceiling.** The doctorate moves from the thesis's levers to an intervention: a joined intake, or accessible low-intensity provision with Creative Health among the candidates. That is the evidence base for the centre, and registration turns it into authority over the documentation gate.

## Operating principles

1. **Phases close on conditions, not dates.** Each review asks one answerable question: *is this phase finished, and what does the next one open?* A phase that takes longer than its target year isn't late. Its conditions just haven't been met yet.
2. **Ceiling first, at every review.** Name what a phase makes possible before what it costs. Every prevention question is paired with a promotion question. Fallbacks are named once.
3. **Carried and set down.** Each phase says what is carried (at what volume) and what is deliberately set down. The floors are never traded: one Make Space session a week, one substantial piece of writing a month, one kept studio Sunday and a catalogue entry for every sheet, and the hours log updated within 24 hours of any service.
4. **No date next to it = not being prepared for.** Anything wanted gets a target date in Notion, or it isn't in the plan yet. Anything with a fixed external date outranks anything without one.
5. **Prepare one phase ahead in detail; keep the ceiling in view always.** The people who later sponsor the centre are met now.
6. **No phase that reduces income is entered before it has been sized and funded.** For the doctorate, funding is a condition of entry with the same standing as the supervisor.
7. **Every phase produces something citable and something visible, and names who helps.** The nominators file is kept current and sent the record twice a year.
8. **Keep a decision log.** One dated line per decision: what was chosen, what was set aside, and why.
