## The ceiling

The MA thesis does two things at once. It **builds and validates the instrument** the field is missing: a measure of how burdensome a university accommodation process is, in its design features rather than in the student. And it **uses that instrument to test the mechanism**: whether process burden shapes depressive symptoms and help-seeking through psycho-emotional disablism. The thesis is written as two publishable manuscripts. A UBC counselling psychology committee reads it as a scientist-practitioner's first programme of research, with a validated tool, a tested model and clear implications for counselling practice. The PhD then uses the tool to change something.

**The principle that makes it extend:** *the MA builds the tool and finds the levers; the PhD uses the tool to test what happens when the levers are pulled.*

## Why this design extends into the PhD

| What the PhD will need | What the MA gives it |
|---|---|
| A way to measure the process as a barrier, before and after a redesign | A validated process-burden instrument with known reliability and structure |
| A mechanism worth intervening on | A tested model linking process features to depressive symptoms and help-seeking |
| Evidence the question belongs to counselling psychology | Outcomes counsellors work with, a psychological mechanism, and implications for campus counselling services |
| A research record that reads as one programme | Leeds pilot → MA manuscripts → dissertation, on one question and one instrument |
| A partner and a population | The partner organisation and recruitment channels from Leeds, carried forward |

## What makes it counselling psychology, not only disability studies

The question keeps its social-model anchor. The thesis specialises it in four ways a counselling psychology committee will recognise:

1. **Outcomes counsellors work with:** depressive symptoms, attitudes toward seeking help, self-stigma of help-seeking, and actual use of campus counselling.
2. **A psychological mechanism:** psycho-emotional disablism, read as what a barrier does to how a person sees themselves and what they expect from institutions. It is the bridge between a social-model cause and a psychological outcome.
3. **Measurement rigour:** instrument development and validation is core scientist-practitioner work, and the area where UBC's counselling and measurement faculty are strongest (`people/faculty-map.md`).
4. **Practice implications:** what intake, triage and collaboration between access services and counselling services should look like if process burden is a risk factor.

The outcome measures stay framed as **costs of the barrier, not properties of the student** (`the-thesis-line.md`, condition 2). Recruitment is by experience of the process, never by diagnosis.

## The design

### Study 1 · Instrument development and validation (Manuscript 1)

- **Starting point:** the process-feature items drafted in the Leeds runway and refined with pilot data (item behaviour, completion, participant feedback).
- **Domains** (from the corpus codes and the policy reading): documentation demanded; waits; re-proving; denials and partial approvals; interim measures; fit with fluctuation; clarity and respect in communication.
- **Steps:** expert and co-researcher review → cognitive interviews with disabled students → field test → split-sample exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis → reliability → convergent and discriminant validity → measurement invariance across process experience (completed, abandoned, decided against) and institution type, not across diagnoses.
- **Sample:** recruited nationally through the partner organisation and students' associations at several institutions, not at her own employer. Target at least 300 complete responses for split-sample analysis; confirm with a power analysis.

### Study 2 · Testing the mechanism (Manuscript 2)

- **Model:** process burden → psycho-emotional disablism → depressive symptoms and help-seeking (attitudes, self-stigma, intentions, service use), with fit-with-fluctuation tested as a moderator.
- **Analysis:** structural equation modelling on the same sample, preregistered.
- **Qualitative strand (optional, small):** follow-up interviews with a purposive subset, if the supervisor wants mixed methods. The Leeds pilot already carries the main qualitative work, so this can stay light.
- **Candidate measures** (confirm in the measures shortlist, with licences and accessible formats): PHQ-9 (depressive symptoms), WEMWBS (wellbeing), General Help-Seeking Questionnaire, Self-Stigma of Seeking Help, and the short form of Attitudes Toward Seeking Professional Psychological Help.

### Built to be acted on, and followed over time

Four additions in the MA make the doctoral "over time" route and its action layer possible (`over-time-into-action.md`):

- **Consent to recontact**, so the MA sample becomes wave one of a longitudinal cohort.
- **A short form** of the instrument (about eight items), validated alongside the full form, for weekly use.
- **Process events coded as dated steps** (submitted, waiting, re-proving requested, approved, denied), not only overall burden.
- **The lever map** kept current: each feature, who owns it, how it could change. Access and counselling services at two or three institutions invited onto the advisory group.

### Thesis format

Two manuscripts framed by an introduction and a general discussion, if UBC allows a manuscript-based master's thesis (**verify**). Otherwise a conventional thesis with the two studies as chapters, written so each chapter converts directly into a paper.

### Ethics and the dual role

UBC Behavioural Research Ethics Board approval. Recruitment through the partner and other institutions keeps her advisor role and caseload out of the sample entirely (Notion gate *Thesis data and dual role settled*). Consent and the data management plan allow anonymised data to be reused in the doctoral studies.

## How it extends into the PhD

The dissertation picks one of three routes, chosen with the doctoral supervisor. All three need the MA's instrument, which is why it comes first.

| Route | Dissertation | What the MA supplies |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Redesign and test** (the current plan) | Co-design a joined access-and-mental-health intake, then a feasibility study and a hybrid effectiveness-implementation trial | The primary process outcome (the instrument) and the levers to redesign |
| **B · Over time** | A longitudinal or intensive longitudinal study of how burden and fluctuation interact across a term, and whether burden predicts later depressive symptoms, help-seeking or withdrawal | The instrument and a cross-sectional model to test causally |
| **C · Beyond one country** | Cross-cultural adaptation and invariance testing of the instrument, leading into the global mental health postdoc | A validated original version and a partner network |

A strong dissertation can combine B and A: a longitudinal study first, then the redesign trial. Route B is her leading interest (4 Nov 2026), designed so findings are acted on during the study, every term, after it and beyond one institution: see [`over-time-into-action.md`](over-time-into-action.md). The specialty comprehensive exam's review manuscript (Notion gate *Comprehensive exam*) can update the scoping review into a systematic review of process-level interventions.

## The runway

- **Item analysis on the pilot data** (permitted by the Leeds consent for future use): instrument v1.
- **Pilot paper** submitted with the co-researcher.
- **RA or volunteer role in a UBC lab** near measurement or depression. Learn the lab's methods and software before the MA starts.
- **One-page fit note** shaping the question to one lab's programme; supervisor conversation (existing gate).
- **Autumn 2030:** the MA thesis proposal written once, serving the UBC and SFU applications, the CGRS M proposal and the thesis (existing gate).

### Before the MA starts · Jan–Aug 2031

- **Partner agreement extended** to national recruitment for the thesis; explore a Mitacs Accelerate internship with the partner.
- **Preregistration and analysis plan drafted**; power analysis done.
- **Ethics application drafted** to the UBC form.
- **Advanced statistics and measurement self-study** (factor analysis, SEM, invariance) so the MA electives deepen rather than introduce.

| When | Ceiling | Plan target |
|---|---|---|

The early proposal is the main acceleration: data collected before the practicum year means the busiest clinical year carries writing, not fieldwork.

## Barriers, as tasks

| Barrier | Task |
|---|---|
| The supervisor may want a different design | Bring the instrument and the model as a starting design; ask what would make it fit their lab. The instrument is useful to most designs, which keeps it portable |
| A practicum-heavy MA leaves little research time | Proposal and ethics in Year 1; data before the practicum year; the part-time option exists if needed |
| A validation sample of 300+ is ambitious for a master's | National recruitment through the partner from day one; honoraria funded through Mitacs or CGRS M; a co-researcher on outreach |
| Manuscript-based theses may not be allowed | Verify; if not, write chapters in paper shape |
| UBC prerequisites for the PhD include courses the MA may not cover | Map MA electives to the PhD's graduate prerequisites in Year 1 (`facts/requirements-by-gate.md`) |

## Support that multiplies

- **A supervisor with measurement strength**, and a committee member from disability studies or campus mental health.
- **UBC's measurement and research methodology faculty** as a methods reader or committee member (whether they sit on CNPS committees is **verify**).
- **The partner organisation and co-researcher** from Leeds, now co-authors on the instrument paper.
- **CGRS M, BC Graduate Scholarship and Mitacs** to fund honoraria and time.
- **The lab group** as a writing and analysis community.

**Fallback, once:** if the validation sample falls short, the thesis tests the model with the pilot-refined instrument, and full validation becomes the first study of the dissertation.
