# The Applied Project runway

> **Purpose:** how the Leeds Applied Project is set up, and as much of it completed as the rules allow, before the project module opens. · **Governs:** the research tasks from Nov 2026 until the project's first week. The project itself (question, design, conditions) is governed by `the-thesis-line.md`. · **Carried out in Notion:** [Tasks](https://app.notion.com/p/1dca49d1884242e28a2f2d21f1216be9) (Area: Research; gate links below), [Gates](https://app.notion.com/p/d4969021f8da4529a41eafec0c323ca4) (view: *Thesis line*), [Meeting Log](https://app.notion.com/p/40bd55c9671b8129a750d199bcd79454)

## The ceiling

The project module opens and the proposal, literature review, ethics draft, timeline and partner agreement are already written. The module's six guided weeks become a polish and a submission, not a start. Ethics goes in during the first weeks, recruitment opens as soon as it is approved, and the report is written into a skeleton that already exists. The report is submitted on time, presented to a partner that helped shape it, and turned into a co-authored pilot paper within months. That paper, the evidence map and the corpus study arrive at the 1 Dec 2030 application as finished work.

**The stretch:** if Leeds allows the nine taught modules to be completed by August 2028, the project can run in the September–February period, with the report done by early 2029. That gives a full extra year to publish the pilot before the MA application.

## What the module asks for (2025/26 catalogue)

- **OSSP5300M Disability Studies Applied Project**, 45 credits, about 450 study hours.
- Runs in one of two six-month periods: **1 March–31 August** or **1 September–28 February**.
- **Prerequisite:** all nine taught modules.
- **First six weeks:** guided workshops on the proposal, literature or evidence review, the ethics submission, a timeline and deliverables, and dissemination. Then independent work with an allocated supervisor and a small peer cohort.
- **Partnering:** projects may be partnered with a disability rights organisation, service provider, or policy or practice organisation; the module is built around change and usable findings.
- **Assessment:** a report of 5,000–6,000 words (or a 30–40 minute presentation), **70%**; a presentation to the partner organisation or a specified audience (verbal, vlog or accessible briefing), **30%**.
- The catalogue notes that the proposal may have been developed in *Doing Disability Research* or *Becoming Disability Change Makers*.

Source: catalogue.leeds.ac.uk/Module/TP/OSSP/5300M/202526 (read 4 Nov 2026). Recheck against the 2027/28 and 2028/29 catalogues.

## The boundary: what can be done now, and what waits

| Can be done before the module opens | Waits for the module and Leeds ethics approval |
|---|---|
| The evidence map (scoping review): protocol, searching, screening, synthesis, preprint | Recruiting participants |
| The project brief, the full protocol and the analysis plan | Any survey or interview data collection |
| The process-feature instrument drafted from the corpus coding frame and the policy reading | Testing the instrument with students (cognitive interviews count as research) |
| Validated measures chosen, licences checked | |
| Partner organisation agreed in principle; a letter of interest | The formal partner agreement, if Leeds requires its own form |
| The advisory group formed for design input (consultation on priorities and wording, not data) | |
| Funding found for the co-researcher and participant honoraria | |
| Data management plan and ethics application drafted, ready to adapt to the Leeds form | Ethics submission and approval |
| Report and presentation skeletons | Findings, analysis, the final report |

**Two rules hold the boundary:**

1. **No participant data before Leeds ethics approval.** Anything gathered earlier could not be used in the report, and could compromise the project.
2. **Declare prior work.** Independent work (the evidence map, the instrument) is cited as your own prior work. Previously published or assessed text is not resubmitted. Confirm how Leeds wants prior independent work declared before relying on it (verify item 45).

## The runway, quarter by quarter

### Now → Mar 2027 · Brief and permission

- **Write the one-page project brief:** the question, the population (recruited by experience of the process, not diagnosis), the partner type, the mixed-methods design, the outputs. It also anchors the Leeds personal statement.
- **Ask Leeds, in writing** (admissions now; the programme team once admitted), and log each answer with a written summary sent back:
  1. Does the Applied Project support primary data collection with Canadian participants, and how long does ethics review take? (existing gate)
  2. How should independent prior work (a published scoping review, a draft instrument) be declared and built on?
  3. Can the nine taught modules be completed by August 2028, so the project runs September 2028–February 2029?
  4. Does a Canadian partner's involvement require a second ethics review in Canada?
  5. Can the consent form allow the anonymised pilot data to inform the MA thesis? (existing gate)
- **Register the evidence map protocol on OSF** (PRISMA-ScR; JBI guidance). See the open choice below on its scope.
- **Read the Leeds Centre for Disability Studies' recent work,** including the module manager's, so the first supervisor conversation starts from their field.

### Apr → Aug 2027 · Partner and instrument

- **Approach two or three possible partners** (for example a national disabled students' organisation such as NEADS, or a disabled people's organisation in BC). Ask what they would want to learn from the project and how the findings could serve them. Aim for a letter of interest by August 2027.
- **Draft the process-feature instrument (v0):** items for documentation demanded, waits, re-proving, denials, interim measures and fit with fluctuation, traced to corpus codes and to the UBC LR7 and SFU GP 26 process steps.
- **Shortlist validated measures** for wellbeing, depressive symptoms and help-seeking. Check each one's licence, cost, Canadian use and accessibility in alternative formats.
- **Draft the data management plan** (`structures/DATA_MANAGEMENT_PLAN.md`).
- **Find funding** for a paid co-researcher and participant honoraria; add each source to Opportunities.
- **Attend CDSA at Congress 2027** (SFU, July). Introduce the question to two people.

### Sept 2027 → Aug 2028 · Leeds Year 1: each essay builds a section

Follow the module map in `the-thesis-line.md`. Specifically for the project:

- **Doing Disability Research:** the essay is the pilot protocol. Write it so it converts directly into the module's proposal and ethics submission.
- **Realising Disability Equality Through Policy:** the policy reading becomes the instrument's feature list.
- **Form the advisory group** with the partner, on paid terms, and agree the co-researcher role and authorship in writing.
- **Evidence map:** screening and synthesis in module breaks; preprint by mid-2028.
- **Ethics application:** a full draft by August 2028, adapted to the Leeds form once it is visible.

### Sept 2028 → project start · Ready to run

- **Becoming Disability Change Makers:** the essay is the knowledge-translation and partner-presentation plan, which is the 30% component.
- **Build the report skeleton** (5,000–6,000 words), structured so it converts into a pilot paper.
- **Agree recruitment channels** with the partner and confirm timing against the project period.
- **Week one of the module:** proposal and ethics application submitted early in the six guided weeks; recruitment ready to open on approval.

## Completion before the module opens

| Component | Done before the module | Done in the module |
|---|---|---|
| Literature or evidence review | ~90% (evidence map preprinted) | Updated search; framed for the report |
| Proposal and protocol | ~90% | Supervisor feedback |
| Ethics | Drafted | Submitted and approved |
| Instrument and measures | Drafted and selected | Tested with participants |
| Partner and advisory group | Agreed and working | Presentation delivered |
| Data | — | Collected and analysed |
| Report and presentation | Skeletons | Written and delivered |

Roughly half of the project's 450 hours can be invested before the module opens, which is what makes an on-time report and a fast pilot paper possible.

## Barriers, as tasks

| Barrier | Task |
|---|---|
| Leeds may not support Canadian participants, or ethics may take months | Ask now (question 1); if slow, plan the September–February period so approval lands before recruitment |
| The nine modules may not compress into one year | Ask (question 3); if not, the March–August period still leaves 15 months to the application |
| A partner may not commit early | Approach two or three in parallel; the advisory group can be built through the co-researcher's networks |
| Clinical symptom measures may be read as individualising by disability studies examiners | Frame them as measuring what the barrier costs (`the-thesis-line.md`, condition 2); give the qualitative strand equal weight |
| Paying a co-researcher needs money | Funding search from spring 2027; the partner may co-apply |

## Support that multiplies

- **The partner organisation:** shapes the questions, opens recruitment, receives the presentation, and stays as a standing advisory group into the MA.
- **A paid disabled co-researcher:** better questions, credibility with participants, and a co-author.
- **The Leeds supervisor:** becomes a research referee for the 2030 application.
- **The Leeds peer cohort:** a ready-made writing group.
- **A methods reader** in measurement (see `people/faculty-map.md`): someone who reads the instrument before it is used.

**Fallback, once:** if the partner or recruitment is thin, the report covers the instrument's development and a smaller interview set. That is still a tested design for the MA.

## Open choice: the scope of the scoping review

`structures/SYSTEMATIC_REVIEW.md` names the scoping review as *who does campus stepped care for depression leave out*. The Applied Project needs a map of **accommodation process features and disabled students' wellbeing and help-seeking**. Either re-scope the scoping review to the thesis question (one review that serves the project, the MA and the profile), or keep it and run a smaller evidence map for the project. Decide before registering the protocol; record the decision in `decisions/`.
