# Three Assets, One Engine

*A trajectory map for the three public faces of the work — what each becomes, and how it gets there, in service of the whole-portfolio mission: scalable, accessible care for depression, carried by someone who can afford to keep doing it. Keyed to the phases of the sequence rather than to years, so the map holds at whatever pace the path actually moves.*

**v2 — revised.** Two changes from the first version. Accessible Learning Labs is no longer described as the stream that funds the others; it is one of two load-bearing columns, with its own scarcity, its own products, and its own route to an academic appointment. And a sixth phase has been added at the far end, because the destination is not the deployment of the engine but the **optionality** the engine buys. See `the-strategy-layer.md` for the financial architecture underneath both changes.

The three assets are not three businesses. They are three organs of one body. **Therapeutic Studio** produces the thing worth scaling. **Accessible Learning Labs** is the discipline that scales it without letting it degrade — and the engine that makes the whole thing financially survivable. **andrearobin.com** is the trust layer that makes both legible to the people who can fund, partner, hire, and amplify them. Read each one down its own column, then read across the phases to see how they move together.

The phases below are the five that structure the sequence, plus the far side:

- **Ground** — now: the health-sector or accessibility role, the CPACC, outcome measurement, the first publications
- **Frame** — Leeds and the LSHTM short courses, run together; the second paper
- **The Room** — the counselling master's, supervised hours, registration, a caseload
- **The Question** — a doctorate, if a question requires one, a supervisor wants it, and it is funded
- **Deployment** — the three assets running as one engine
- **Optionality** — the far side: work chosen rather than accepted, across the room, the lectern, and the studio

## 1. Therapeutic Studio

*Houses Make Space and Other Bodies.*

**What it is today:** a living, open studio — creative-health practice shared in public, honestly framed as a record of questions in a young field where the evidence is still being gathered.

**What it becomes:** an evidence-based, manualized, transferable depression intervention with its own training system and a published evidence base — intervention IP that can travel beyond the room it was born in.

**How it gets there:**

*Ground.* The single highest-leverage move is the smallest: add a validated depression-screening measure at the start and end of each Make Space cycle. The day you have even simple pre/post data, Make Space stops being a beautiful practice and becomes a program with a claim you can defend — and that one dataset becomes the spine of the tools paper, the systematic review's real-world anchor, the grant application, and the social-prescribing pitch. In parallel, begin a structured reflective journal of every session and a light participant-feedback loop. The Substack continues as the public record.

*Frame and The Room.* Therapeutic Studio becomes your empirical *site* — the place your research draws its data from. Other Bodies graduates from a 52-card deck into a documented, citable set of clinical instruments (the emotional compass, the topography, the emotion stories), described well enough that someone else could use them faithfully. The systematic review and the first qualitative study of participant experience are published. The Substack matures from reflective journal into a public lab notebook the field begins to cite. Once provisional registration allows, expressive approaches integrate into a real clinical caseload, so the practice is now generating both research data and clinical knowledge.

*The Question.* This is where the mission lives or dies. Make Space is manualized into a facilitator-training curriculum, then a train-the-trainer architecture, then culturally adapted and evaluated in low-resource settings through the LSHTM partnerships — with local leads holding first authorship. Other Bodies is open-licensed so it can travel freely. A feasibility trial and the first implementation studies are published.

*Deployment.* Therapeutic Studio is intervention IP + an evidence base + a self-propagating training system. It very likely outgrows being "your studio" and becomes a small program or nonprofit run partly by facilitator-trainers who don't need you in the room. That independence is the proof the multiplier multiplied.

*Optionality.* The program runs without you. Your relationship to it becomes governance, mentorship, and the occasional room you choose to be in because you want to be — not because it collapses otherwise.

**Its role in the mission:** Therapeutic Studio answers the question *what travels.* It is the source of the thing that is worth scaling at all.

## 2. Accessible Learning Labs

*Houses Fieldwork. The second column, not the scaffolding.*

**What it is today:** considerably more than the first version of this document credited. `landscape.html` records roughly a hundred mapped contributions across a shared trunk and five branches — learning experience design, universal design for learning, knowledge mobilization, learning systems design, and accessibility — each cited and grounded in named theory. That is not a consulting side practice. It is a body of expertise deep enough to teach from, publish from, price at a premium, and build products out of.

**What it becomes:** the methodology-and-dissemination engine of the whole portfolio — the place where your rarest skill, designing fidelity-preserving training systems for accessible care, is named, packaged, published, licensed, and turned into field-shaping influence. It is also the asset with the shortest path to money and the strongest independent claim on an academic appointment.

**How it gets there:**

*Ground.* Three moves, and the first has been brought forward from Phase two. **The CPACC now** — it is the only credential in the entire plan that raises earning power before the next role is signed, and the study design is already written. **Publish the four written accessibility resources**, which currently sit finished and doing nothing; the essential-requirements piece needs its scarcity claim revised first. **Reprice the offer as a specialism, not a service** — the market rate for a learning designer is not the rate for someone fluent in disability studies, accommodation law, essential requirements, procurement, clinical placement access, and health-sector knowledge translation at once. The scarcity is the intersection, and it should be named narrowly enough to be memorable. Alongside all three, steer the consulting toward health and mental-health-practitioner training rather than generic course design, so the work you are paid to produce is already the raw material for implementation-science papers later.

*Frame and The Room.* ALL becomes the place where your actual superpower gets named and methodized. The consulting case studies start to read as applied implementation science. Fieldwork narrows from broad accessibility toward the specific, underserved intersection that is yours: disability, depression, and access. The conceptual paper — that designing the training system which delivers an intervention is as important as the intervention itself — is the intellectual seed ALL exists to grow. Through The Room, ALL is also the stream that keeps earning while clinical training suppresses everything else, which is precisely why it must by then be built into something that does not require your full attention to survive.

*The Question.* ALL is now the bridge that carries learning-systems design into global mental health. Its methodology — how you take something that worked in one room and make it deliverable, faithfully, by a non-specialist, at scale, accessibly — becomes a documented, publishable method, applied directly to the Make Space training system. Fieldwork matures into keynotes; the inquiry begins to take book shape. The teaching-stream evidence accumulates here too: invited lectures, curriculum work, documented educational leadership.

*Deployment.* ALL is the dissemination engine, the public methodology platform, and the second income engine: publications, a premium consulting offer rooted in genuine rarity, licensed training systems and courses that earn without you in the room, keynotes, and the long-form writing that moves your contribution from the field's periphery toward its centre.

*Optionality.* The consulting is chosen rather than needed. The licensed methodology and the published work carry income that is not exchanged for hours — which is the mechanism, more than any other in this document, by which a small caseload and a protected studio practice become financially possible.

**Its role in the mission:** Accessible Learning Labs answers the question *how it travels, and who can be trained to carry it.* It is the discipline that keeps scaling from becoming dilution — and the reason the person doing all of this can afford to keep doing it.

## 3. andrearobin.com

*Currently the professional home where organizations and hiring managers find you and see, through your work, that you are a credible, trusted advisor.*

**What it is today:** a learning-design portfolio that demonstrates credibility through anonymized case studies and frameworks — built to convert a hiring manager's attention into trust.

**What it becomes:** the authoritative personal hub of a clinician-researcher-practitioner — the canonical front door and trust layer that routes every kind of opportunity (organizations, collaborators, funders, students, journalists, conference organizers) to the right part of the work, and carries the authority to make that routing land.

**How it gets there:**

*Ground.* It stays the professional anchor and sharpens its positioning toward health and mental-health systems specifically. The CPACC goes on it the day it is granted, alongside the four published accessibility resources — because in this phase the site's job is conversion into a role and a rate. The "trusted advisor" frame deepens: this is where a health authority or research institution confirms you are credible before they hire or partner.

*Frame and The Room.* As research literacy, the disability studies credential, and the first publications arrive, the site's identity shifts from "learning design specialist" to "learning-design specialist building genuine standing at the intersection of knowledge translation and mental health." It becomes the **bridge identity** — the place that shows you are not a learning designer making a hopeful pivot, but someone deliberately constructing a rare profile, with publications and credentials to prove the trajectory is real. Case studies begin to include mental-health-adjacent work.

*The Question.* Post-MA and into any doctorate and licensure, andrearobin.com becomes the unifying hub. It carries the authority markers — the degrees, registration standing, the publication list, the speaking and teaching history — and it links out cleanly to the clinical practice, Therapeutic Studio, Accessible Learning Labs, and the writing. Its canonical "about" is the story of the whole combination, and it begins doing conversion work that has nothing to do with employment: grant invitations, research partnerships, student inquiries, referrals, and speaking requests.

*Deployment.* andrearobin.com is the trust-and-routing layer for a field-shaping figure. Its job has scaled from *"hire me for learning design"* to *"this is who I am across all of it, here is the proof, and here is the door to each part."* A health system finds the consulting; a funder finds the research; a journalist finds the public writing; a prospective student finds the teaching; a person seeking care finds the practice. One front door, many credible destinations.

*Optionality.* The site stops being a pitch and becomes a filter. Its function is no longer to attract opportunity but to sort it — which is what a front door is for, once more is arriving through it than you intend to accept.

**Its role in the mission:** andrearobin.com is the *credibility and routing layer.* Therapeutic Studio and Accessible Learning Labs do the work; andrearobin.com is how the right people come to trust it and find their way in.

## How the three move together

Picture them as a single system across the whole path:

| | Therapeutic Studio | Accessible Learning Labs | andrearobin.com |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Function** | What travels | How it travels, and what pays for it | Why people trust it |
| **Ground** | Add outcome measurement; reframe as a program | CPACC now; publish the four resources; reprice as a specialism | Sharpen toward health systems; add the credential |
| **Frame · The Room** | Empirical site; tools published | Methodology named; Fieldwork narrows; income held through the trough | Bridge identity; publications appear |
| **The Question** | Manualized, adapted, open-licensed | Method published; applied to the curriculum; teaching-stream evidence gathered | Unifying hub; authority markers |
| **Deployment** | Self-propagating intervention IP | Dissemination engine; licensed methodology; the book | Field-shaping front door |
| **Optionality** | Runs without you | Earns without you in the room | Filters rather than attracts |

The moment to watch for — the one that proves the whole thesis — is when the **Make Space facilitator-training curriculum (Therapeutic Studio's content) is built using Accessible Learning Labs' learning-systems methodology, evaluated as a task-shifting model, and presented to the world through andrearobin.com's credibility.** That single artifact — a creative-health intervention, made transferable by a rigorous training system, designed accessibly from the start, tested in a low-resource setting, carried by a trusted public identity — *is* the thesis of the entire career. None of the three can produce it alone.

## Three design choices to make on purpose

**Keep the two Substacks distinct early, converge late.** Fieldwork (practitioners and systems) and the Therapeutic Studio journal (creative health and participants) serve different readers. Let them grow separately and cross-pollinate, then unify toward a single public voice when the keynotes and book arrive — rather than merging prematurely and blurring both.

**Open the intervention; monetize the methodology.** Keeping Other Bodies and the practice sessions free is mission-aligned and builds reach. The *training system* is the thing that can carry premium value and fund the rest. Give away what should travel; charge for the rare skill that makes it travel well.

**Build at least one thing that earns without your presence.** New in v2, and the design choice that determines whether the far side is optionality or obligation. A licensed curriculum, a course, a training system others run under agreement. Everything else in this portfolio trades hours for money; the small caseload and the protected studio practice are only affordable if something in the engine does not.

## What to hold

Therapeutic Studio is the *what.* Accessible Learning Labs is the *how* — and the *how it gets paid for.* andrearobin.com is the *why-you-can-be-trusted.* You are not running three projects competing for your attention — you are building one engine with three visible faces, and every hour spent on any of them, if pointed correctly, compounds the other two.

The correction v2 makes is worth stating once more, plainly: the fifteen years of learning design and accessibility work are not the runway. They are half the aircraft.
