# Fact-Checking & Accuracy Instructions — Knowledge Resources

**Verification standards for the andrearobin.com knowledge areas and Accessible Learning Labs work pages**

Status: standing reference · applies to every resource before publication
Adapted from: `Fieldwork_Factchecking_Instructions.docx` (compiled from the Weeks 1–3 fact-checking review, June 2026)
Companion to: `accessibility-area-knowledge-resources.md`, `landscape.html`, `area-map.html`

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

The Fieldwork instructions were written for a weekly post series. This document carries the same standards into a different artifact: a portfolio of knowledge resources that present themselves as syntheses of a literature.

**What this applies to:**

- Every knowledge resource page (`*.html`) and its linear companion (`*-linear.html`)
- The landscape and area maps — including reference lists, source attributions, and cross-links
- Accessible Learning Labs work and case study pages
- Planning documents that will later become page copy

**Why the standard is higher here, not lower.** A post is read as one person's argument on one day. A knowledge resource is read as a claim to domain authority — it says *this is what the field knows*. Its Sources section is not a courtesy; it is the artifact's entire warrant. And because sources recur across twenty-plus files plus their linear duplicates, a single wrong edition or misattributed framework does not appear once. It propagates, and it is found by exactly the audience most able to spot it: advisors, educational developers, and researchers who know this literature.

**The core test is unchanged.** Can an independent reader verify this claim without contacting the author? If not, the claim needs a stronger source or a weaker formulation.

## 2. The risk profile of this portfolio

Ranked by likelihood of error and cost when wrong.

| Rank | Risk area | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Specific figures inside qualitative prose** | The house voice is qualitative and careful. Isolated numbers stand out precisely because they are rare — and they are almost always the AI-drafted sentence. |
| 2 | **Framework attribution** | The portfolio names originators constantly (Sweller, Mayer, Gilbert, Brinkerhoff, Rossett, Schön, Mezirow, Kolb, Wenger, Knowles, Hiatt, Kotter, Bridges, Treviranus, Mace, Rose & Meyer). Wrong attribution is the error this audience notices fastest. |
| 3 | **Editions, years, publishers** | Long citation lists, many multi-edition texts. Errors here are quiet, cumulative, and cross-file. |
| 4 | **Law, standards, version numbers** | ACA, AODA, *Accessible BC Act*, ADA, Section 504/508, WCAG versions, UDL Guidelines version, IAAP BoK versions. Dated content that is read as current. |
| 5 | **Scope and posture claims** | Not a factual error but an accuracy failure with professional consequences. See §7. |
| 6 | **Cross-file drift** | Interactive and linear versions, duplicate files, area map ↔ landscape reference numbering. |
| 7 | **Practice case studies** | Lower volume, but the only place where the author is the sole source. See §6. |

## 3. Statistics and specific figures

*The highest-risk area in this portfolio — a reversal from the Fieldwork series, where case studies ranked first.*

- Every specific figure — percentages, ratios, learner counts, durations, years — must be traceable to a named, accessible source with a page number, section, or URL.

- **In a qualitative resource, a number is a red flag before it is a fact.** These pages make their arguments in prose. When a percentage appears, ask first whether it is doing work the surrounding sentence could do without it. Usually it is not, and the safe edit is to cut it.

- "Roughly," "approximately," and "up to" do not remove the verification requirement. "Up to 30%" is a stronger claim than "measurably," not a weaker one — it asserts a measured ceiling.

- **Never attach a figure to a named researcher unless that figure appears in their work.** The pattern to watch for: a real scholar, a real finding, and a fabricated number welded to both. This is the single most damaging error available in this portfolio, because it is checkable in one search by anyone who knows the scholar.

- Ratio-style frameworks (70:20:10 and similar) are **models, not findings**. Describe them as heuristics with a named origin and note where their empirical support is contested. Do not present them as measured distributions of how adults learn.

- Figures describing organisational or sector scale — learners served, audit pass rates, adoption rates — change annually. Cite the current edition of the publishing organisation's own report, with the year in the citation, and diarise a re-check.

## 4. Citations

### Format

The house format across the portfolio is: **Author. *Title.* Edition where relevant. Publisher, Year.** Journal articles add journal name, volume, issue, and page range. Online sources add a resolving URL.

- Do not omit the publisher.
- Specify the edition whenever it is not the first, and make the edition and the year agree. Wiggins & McTighe, *Understanding by Design* — first edition 1998, 2nd ed. 2005. Citing 2005 without "2nd ed." is misleading.
- Where a source recurs across resources, the edition, year, and format must match in every file. Copy the verified entry; do not retype it.
- Standards and guidelines are cited with version, issuing body, and publication date — never as a bare name.

### Verification

- Verify author name, title, publisher, year, and edition against the source itself or a library database. Never against memory, and never against AI-generated text.
- Check titles character by character. The recurring failure mode is a plausible-but-wrong title: *Participatory Action Research and the Politics of Knowledge* for a book actually titled *Participatory Action Learning and Action Research*.
- Never cite a source you have not read. If it appears in another work's reference list, find and read the original first.
- Confirm every URL resolves to the correct page — in both the interactive and linear versions.

### Citation entries that are not citations

Some entries in the current files describe a body of scholarship without naming a work — for example, the "Decolonizing evaluation frameworks" entry in `needs-analysis-organizational-transformation.html`. This is honest but it is not a citation, and it sits inside a list of citations.

Either name a specific work, or move the paragraph out of the citation list and into the body text where it reads as context rather than as a source.

## 5. Framework and concept attribution

- Verify the originator of every framework, term, and methodology before publishing. Locate the specific publication where it was articulated — do not attribute from general knowledge.

- Attribution requires three things: the right person or organisation, the right work, and the right year. Getting the person right and the work wrong is still an error.

- Where a concept is genuinely collective or contested in origin, say so rather than assigning it to the most famous name associated with it.

- Where a framework has been substantially revised, cite the version being described. The UDL Guidelines are the live example: v3.0 (CAST, 2024) differs from v2.2 in structure and language, and a resource describing v3.0 while citing v2.2 is wrong in both directions.

- Cite the professional literature of the field being described alongside the design literature — CACUSS, CADSPPE, NEADS for accommodation practice; IAAP bodies of knowledge for accessibility practice — wherever a claim concerns what that practice is actually like.

## 6. Practice case studies

The case studies in this portfolio are **first-person accounts of the author's own work**, not documented external cases. That changes the verification question from *can a reader find the source* to *would the account survive contact with the people who were there*.

- Label practice case studies as practice-based. A reader must never be able to mistake an account of one's own facilitation work for a documented institutional case study with an external evidence base.

- **Do not attach outcome statistics to your own work unless the measurement actually happened and the instrument can be described.** Qualitative accounts of what shifted are credible and appropriate. Invented percentages are not, and they are the fastest way to lose a reader who does this work.

- Name partner institutions, funders, and community organisations only with permission. Where permission is not held, describe the setting by type rather than by name. This is a confidentiality requirement, not a stylistic preference.

- Describe participants in terms they would recognise and accept. Accounts involving disabled people, patients, families, and community members carry an obligation that goes beyond accuracy.

- If a case is a composite of several engagements, say so in the case itself.

- Where a case study makes a claim about an institution — its compliance status, its programmes, its internal processes — that claim needs a source or must be softened. Prefer "operating under" or "working toward compliance with" over "fully compliant."

## 7. Scope, posture, and claims about practice

Accuracy in this portfolio is not only factual. A sentence can be true and still misrepresent the author's standing to say it. The scope rules in `accessibility-area-knowledge-resources.md` §3 are verification criteria, and they are checked at the same time as the citations.

Before publishing, confirm the resource does not:

- Offer determinations in live individual cases, legal opinion, clinical judgment, or assistive technology assessment
- Address accommodation advisors in the second person, or narrate casework from inside it
- Use "reduce the need for accommodation" — the formulation is always "reduce how often the duty must be invoked for barriers that were avoidable"
- Discuss reduced request volume without stating what the freed capacity is *for*
- Claim direct service experience the portfolio does not hold

Where the resource describes what accommodation practice is like, that description is attributed — "advisors describe," "as practised by accommodations professionals" — and sourced to the profession's own literature.

## 8. Legal and technical standards

Verify the full name, the year of enactment or Royal Assent, and the jurisdiction. Anchors:

| Standard | Detail | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Accessible Canada Act (ACA) | Royal Assent June 2019 | verified ✓ |
| Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) | Enacted 1990 | verified ✓ |
| Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) | 2005 | verified ✓ |
| WCAG 2.1 | W3C, June 2018 | verified ✓ |
| WCAG 2.2 | W3C, October 2023 | verified ✓ |
| CAST UDL Guidelines | Version 3.0, 2024 | verified ✓ |
| *Accessible British Columbia Act* | Year of Royal Assent and current regulation status | **verify before next use** |
| Section 504 / Section 508 | Statute, year, and current refresh status | **verify before next use** |
| EN 301 549 | Current version and date | **verify before next use** |
| IAAP CPACC / WAS Bodies of Knowledge | Version number and release date | **verify — versions change** |

Rules:

- Never conflate WCAG versions. Where a resource refers to the standard generally, say "WCAG 2.1 and 2.2"; where it makes a specific conformance claim, name the version.
- Do not describe any institution — including a former employer — as "fully compliant" with a standard without a source that makes that specific claim.
- Legal content carries a review date. A resource describing a regulatory landscape is a dated artifact; treat it as one and re-check annually.

## 9. Cross-file consistency

The portfolio's structure creates duplication, and duplication creates drift.

- **Interactive and linear versions must be corrected together.** Every fix to `x.html` is applied to `x-linear.html` in the same working session. A correction applied to only one version is worse than no correction, because it produces two conflicting published claims.

- **Resolve duplicate files.** `organizational-learning-design.html` / `organizational-learning-design__1_.html` and `research-to-practice-learning-systems.html` / `research-to-practice-learning-systems__1_.html` are currently byte-identical. They will not stay that way through the next round of edits. Delete or clearly supersede the duplicates before the next correction pass.

- **Reference numbering must match** between `landscape.html` and `landscape-linear.html`, and between each area map and its linear companion. Renumbering a reference in one file silently breaks every in-page link in the other.

- **Maintain the source register.** One running document of every source cited anywhere in the portfolio, with verified details. Before adding a source to a new resource, check the register. When a source recurs, copy the entry — retyping introduces errors.

- **Flag claims that appear in more than one file with different wording.** Where a figure or attribution shifts between resources, one version is wrong. Determine which, then correct both.

- Alt text and long descriptions are content, and are fact-checked as content. A visual description that does not match what the visual shows is an accuracy error as well as an accessibility one.

## 10. AI-assisted drafting

AI tools draft structure, argument, and prose well. They do not reliably produce accurate citations, figures, attributions, or biographical facts.

Treat all factual content in an AI-generated draft as unverified until checked against a primary source: names, dates, titles, editions, figures, institutional descriptions, and outcomes.

Failure modes observed in this body of work:

- **A real scholar with a fabricated statistic.** The most dangerous pattern, because the scholar checks out and the reader may not check further.
- Plausible but incorrect book titles
- Correct book, wrong edition or year
- Frameworks attributed to the best-known name rather than the originator
- Heuristics presented as measured findings
- Institutional or biographical claims that cannot be confirmed
- Confident synthesis of a literature that includes one source that does not exist

**The working rule.** If a fact in a draft is doing argumentative work — supporting a claim, establishing credibility, connecting two sections — it is the fact most likely to be wrong. Check it first.

## 11. Pre-publication checklist

*Work through every item before a resource goes live. Both versions, same session.*

- [ ] Every specific figure is traceable to a named source with a page number or URL — or has been removed
- [ ] No figure is attributed to a named researcher without confirming it appears in their work
- [ ] Every citation verified: author, full title, publisher, year, edition
- [ ] Every recurring citation matches the source register exactly
- [ ] Every framework attribution has a named originating publication
- [ ] Every standard is correctly named, versioned, and dated
- [ ] Every legal reference has correct name, year, and jurisdiction
- [ ] Every citation entry names an actual work, or has been moved out of the citation list
- [ ] All URLs resolve, in both the interactive and linear versions
- [ ] No source cited that has not been read
- [ ] Practice case studies labelled as practice-based; no invented outcome figures; partner names used only with permission
- [ ] Scope and posture rules (§7) checked line by line
- [ ] Linear version matches the interactive version in content, citations, and reference numbering
- [ ] Alt text and long descriptions match what the visuals show
- [ ] Source register updated with anything new
