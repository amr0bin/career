## 1. The Submission Package

> **My reality:** almost no paper is accepted as first submitted. Publication is a process of revision, and the researchers who publish are the ones who navigate that process well — not the ones who never get criticised.

A journal submission is a package, not just a manuscript. Getting the package right signals professionalism before a word of the science is read.

- **The manuscript**, formatted exactly to the journal's author guidelines
- **A cover letter** to the editor (see §2)
- **An abstract and keywords**, matched to the journal's structure
- **Declarations** — funding, conflicts of interest, ethical approval, data availability
- **Suggested reviewers**, where invited — and any I ask to be excluded, with reason
- **Supplementary files** — data, instruments, reporting checklists (e.g. PRISMA for a review)

> ⚠️ **Avoid:** Ignoring the author guidelines. Editors read non-compliance as carelessness, and many desk-reject on formatting alone before the work is ever assessed. Compliance is the cheapest possible way to be taken seriously.

## 2. The Cover Letter

The cover letter is my direct address to the editor, and it frames how the manuscript is received.

- State what the paper is and its central finding in two or three sentences
- Say why it fits *this* journal specifically — its scope, its readership, the conversation it publishes
- Confirm the work is original, not under review elsewhere, and that all authors approve
- Keep it short, precise, and free of overclaiming; the editor wants the gist and the fit, not a sales pitch

> 💡 **Key question:** Can the editor tell, from my letter alone, why this paper belongs in their journal and not a dozen others? A letter that could have been sent anywhere signals that I have not thought about fit — the first thing editors screen for.

## 3. Understanding the Decision

Editorial decisions come in a small set of forms, and reading them accurately keeps me from despair or misplaced hope.

| Decision | What it really means |
|----------|---------------------|
| **Reject** | Not for this journal — often about fit or scope, not the quality of the work. I revise per any comments and submit elsewhere |
| **Major revisions** | The journal is interested but needs substantial work — a genuine opportunity, not a rejection |
| **Minor revisions** | Close to acceptance; address the specifics carefully |
| **Accept** | Rare on first submission; accept graciously and check proofs meticulously |

> 📝 **Tip:** A "revise and resubmit" is good news wearing a stern face. It means the editor can imagine publishing my work. The task now is to make revision impossible to refuse.

## 4. The Response to Reviewers

This is the single most valuable genre in the publication process, and the one most early researchers handle badly. Done well, it turns a "major revisions" into an acceptance.

---

### 4.1 The Structure

- A **point-by-point response** document addressing every reviewer comment in turn
- For each comment: quote it, state what I changed, and point to where in the manuscript
- Where I disagree, say so respectfully and reason from evidence — I am not obliged to make every change, but I am obliged to answer every point
- A brief, courteous opening thanking the reviewers and editor for their time

> ⚠️ **Avoid:** Defensiveness, and its opposite, capitulation. I neither dismiss criticism nor make every demanded change uncritically. I engage with each point on its merits — that is what reviewers and editors are looking for.

---

### 4.2 The Posture

- Treat reviewers as collaborators improving the work, even when a comment stings — because the improved paper is the goal
- Address the hardest comment most thoroughly; that is where the decision usually turns
- Make the changes easy to verify: the editor should be able to see, at a glance, that I took the review seriously

> 💡 **Key question:** Have I answered every single comment — including the ones I disagree with — clearly enough that the editor never has to wonder whether I engaged? Unanswered comments are the fastest route to a second rejection.

---

## 5. Becoming a Reviewer

As I establish myself, I will be invited to review — and reviewing well is both a scholarly contribution and a way to sharpen my own writing.

- Review as I would want to be reviewed: rigorous, specific, and constructive, never gratuitously harsh
- Assess the work on its own terms — its rigour, contribution, and clarity — not against the paper I would have written
- Be concrete: point to the specific passage, name the specific concern, suggest the specific improvement
- Decline reviews I cannot do well or on time; a late, thin review helps no one

> 📝 **Tip:** Reviewing others' work is the fastest way to internalise what strong work looks like from the other side of the desk. Every review I write makes my own submissions better.

## Writing Process & Strategy

### ✍️ Choosing the Journal

| Order | Step | Why |
|-------|------|-----|
| 1 | **Identify where my target readers actually read** | The right journal reaches the people I want to influence |
| 2 | **Check scope and recent issues** | Confirm my paper fits the conversation the journal publishes |
| 3 | **Weigh reach, fit, and realism** | The most prestigious venue is not always the right first target |
| 4 | **Read the author guidelines before formatting** | Compliance is the cheapest professionalism there is |
| 5 | **Prepare the full package** | Manuscript, cover letter, declarations, checklists |

---

### ✅ Submission & Revision Checklist

- [ ] Journal chosen for genuine fit with my readership
- [ ] Manuscript formatted exactly to author guidelines
- [ ] Cover letter states the finding and the fit concisely
- [ ] All declarations complete (funding, ethics, conflicts, data)
- [ ] Reporting checklists included where required
- [ ] Every reviewer comment answered point by point on revision
- [ ] Changes are easy for the editor to verify
- [ ] Disagreements are reasoned respectfully, not dismissed or capitulated to
- [ ] The hardest comment is addressed most thoroughly
- [ ] Tone throughout is professional and collaborative

### 🎓 Notes for My Pathway

Publishing begins before the doctorate ends. My conceptual paper and MSc systematic review are both genuinely publishable, which means I will meet peer review early — and the resilience it builds is as valuable as the publications themselves. Learning that a "major revisions" is an opportunity, not a verdict, is one of the most useful lessons of an academic life, and the earlier I learn it the better.

Over time, a body of published work and a record of good reviewing establish me as a member of the field rather than a visitor to it. Combined with my public scholarship, this is how a non-traditional researcher becomes an authority whose voice carries — in the journals and beyond them.
