## 1. What a Conceptual Paper Is

> **My target length:** typically 6,000–10,000 words · no primary data · no ethics approval required

A conceptual paper makes a contribution through reasoning rather than through new empirical evidence. Its currency is the quality of the argument: the clarity of the problem it names, the rigour with which it draws on existing literature, and the originality of the synthesis it produces. Where an empirical paper asks *what did I find*, a conceptual paper asks *what should we understand differently, and why*.

This is the correct first output for my position for three reasons. It requires no university affiliation, no funding, and no participant access — only disciplined reading and clear thinking. It establishes my presence in the research ecosystem before I hold a formal credential. And it forces me to articulate the intellectual core of my whole research programme: the argument that designing the learning systems which train practitioners to deliver depression interventions is as important as designing the interventions themselves — and that accessibility must be built into both from the start.

> 💡 **The test I hold myself to:** Could a specialist reader finish my paper and say *"I had not framed it that way, and now I cannot unsee it"*? If the paper only restates what the field already accepts, it summarises rather than contributes.

## 2. The Four Types

A conceptual paper is not formless. Jaakkola (2020) identifies four recognised types, each with a distinct logic. Naming which one I am writing keeps the argument disciplined and tells reviewers I understand the genre.

| Type | What it does | What my version might look like |
|------|-------------|--------------------------------|
| **Theory synthesis** | Integrates two or more literatures that rarely speak to each other into a coherent new framework | Bringing learning-systems design, task-shifting, and accessible delivery together as one infrastructure for depression care |
| **Theory adaptation** | Takes a theory from one domain and applies it to a new problem, revealing something the host field missed | Applying instructional-systems design theory to the problem of scaling psychological interventions |
| **Typology** | Organises a messy field into a structured classification that clarifies distinctions | A typology of how creative-health interventions for depression are trained and transmitted |
| **Model** | Proposes a conceptual model of relationships between constructs, to be tested by others later | A model of how training-system design mediates the reach and fidelity of a depression intervention |

> 📝 **My likely home:** Theory synthesis. My distinctive contribution is the connection nobody in the field has yet made explicit — that the intervention and the system that teaches it are a single design problem. Synthesis is the type that rewards exactly that move.

## 3. Structure

A conceptual paper has more structural freedom than an empirical one, but freedom is not absence of form. The strongest conceptual papers move through a recognisable arc.

---

### 3.1 The Arc

1. **The problem and its stakes** — open by naming the real-world problem precisely (the depression treatment gap; the fact that most people who need care receive none) and why it matters now. Ground the significance in the literature, not in assertion.
2. **The gap in how the field thinks** — not a gap in data, but a gap in framing. What has the field failed to connect, name, or take seriously? This is the hinge of a conceptual paper.
3. **The central thesis** — state, in one or two sentences, the argument the paper will defend. Everything else exists to earn this claim.
4. **The conceptual framework** — define the key constructs and the theoretical resources I am drawing on. This is where I show command of the literatures I am synthesising.
5. **The argument, developed in propositions** — build the case in deliberate steps, each one a defensible claim supported by existing evidence and reasoning (see §4).
6. **Implications** — what follows if I am right? For theory, for practice, for policy, for how the field allocates its attention.
7. **Conclusion** — restate the contribution plainly and name what research should now test.

---

### 3.2 What Replaces Method and Results

An empirical paper spends its middle on method and results. A conceptual paper spends its middle on *the development of the argument*. The rigour that a reader would look for in my sampling and analysis, they will instead look for in the transparency of my reasoning: which literatures I drew on, why those and not others, and how each step of the argument follows from the last.

> ⚠️ **Avoid:** Presenting a literature review and calling it a conceptual paper. A review maps what is known. A conceptual paper *uses* what is known to build something new. If my paper has no thesis that could be argued against, it is a review wearing the wrong label.

## 4. Building the Argument

The intellectual core of a conceptual paper is the chain of propositions that carries the reader from the problem to the contribution.

---

### 4.1 Reasoning from Propositions

A proposition is a claim I assert and then defend — the conceptual equivalent of a finding. Strong conceptual papers are built from a small number of clearly stated propositions, each developed in turn.

- State each proposition explicitly, so the reader can see the scaffolding of the argument
- Support each one with existing evidence, established theory, and reasoning — never with unsupported assertion
- Make the logical connections between propositions visible: does proposition two depend on proposition one, or stand beside it?
- Anticipate the strongest objection to each proposition and address it

> 💡 **Key question:** For every claim I make, can I name the literature or the reasoning that warrants it? A conceptual paper that asserts more than it warrants is the failure mode reviewers punish most reliably.

---

### 4.2 Using the Literature Honestly

In a conceptual paper the literature is my evidence. How I handle it *is* my method.

- Draw on primary sources, not on other people's summaries of them
- Represent each source accurately, including where it complicates my argument
- Be explicit when the evidence base is thin — for the arts-and-depression literature especially, the honest position is that the evidence is promising and contested, not settled. Naming that is a strength, not a weakness
- Distinguish clearly between what the literature establishes and what I am proposing beyond it

---

### 4.3 Guarding Against My Own Bias

I come to this argument with creative practice over the past decade and a strong prior that creative approaches help. That prior can shape a conceptual paper invisibly.

- Argue the position I would have to answer if I disagreed with myself
- Where I rely on association rather than established mechanism, say so plainly
- Let the paper's claim be appropriately bounded: not *"creative approaches work and here is how to scale them,"* but *"here is why the training system is an under-theorised part of the scaling problem, whatever the eventual effect sizes turn out to be"*

## 5. Targeting a Journal

A conceptual paper must be aimed. The same argument is framed differently depending on where it is going.

| Journal | Best fit when my paper foregrounds… |
|---------|-------------------------------------|
| **Global Mental Health (Cambridge)** | The treatment gap and scalable, accessible approaches — open access, oriented to exactly this problem |
| **The Arts in Psychotherapy** | The creative-health methodology as the central object |
| **International Journal of Mental Health Systems** | Learning-systems infrastructure and scalable delivery as the core frame |

- Read the author guidelines before drafting — conceptual papers are welcome at some journals and out of scope at others; confirm the journal publishes them
- Read two or three recent conceptual papers in my target journal and match their register and depth
- Frame the contribution in the language the journal's readers already use

> 📝 **Tip:** The framing decision is strategic, not cosmetic. Foregrounding *systems infrastructure* positions me among implementation and health-systems researchers; foregrounding *creative methodology* positions me among arts-in-health researchers. I should choose the community I most want to enter first.

## Writing Process & Strategy

### ✍️ Recommended Order

| Order | Step | Why |
|-------|------|-----|
| 1 | **Write the thesis in one sentence** | If I cannot state the argument in a sentence, it is not ready to build |
| 2 | **Name the type** (§2) | Synthesis, adaptation, typology, or model — this disciplines the structure |
| 3 | **List the propositions** | The skeleton of the argument before any prose |
| 4 | **Assemble the evidence for each** | Confirm every proposition is warranted before writing it up |
| 5 | **Draft the argument section** | The core; write it first, while the logic is sharp |
| 6 | **Draft implications and conclusion** | Now I know what I have argued |
| 7 | **Draft the problem framing and introduction last** | I can only introduce the argument once it exists |

---

### ✅ Submission Checklist

- [ ] The paper has a single, arguable thesis — statable in one sentence
- [ ] The type of conceptual paper is clear (synthesis, adaptation, typology, or model)
- [ ] The gap named is a gap in *framing or theory*, not merely a gap in data
- [ ] The argument is built from explicit, individually defended propositions
- [ ] Every claim is warranted by primary literature or transparent reasoning
- [ ] The thinness of the evidence base is acknowledged where relevant
- [ ] My own positional bias is guarded against, not hidden
- [ ] Implications for theory, practice, and policy are stated
- [ ] The target journal publishes conceptual work and the framing matches its readership
- [ ] All sources cited accurately in the journal's required style
- [ ] The contribution could make an informed reader reframe the problem
