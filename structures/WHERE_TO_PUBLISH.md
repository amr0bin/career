# 🗞️ Where to Publish

> **A complete reference for getting my thinking into the world — the full spectrum, from idea essays to lived-experience craft**
> There is a large, active ecosystem of venues that will publish an unaffiliated writer on the strength of the work alone. This guide maps it by *mode of thought* — because the question is never just "where can I publish" but "where does *this particular piece* belong." It also names the access constraints honestly, so I never waste a good piece on a door that is closed to me right now.

## 1. Match the Piece to the Mode

> **The core move:** I don't ask "where can I get published." I ask "what *kind* of thinking is this piece, and which venue rewards that kind." A conceptual argument, a lived-experience essay, and a research explainer each have different natural homes.

Every venue rewards a particular mode of thought. Sending a raw personal essay to an idea magazine, or a dense conceptual argument to a flash-nonfiction journal, wastes a good piece on the wrong reader. The five modes below cover almost everything I will write outside the peer-reviewed literature. Before I submit anything, I name which mode it is — and that decision alone rules most venues in or out.

| The piece is really… | Its natural home | Section |
|----------------------|------------------|---------|
| An **idea or argument** for a smart general reader | Idea-essay magazines | §2 |
| A **lived experience**, rendered with craft | Creative-nonfiction venues | §3 |
| Where my **mental-health and art** worlds meet | Intersection venues | §4 |
| Something I want out **now, on my terms** | Platforms I control | §5 |
| An **evidence-based explainer** tied to my research | Academic platforms (once affiliated) | §6 |

## 2. Idea-Driven Essay Venues

This is my intellectual home — venues that publish serious thinking for a general-but-thoughtful reader, and take unaffiliated writers on the strength of the idea alone.

- **Aeon** — big-idea essays on philosophy, psychology, science, and culture; the premier home for exactly the conceptual thinking I am drawn to
- **Psyche** — Aeon's sister site, shorter pieces specifically on psychology, mental health, and the human condition; an almost exact fit for my focus
- **Nautilus** — science and ideas with a literary sensibility
- **Boston Review**, **Public Books**, **The Baffler**, **Dissent**, **Literary Hub** — essays and criticism that regularly feature non-academic writers

> 💡 **Key question:** Does this piece tell a smart reader something they did not know, or reframe something they thought they understood? Idea magazines buy *insight*, not information. If the piece only summarises, it is not yet ready for this mode.

> 📝 **Tip:** Psyche is the single best first target for my thinking. It sits precisely at psychology-and-the-human-condition, welcomes accessible expertise, and reaches exactly the readership I want to build — without any affiliation requirement.

## 3. Creative Nonfiction & Lived Experience

When I want to write *from* experience with literary care rather than argue a case, these venues are open, mostly free, and friendly to emerging writers.

- **Brevity** — the premier flash creative-nonfiction venue, 750 words maximum; a superb place to learn compression
- **Hippocampus Magazine** — dedicated entirely to creative nonfiction, and welcoming to newer writers
- **The Sun** — pays professional rates and *particularly* seeks work by writers who are marginalised by their circumstances or identity, explicitly including writers with disabilities (a small online submission fee; free by mail)

> ⚠️ **A boundary I hold:** writing from lived experience is not the same as disclosure without craft. The strongest lived-experience essays use the experience in service of an idea a reader can carry — not as raw confession. My [reflective essay](REFLECTIVE_ESSAY.md) and [public scholarship](PUBLIC_SCHOLARSHIP.md) guides both hold this line, and it applies here too.

## 4. The Intersection: Mental Health + Art

A few venues sit at the exact meeting point of my worlds — mental health, lived experience, and creative practice — and are worth watching and submitting to as they open.

- **The Shallot: Journal of Mental Health, Art, and Literature** (from The Layered Onion) — a journal whose stated goal is to amplify the voices of artists with lived mental and emotional health experience; my vision in journal form
- Disability-arts and mad-studies publications, small mental-health zines, and lived-experience anthologies appear regularly in the submission databases (§7) — these are where my creative and clinical selves publish as one

> 💡 **Key question:** Is there a venue whose entire purpose is the intersection this piece lives at? When there is, it is almost always a better home than a general venue — the readership is already the one I most want to reach.

## 5. Platforms I Control

Some routes have no gate at all. I own the decision to publish, start to finish.

- **My Substack** — the anchor of my public scholarship; the one venue no editor stands between me and my reader (see [Public Scholarship](PUBLIC_SCHOLARSHIP.md))
- **Preprint servers** — PsyArXiv for psychology; a way to timestamp and share scholarly work instantly, entering it into the record before or alongside formal publication
- **Reputable Medium publications** — some, such as De Minimis Magazine, exist specifically to platform new, emerging, and underrepresented writers

> 📝 **Tip:** Controlling the platform is freedom and responsibility at once. Without an editor, the rigour, the fact-checking, and the restraint are entirely mine to supply. I hold my self-published work to the same standard I would want an editor to hold it to — that is what keeps the platform an asset rather than a liability.

## 6. The Academic Platforms

The most prominent public-scholarship platform is worth understanding precisely, because its gate directly shapes my sequencing.

- **The Conversation** publishes short, evidence-based explainers (around 800 words) written by academics in collaboration with journalists, and republished free by major outlets — enormous reach. It has a Canadian edition
- **The constraint:** writers must be scholars or researchers currently affiliated with a university or research institution; being a past graduate does not qualify. Master's students can co-author with a faculty advisor; doctoral candidates can write on their research topic
- **What this means for me:** The Conversation is not a *now* option — it is a reward the MSc unlocks quickly. It is a concrete reason enrolment opens doors, and worth planning a pitch for early in the master's

> 💡 **Key question:** Is this venue gated by affiliation? If so, I file it under "once enrolled" and spend my current energy on the ungated venues in §2–§5 — which are plentiful. The affiliation gate is real, but it fences off a small corner of a large field.

## 7. The Infrastructure

I do not hunt venue by venue. These continually updated databases do the finding for me — searchable by genre, fee, reading period, and response time.

| Resource | What it gives me |
|----------|------------------|
| **Chill Subs** | The go-to free database for emerging writers — thousands of opportunities, filterable, with response-time and fee data |
| **CLMP — Calls for Submissions** | Current open calls from literary magazines and presses |
| **NewPages "Where to Submit"** | A weekly roundup of open opportunities across genres |
| **Poets & Writers** | A near-1,000-magazine database plus an "Open Reading Periods" page of who is reading right now |

> 📝 **Tip:** I keep a personal submission tracker — where I sent each piece, on what date, with what response deadline. Submitting is a numbers game with long response times; without a tracker it becomes a second job I lose track of. A simple spreadsheet is enough.

> ⚠️ **Avoid:** Content-marketing "best places to publish" lists run by ghostwriting or paid-service sites. They exist to sell editing, not to guide me. I trust writer-run and institution-run resources — the four above — and treat everything else with caution.

## Writing Process & Strategy

### ✍️ Angle, Not Topic — the Lesson Behind Every Rejection

The single most common reason editors reject a pitch is that it offers a topic rather than an angle: *"This is a topic, not a story. What's the angle?"* A topic is a subject area; an angle is a specific, fresh, arguable slice of it.

- "Accessible depression care" is a topic
- "What training fifty peer workers taught me about why interventions don't scale" is an angle
- The move from one to the other is the same one-idea discipline my [public scholarship guide](PUBLIC_SCHOLARSHIP.md) already teaches: one sharp idea, statable in a sentence, before I write

> 💡 **Key question:** Can I state the angle — not the subject — in one sentence a stranger would find intriguing? If I can only name the topic, the piece is not ready to pitch, whatever its venue.

---

### ✍️ Submission Discipline

- **Read the venue first.** Every editor says it: read what they publish before submitting. Match the register, the length, the sensibility
- **Follow the guidelines exactly.** Word limits, formatting, simultaneous-submission policies — non-compliance is the cheapest way to be rejected
- **Expect rejection, and depersonalise it.** Even strong pieces are declined for fit, timing, or because someone covered it first. Rejection is routing information, not a verdict
- **Keep pieces moving.** When one is declined, it goes back out the same week. A piece sitting in a drawer helps no one

---

### ✅ Submission Checklist

- [ ] I have named which *mode* this piece is (§1)
- [ ] The venue's mode matches the piece's mode
- [ ] I have confirmed the venue accepts unaffiliated writers (or filed it under "once enrolled")
- [ ] I can state the *angle*, not just the topic, in one sentence
- [ ] I have read recent pieces in the venue and matched its register
- [ ] I have followed the submission guidelines exactly
- [ ] Self-published work meets the standard I would want an editor to hold
- [ ] Lived-experience writing serves an idea, not raw disclosure
- [ ] The piece is logged in my submission tracker
- [ ] If declined, it is already scheduled to go back out

### 🎓 Notes for My Pathway

The through-line of this whole guide is that the ungated field is large. The one prominent affiliation-gated platform, The Conversation, is a reward the MSc unlocks — worth planning for, not waiting on. Everything in §2 through §5 is open to me today, and the venues that fit my focus most precisely — Psyche for psychology-and-the-human-condition, The Shallot for mental-health-and-art — need no credential at all.

Publishing across this spectrum does more than build a bibliography. It teaches me to move one idea between registers — the same argument as a scholarly conceptual paper, a public essay, and a lived-experience piece — which is the core skill of a knowledge-translator. Each venue is a different reader for the same commitment, and reaching all of them is how a portfolio scholar's voice actually carries. It pairs directly with my [public scholarship](PUBLIC_SCHOLARSHIP.md), [conceptual paper](CONCEPTUAL_PAPER.md), and [book review](BOOK_REVIEW.md) guides — this one simply tells me where the finished thinking should go.
