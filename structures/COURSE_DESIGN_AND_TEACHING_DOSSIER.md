# 🧑‍🏫 Course Design, Syllabus & Teaching Dossier

> **Designing a course backwards from outcomes, and documenting teaching so it counts for appointments.**
> Sessional and adjunct teaching is a seat in the ceiling, and your decade of learning design is a rare advantage. The teaching dossier is how that advantage is read by a hiring committee.

---

## 1. The structure

| Part | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Learning outcomes | What students will be able to do | Measurable verbs |
| Assessment | How each outcome is evidenced | Aligned before content is chosen |
| Learning activities | What students do each week | UDL: multiple means of engagement, representation, action |
| Syllabus | Outcomes, schedule, assessments, policies, access statement | Access built in, not added on |
| Teaching philosophy | One to two pages: beliefs, practices, evidence |  |
| Dossier evidence | Syllabi, materials, evaluations, peer observations, reflections | Curated, not exhaustive |

---

## 2. How to do it well

1. Design backwards: outcomes, then assessment, then activities.
2. Build UDL into every assessment (choice of format where the construct allows) — the same construct/method distinction as your corpus study.
3. Collect evidence from the first guest lecture: a peer observation and a short student survey.
4. Write the philosophy statement with one concrete example per belief.
