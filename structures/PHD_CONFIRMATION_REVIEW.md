# 🪪 PhD Confirmation / Upgrade Review

> **A complete reference for the first major doctoral milestone — the gate from provisional to full PhD candidate**
> Most doctoral programmes require a formal review in the first year to eighteen months, variously called confirmation, upgrade, transfer, or the qualifying review. It is where I demonstrate that my project is viable, that I am capable of doctoral work, and that the whole thing can realistically be finished. Passing it converts me from a provisional registrant into a confirmed PhD candidate.

## 1. What Confirmation Decides

> **My reality:** this is the first point at which the institution formally asks *should this project, and this candidate, continue as a PhD?* It is a genuine gate, not a formality — but one that a well-prepared candidate passes.

Confirmation exists to catch problems early, while they can still be fixed: a project that is too big, too small, or unviable; a candidate who is not yet working at doctoral level; a plan that cannot be finished in the time. The review is an act of protection — it is far kinder to redirect a project at eighteen months than to let it fail at the viva. Approached that way, the milestone is an opportunity to get expert eyes on my work while there is still time to act on what they see.

> 💡 **Key question:** Can I convince a panel that this project is genuinely doctoral in ambition, that I am the person to do it, and that it can be completed in the time remaining? Those three claims are the whole assessment.

---

## 2. The Written Report

The confirmation report is, in effect, the skeleton of the eventual thesis with the completed work attached. Requirements vary, but it typically includes:

- **Introduction and research questions** — the problem, its significance, and the questions the thesis will answer
- **Literature review** — a substantial, critical review demonstrating command of the field and locating the gap
- **Methodology** — the design, justified, with evidence I understand how to execute it
- **Work completed to date** — pilot data, a completed systematic review, early analysis: proof the project is underway
- **A detailed plan to completion** — the remaining chapters, phased over the time remaining, with milestones
- **A timeline** — realistic, accounting for data collection, analysis, writing, and the unexpected

> 📝 **Tip:** The literature review and methodology chapters written for confirmation are often the first real drafts of thesis chapters. Time invested here is not spent twice — it is the thesis beginning to exist.

---

## 3. The Review Meeting

Confirmation usually includes an oral component — a panel discussion or mini-viva, sometimes with an independent assessor. It rehearses the final viva in miniature.

- I present or summarise the project and answer questions on its rationale, design, and feasibility
- The panel probes exactly where the final examiners eventually will: is the question doctoral, is the method sound, is the plan realistic?
- It is a lower-stakes rehearsal of defending my work aloud — a skill that only develops through practice

> 💡 **Key question:** If the panel asks *"why this method and not the obvious alternative?"* or *"what is your contribution going to be?"* — can I answer clearly, now? These are the same questions the viva will ask; confirmation is the first place to practise the answers.

---

## 4. The Three Things Assessed

Every confirmation panel weighs the same three things. I make sure each is unmistakable in both the report and the meeting.

| What is assessed | What convinces the panel |
|------------------|--------------------------|
| **Viability of the project** | A doctoral-scale question, a sound design, and a real contribution the field does not already have |
| **Capability of the candidate** | Writing and reasoning already at doctoral level; command of the literature; completed work that proves I can execute |
| **Feasibility of completion** | A realistic, phased plan that fits the time and resources, with the risks named and managed |

> ⚠️ **Avoid:** A report that is all plan and no product. The single most reassuring thing I can show a panel is completed work — a finished review, clean pilot data — because it proves the project is not merely intended but underway.

## Writing Process & Strategy

### ✍️ Recommended Order

| Order | Step | Why |
|-------|------|-----|
| 1 | **Draft the literature review** | It doubles as a real thesis chapter and proves command of the field |
| 2 | **Draft the methodology** | Shows I know how to execute, not just what I plan |
| 3 | **Write up work completed** | The evidence that the project is underway |
| 4 | **Build the plan and timeline to completion** | The feasibility case, phased and realistic |
| 5 | **Write the introduction and questions last** | Framed once the rest exists |
| 6 | **Rehearse the meeting with my supervisor** | The oral component rewards spoken practice |

---

### ✍️ Possible Outcomes

| Outcome | What it means |
|---------|--------------|
| **Confirmed / upgraded** | The most common result for a prepared candidate — the project proceeds as a full PhD |
| **Minor revisions** | Small changes to the report or plan, then confirmation |
| **Referred / resubmit** | The panel needs more before confirming — a signal to address specific weaknesses, not a failure |
| **Recommended for a different award** | Rare — the panel judges the work better suited to an MPhil; a redirection, caught early enough to matter |

---

### ✅ Preparation Checklist

- [ ] Research questions are doctoral in ambition and clearly stated
- [ ] Literature review demonstrates command of the field and a real gap
- [ ] Methodology is justified and shows I can execute it
- [ ] Completed work is presented as proof the project is underway
- [ ] A phased plan to completion fits the time remaining
- [ ] Timeline accounts for data, analysis, writing, and slippage
- [ ] Risks to the project are named and mitigated
- [ ] I can defend every major choice aloud
- [ ] The contribution the thesis will make is stated plainly
- [ ] Supervisor has reviewed the report and rehearsed the meeting with me

---

### 🎓 Notes for My Pathway

Confirmation is the doctorate's early proof of concept, and my strategy of building outputs before and during the degree pays off exactly here. Arriving at confirmation with a published conceptual paper and a completed systematic review means the "work completed to date" section is already strong, and the panel meets a candidate whose project is visibly real.

It is also the first of the two oral examinations of doctoral study. The confidence to defend my work aloud — to answer the challenge question without hedging — is built through practice, and confirmation is where that practice begins, with the final viva still years away.
