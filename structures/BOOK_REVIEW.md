# 📕 Scholarly Book Review

> **A complete reference for the short, publishable review I can write now — and throughout my career**
> A scholarly book review is a concise, critical assessment of an academic book, written for an academic journal. It is one of the most accessible entry points into the published record: journals actively need reviewers, the form is short, and a strong review demonstrates exactly the critical reading that scholarship is built on. It is a genuine publication I can begin producing before I hold any credential.

## 1. Why Review Books

> **My format:** typically 800–1,500 words · commissioned or proposed to a journal · a real citable publication

Reviewing books is the shortest path from where I am to a line on my academic record. Journals have a standing need for competent reviewers, the barrier to being commissioned is low, and the resulting review is a genuine, citable publication. Beyond the credential, reviewing does real intellectual work: it forces me to read a whole scholarly argument critically, situate it in its field, and articulate a judgement — the same muscles every other output in this repository depends on.

For me specifically, reviewing recent books in disability studies, mental health, and arts-and-health does something more: it keeps me current in exactly the literatures my research programme draws on, and it puts my name into those scholarly conversations early.

> 💡 **Key question:** Have I read this book carefully enough to say not just what it argues, but how well it argues it, and what it contributes to the field? A review that only summarises has done half the job.

## 2. Choosing a Book and a Venue

The review begins with a good match between the book, the journal, and my own knowledge.

- Choose a **recent** book — most journals review within a year or two of publication
- Choose one close to my expertise, so my critical judgement is credible
- Identify journals in the field that publish reviews, and read their review section to learn the length and register they expect
- Many journals invite proposals: I can approach the reviews editor offering to review a specific recent title, which is often welcomed

> 📝 **Tip:** A book review editor is far more likely to say yes to a specific, well-judged offer ("I'd like to review [recent title] for your journal") than to a general availability. Naming the book shows I already know the field.

---

## 3. Reading for Review

Reading a book to review it is different from reading to learn from it. I read with a set of questions running throughout.

- **What is the central argument, and does the book deliver it?** The through-line, not just the topics covered
- **Who is the intended audience, and does it serve them?**
- **What is the evidence or method, and is it sound?**
- **Where does it sit in the field — what does it confirm, extend, or challenge?**
- **What are its limitations, silences, or unexamined assumptions?**
- **What did it contribute that I did not have before reading it?**

> 📝 **Tip:** I take notes as I read, marking passages for both praise and criticism with page numbers. Reconstructing my evidence afterward is slow and imprecise; a review anchored in specific pages is more credible and far easier to write.

## 4. The Structure

A book review is short, so every part earns its place. The shape is consistent.

| Element | What it does | Rough share |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Framing** | The book's subject, its aim, and where it sits in the field | ~15% |
| **Summary of the argument** | What the book argues and how it is organised — concise, not chapter-by-chapter | ~35% |
| **Critical evaluation** | Strengths and limitations, with specific evidence | ~35% |
| **Verdict** | Who it is for, what it contributes, whether I recommend it | ~15% |

- Lead with the book's argument, not with throat-clearing about the field
- Summarise selectively — I convey the shape of the argument, not a table of contents
- Make the evaluation the centre of gravity; a review without judgement is a blurb

> ⚠️ **Avoid:** The chapter-by-chapter walk-through. Summarising each chapter in turn produces a description, not a review. I characterise the argument as a whole and spend my words on assessing it.

## 5. The Craft of Fair Critique

The reputation a review builds — mine — rests on being rigorous and generous at the same time.

- Assess the book the author actually wrote, on its own terms, not the book I would have written
- Ground every criticism in specific evidence from the text; unsupported dismissal reflects on me, not the author
- Acknowledge real strengths genuinely; a review that finds only fault reads as posturing
- Where I disagree, disagree with the argument, respectfully and precisely
- Remember that scholarly communities are small, and the author may one day read my work, examine my thesis, or sit on my panel — fairness is both right and wise

> 💡 **Key question:** Would the author, reading my review, recognise it as a fair account of their book — even where it is critical? That is the standard of a review that builds my credibility rather than spending it.

## Writing Process & Strategy

### ✍️ Recommended Order

| Order | Step | Why |
|-------|------|-----|
| 1 | **Secure the commission or agreement** | Confirm the journal wants the review before writing |
| 2 | **Read the whole book, taking anchored notes** | Judgement requires the complete argument |
| 3 | **Name the book's central argument in a sentence** | The spine of the review |
| 4 | **Draft the evaluation** | The heart; write it while my judgement is sharp |
| 5 | **Draft the summary and framing** | Written to set up the evaluation |
| 6 | **Write the verdict** | Who it is for, and whether I recommend it |
| 7 | **Cut to length** | Reviews are short; every sentence must earn its place |

---

### ✅ Submission Checklist

- [ ] The review is commissioned by or proposed to a suitable journal
- [ ] The book is recent and close to my expertise
- [ ] I have read the whole book, with page-anchored notes
- [ ] The central argument is characterised, not just the topics listed
- [ ] The summary is selective, not chapter-by-chapter
- [ ] Evaluation is the centre of gravity, with specific evidence
- [ ] Strengths and limitations are both addressed
- [ ] Criticism is fair, precise, and grounded in the text
- [ ] The verdict names the audience and the contribution
- [ ] The review is within the journal's length and style
- [ ] Bibliographic details and formatting follow the journal's convention

### 🎓 Notes for My Pathway

The book review is the write-now output with the lowest barrier of all: a single well-read book and a short, well-judged piece produce a real publication. Written across my career, reviews keep me current in my fields and keep my name in the conversation — and each one rehearses, in miniature, the critical reading that my literature reviews, annotated bibliographies, and eventual examining all depend on.

It also pairs naturally with my public scholarship. A book that matters can become both a scholarly review for the field and an accessible essay for a wider audience — the same reading, translated to two registers, doing double duty.

### 📚 Credible examples

- Venues: *Canadian Journal of Disability Studies*, *Disability & Society* and *Disability Studies Quarterly* all publish book reviews. Read three recent ones in the target venue before drafting.
- A good first book to review is a recent title on disability and higher education in Canada. Ask the venue's review editor for a copy.

### ⚡ Do it well and fast

- Review a book you're already reading for the thesis line, so the reading counts twice.
- Draft in one sitting from the annotation: summary (40%), evaluation (40%), who should read it (20%).
