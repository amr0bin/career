## 1. What the Portfolio Demonstrates

> **My reality:** the portfolio is assembled across a whole placement year, not written at the end. Its integrity depends on evidence gathered while the work is live.

The clinical portfolio answers a question no exam can: *is this person becoming a practitioner I would trust with a vulnerable client?* It demonstrates three things at once — that I am developing the required competencies, that I practise safely and ethically, and that I reflect on my work in a way that drives genuine development. The assessors are experienced clinicians; they are reading for the difference between performed competence and the real thing.

For me, this is also where the threads of my whole vision meet the client in the room. Everything abstract about accessible, humane care becomes concrete here, in how I actually sit with a person's distress.

> 💡 **Key question:** Does my portfolio show a practitioner who is growing — who names their edges honestly and works on them — rather than one performing a finished competence they do not yet have? Assessors trust honest development over polished perfection.

## 2. The Components

Programmes vary, but a clinical portfolio typically gathers:

- **Process reports** — close analyses of recorded or transcribed sessions (see §3)
- **Competency evidence** — a mapping of my practice against the programme's or accrediting body's competency framework
- **Supervision records** — logs of supervision, and reflection on what supervision surfaced
- **Reflective commentary** — the developmental narrative connecting the evidence
- **Case discussion** — fuller accounts of work with particular clients, formulation included
- **Placement documentation** — hours, settings, and supervisor sign-off

> 📝 **Tip:** The portfolio is more than the sum of its documents. The reflective commentary is what turns a folder of evidence into an argument that I am developing as a practitioner. It is where the assessor meets *me*.

---

## 3. The Process Report

The process report is the most demanding single component: a close, honest analysis of a real therapeutic interaction, usually built from a transcript or recording of part of a session.

---

### 3.1 What It Contains

- **Context** — where in the therapy this session sits, and what is being worked on
- **The extract** — a transcribed passage of the session, turn by turn
- **Moment-by-moment analysis** — what was happening between me and the client, what I did, and why
- **Theoretical commentary** — how my framework illuminates the interaction
- **Reflexive analysis** — what was activated in me, what I might have missed, and what I would do differently

> ⚠️ **Avoid:** Selecting a passage where I performed well and analysing why it worked. The most valuable process reports examine a moment of difficulty, rupture, or uncertainty — because that is where the real learning, and the real evidence of reflective capacity, lives.

---

### 3.2 The Discipline of Honesty

A process report rewards the same honesty as a reflective essay, applied to a live clinical moment.

- Name the moments I missed a cue, moved too fast, or retreated into technique
- Analyse my own responses — the countertransference, the discomfort, the pull to reassure — as data, not as failures to hide
- Show that I can hold my own fallibility and still practise safely; that combination is exactly what assessors are looking for

> 💡 **Key question:** Would my supervisor recognise the practitioner in this report as the one they actually supervise? A process report that airbrushes my practice into competence I have not yet reached will not ring true to an experienced reader.

---

## 4. Evidencing Competencies

Much of the portfolio involves mapping my actual practice against a formal competency framework (the programme's, and often an accrediting body's such as the BPS or BACP standards).

- For each competency, point to specific evidence — a moment in a process report, a supervision reflection, a case discussion — rather than asserting it generically
- Be precise: "I demonstrate the capacity to work with rupture" means nothing without the moment that shows it
- Where a competency is still developing, say so and show the plan; assessors distinguish honest development from concealment

> 📝 **Tip:** I keep a running competency log throughout placement, tagging moments as they happen. Reconstructing the evidence at the end of the year is far harder, and the reconstruction always shows.

---

## 5. Supervision and Reflexivity

Supervision is central to counselling training, and the portfolio must show that I use it well.

- Record supervision consistently, and reflect on what it surfaced — not just what was discussed, but what changed as a result
- Show the loop: material arises in practice → I bring it to supervision → my practice shifts → the portfolio records the shift
- Demonstrate that I can receive challenge and use it, rather than defend against it

Reflexivity runs through the whole portfolio. My history, identity, and assumptions shape how I practise; the portfolio shows that I know this and work with it rather than pretending to a neutral clinical stance that does not exist.

## Writing Process & Strategy

### ✍️ Building It Across the Year

| When | What | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Throughout | Log hours, supervision, and competency moments as they happen | Reconstruction at year-end is harder and less honest |
| Mid-placement | Draft one process report early | The first is always the hardest; do it while there is time to learn from it |
| Ongoing | Keep reflective notes close to the experience | Reflection loses its truth when it goes cold |
| Final term | Write the reflective commentary that ties the evidence together | It can only be written once the evidence exists |
| Before submission | Check every competency claim against real evidence | Unsupported claims are the most common weakness |

---

### ✍️ Ethics and Confidentiality — Non-Negotiable

- Every client is fully anonymised — no names, no identifying details, altered where necessary to protect identity
- Recording and transcription happen only with explicit, documented client consent
- The portfolio itself is handled as confidential clinical material
- If in doubt about whether a detail is safe to include, I remove it or consult my supervisor first

---

### ✅ Submission Checklist

- [ ] Evidence was gathered across the placement, not reconstructed at the end
- [ ] Process report(s) analyse real, often difficult, clinical moments
- [ ] Process reports are honest about what I missed or would do differently
- [ ] Each competency claim points to specific evidence
- [ ] Developing competencies are named honestly, with a plan
- [ ] Supervision is recorded and its impact on practice is shown
- [ ] Reflexivity runs throughout — my positionality is examined
- [ ] A reflective commentary ties the evidence into a developmental argument
- [ ] Every client is fully anonymised; consent for recording is documented
- [ ] Placement hours and supervisor sign-off are complete
- [ ] All theoretical and clinical sources cited in APA 7th
