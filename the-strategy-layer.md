# The Strategy Layer

**Stability as a condition of contribution — and the twenty-five-year shape of the whole thing**

Status: working document · v1
Companion to: `the-sequence.html`, `my-vision.html`, `three-assets.md`, `landscape.html`, `cpacc-twelve-weeks.md`
Governed by: `factchecking-accuracy-vision-documents.md` — see §10 for what in here is fact, intention, and projection

## 1. What this document is, and why it did not exist until now

The portfolio has an impact thesis, a credential path, a skills map, a hundred-node map of what is already known, and an honest account of its own weaknesses. It has never had a **stability thesis** — a stated position on money, security, and the conditions under which the person carrying all of this can keep carrying it for twenty-five years.

That absence was not modesty. It was a category error. Every one of these documents treats income as a supporting condition: the day job "funds the credential years," consulting is "growth income," the financial plan is a task belonging to Phase three. The word that recurs is *funds*. Money is treated as the thing that pays for the work rather than as part of the design of the work.

The correction is one sentence, and the rest of this document follows from it:

> **A contribution requires a contributor who is still standing, and unafraid.** Financial precarity does not merely make impact harder to achieve; it changes which work is possible. It makes you take the client you should decline, stay in the role you have outgrown, publish the thinner paper because the funded one takes another year, and — most expensively — it makes you unable to say no. The capacity to refuse is the mechanism by which values become choices. Everything about the shape of the far side depends on having it.

So this document does three things. It states the destination in terms of **optionality** rather than titles. It corrects a strategic undervaluation running through every other file — the treatment of the accessibility and learning design portfolio as scaffolding rather than as one of two load-bearing columns. And it names the financial architecture, phase by phase, including the one point on the path where the current plan is genuinely exposed.

## 2. The destination, stated honestly

The far side is not a job title. It is a **capacity to choose**, exercised across four modes held simultaneously:

| Mode | What it means in practice | What it should *not* be |
|---|---|---|
| **The chooser** | Projects taken because they are the right work, at a rate that reflects genuine scarcity | A calendar filled reactively by whoever asked first |
| **The room** | A small, deliberate caseload — depression, expressive approaches, deep work | The financial engine. Therapy income is linear and capped by hours in a body |
| **The lectern** | Teaching and supervising at SFU, UBC, or UVic — the strange interdisciplinary fluency handed forward | A title pursued for standing rather than for the students |
| **The studio** | Dear Ordinary as art in its own right, protected structurally | The stream that gets postponed when the others expand |

The financial definition that matches this, and it is worth writing plainly:

> **Financial freedom here means that no single income stream can coerce a decision.** Not wealth. Not retirement. Specifically: enough durable capital and enough uncorrelated income that losing any one stream is an inconvenience rather than an emergency — and that the room, the lectern, and the studio can each be sized by desire rather than by need.

That is a measurable target, and it converts an aspiration into a design problem. Two numbers make it concrete, and both should be worked out rather than assumed: the annual cost of the life actually wanted, and the buffer required to survive the deliberate income trough in Phase three. Neither number is in any document in this portfolio. Both belong in the next revision.

## 3. The correction: the landscape is a column, not the scaffolding

Across `my-vision.html`, `three-assets.md`, and the sequence, learning design appears as *core income*, *the stable foundation*, *the day job*, and *the income that funds the credential years*. Read together, they describe a career that is waiting for its real subject to arrive.

The `landscape.html` map says something different. It records roughly a hundred mapped contributions across a shared trunk and five branches — learning experience design, universal design for learning, knowledge mobilization, learning systems design, and accessibility — each cited, each grounded in named theory. That is not a day job. That is a **body of expertise deep enough to teach from, publish from, consult at a premium from, and build products out of.**

Three consequences follow, and they change the plan.

**First, it is the fastest-moving asset in the portfolio.** Every other stream compounds on a horizon of years: papers, credentials, supervised hours, a training system with fidelity data behind it. The accessibility and learning design work can raise earning power *this quarter*. It is the only stream that can. Anything that treats it as a holding pattern is spending the one asset that is already liquid.

**Second, the accessibility specialism is where scarcity actually lives.** Learning designers are numerous. Learning designers who can work fluently in disability studies, universal design, essential requirements, accommodation law, procurement, clinical placement access, and health-sector knowledge translation are not. The premium is not paid for instructional design; it is paid for the intersection. That is a pricing fact, not a compliment — and it argues for the consulting practice to be scoped, named, and priced as a specialism rather than as generalist course design.

**Third — and this is the part no other document in the portfolio has noticed — this is a credible route to the professorship.** The plan currently assumes the lectern arrives after the doctorate, through research standing. There is a second door. Universities in BC appoint teaching-focused faculty in streams that are evaluated on exactly the evidence this landscape already contains: excellence in teaching, demonstrated educational leadership, and sustained contribution to curriculum development and innovation. UBC's educational leadership stream (Assistant, Associate, and full Professor of Teaching) is tenure-track and evaluated on those criteria. That does not make the door easy — appointments in these streams normally require a terminal degree, and competition is real — but it means the fifteen years already spent are *directly assessable qualifications for an academic appointment*, not merely the background to one. See §10; the specifics of each institution's streams and their degree requirements must be verified before any of this is acted on.

The reframe, in one line: **accessibility and learning design is not what pays for the work. It is one of the two things the work is made of.**

## 4. Three engines, and only one of them is a salary

Financial freedom over twenty-five years is not produced by a higher salary. It is produced by holding three different kinds of income at once, because they fail in different ways and at different times.

**Engine one — rate.** Time sold, priced at scarcity. Salary and consulting both live here. It is reliable, immediate, and hard-capped: it stops the day you stop. The lever is not more hours but higher rate, and rate responds to legible scarcity markers — the CPACC, the health-sector position, the published resources, the specialism named narrowly enough to be memorable.

**Engine two — the asset that earns without you in the room.** This is the engine the portfolio keeps *describing* without ever naming as income: the facilitator-training curriculum, the licensed training system, the resources, the courses, the book. `three-assets.md` already contains the correct instinct — *open the intervention, monetize the methodology*. What it has not said is that the methodology is the only part of this plan capable of producing money that is not exchanged for hours, and that this is the mechanism by which a therapist with a small caseload and a protected studio practice is nonetheless secure.

**Engine three — capital.** The unglamorous one, and the one entirely absent from the portfolio. Savings rate, registered accounts, and a deliberately built buffer. It does not compound because it is clever; it compounds because it is early and uninterrupted. Note the structural fact plainly: **every year of full-time study is a year this engine is switched off**, and that cost belongs in the decision about each credential rather than discovered afterward. This is a structural observation, not investment advice — the specifics belong with an accountant or fee-only planner who knows the BC picture.

The design principle: **let engine one fund engine three while engine two is built.** That sentence is the financial plan in miniature.

## 5. The money seasons, mapped onto the phases

Keyed to the same five phases, so it reads alongside the sequence.

**Ground — the accumulation phase, and the most important one financially.**
Highest earning capacity relative to demands, and the only phase where a buffer can be built without cost to anything else. Three moves: get the role inside health or accessibility, hold the CPACC before signing it, and start the buffer immediately rather than when Phase three appears on the horizon. The published resources and the conceptual paper do double duty here — they are scholarship and they are rate justification.

**Frame — hold flat, deliberately.**
Leeds is part-time and modular; income should not drop. The discipline is refusing to let coursework justify a reduction in earnings that the plan does not require. Consulting holds existing clients and does not grow. The buffer keeps filling.

**The Room — the exposure.**
This is the single largest financial risk in the entire plan, and it is currently a one-line note in the sequence: a cohort-based master's with several hundred supervised practicum hours in daytime placements, which cannot be carried alongside a senior full-time role. Translated into money: **a multi-year period of sharply reduced income, increased outgoings, and near-zero capacity to take on new work** — arriving at the point in life when the capital engine most needs to be running.

The plan is not wrong to include this phase. It is wrong to leave it unfunded. The response has three parts, and each belongs in Ground rather than in Phase three:

1. **Size the trough before entering it.** Tuition, plus the earnings not made, plus the years of it. One page of arithmetic, done years early.
2. **Build the buffer to cover it.** This is what the accumulation phase is *for*, and it is the strongest argument for pushing rate hard now.
3. **Keep one non-time-bound income stream alive through it** — licensed materials, an existing retainer, a course. Engine two exists precisely so that this phase does not require the buffer to carry the whole weight.

There is also a legitimate fourth option, and the portfolio should say so out loud: **the trough can be made shallower by taking Phase three more slowly**, part-time where the programme permits it, at the cost of years. Time is the cheapest currency this plan has. It is keyed to conditions, not dates, precisely so that this trade is available.

**The Question — enter funded, or do not enter.**
The sequence already makes the doctorate conditional on a question and a supervisor. Add a third condition of equal standing: **funding**. Scholarship, stipend, employer support, or grant. An unfunded doctorate in this position does not merely cost tuition; it switches off the capital engine during the highest-earning years available, and it does so for a credential the sequence already describes as optional. Funded, it is an excellent decision. Unfunded, it is the most expensive item in the plan by an order of magnitude, and the honest answer may be no.

**Deployment — diversified, and finally chosen.**
Four or five uncorrelated streams: consulting at premium rate, licensed training system, a small caseload, teaching income, and whatever the writing becomes. No single one is load-bearing. That is what optionality is made of.

## 6. The lectern: four doors, honestly ranked

The vision documents assume one route to teaching — post-doctorate, research stream. There are four, and they differ enormously in cost, timing, and money.

**Sessional and adjunct instruction.** Reachable earliest, often with a master's plus significant practice expertise. Pay is per-course and modest; the value is relationship, access, and a teaching record. Best understood as a foot in the door and a supplement, never as income.

**Clinical supervision and practicum instruction.** Available once registered and experienced. Pays reasonably, deepens the clinical work, builds standing inside counselling psychology programmes, and is directly on the path already planned.

**The educational leadership / teaching stream.** Tenure-track, salaried, and assessed on teaching excellence, educational leadership, and curriculum development and innovation. This is the door the landscape portfolio was unknowingly building toward for fifteen years. It typically requires a terminal degree, which makes it an *argument for* a doctorate rather than an alternative to one — but it widens which doctorate counts, since education, health sciences, and interdisciplinary degrees all serve it.

**The research stream.** The route the plan currently assumes. Requires the doctorate and a research record, offers the most research freedom, and is the most competitive.

The strategic note: doors two and three are reachable *without* the research-stream commitment, and door three rewards precisely the evidence already accumulated. If the lectern matters at twenty-five years — and it does — the plan should stop treating it as a downstream consequence of the doctorate and start treating it as a target with its own qualifying evidence, gathered continuously.

## 7. The highest-leverage moves, ranked

Not a to-do list. The small number of moves with disproportionate downstream effect, ordered by leverage per unit of effort.

1. **CPACC now, before the next role is signed.** The only credential in the plan that raises earning power inside the current job search, and it is already scheduled in `cpacc-twelve-weeks.md`. Every month it is deferred is a month of foregone rate. *(This has been moved to Phase one across the vision, checklist, and deployment documents.)*
2. **Publish the four written accessibility resources.** Written, unpublished, and doing nothing. They cost an editing pass and convert directly into credibility, search presence, and pricing power. The essential-requirements piece needs its scarcity claim revised first.
3. **Price the consulting as a specialism, not a service.** The intersection is the product. A named, narrow, defensible offer at a defensible rate beats a broad one at a market rate, and it takes an afternoon to rewrite.
4. **Add outcome measurement to Make Space.** Already identified as the highest-leverage small move in the portfolio. It also converts a practice into an asset that can be licensed — which is engine two.
5. **Size the Phase three trough and start the buffer.** Ten years early is not too early. This is the move that determines whether the room is entered from strength or from strain.
6. **Build the training system as a product, not only as research output.** Manualized, licensable, teachable by others. It is the mechanism for both scale and security, and those turn out to be the same mechanism.
7. **Gather teaching-stream evidence continuously.** Guest lectures, curriculum work, invited workshops, documented educational leadership. Cheap now, decisive later.
8. **Take the doctorate only when funded.** The highest-cost decision in the plan deserves the strictest condition.

## 8. Decision rules

Rules exist so that the same question is not re-argued every time it arrives tired.

**The coercion test.** Before accepting work: *would I take this if I did not need the money?* A steady no across a quarter is a signal about the financial structure, not about the work.

**The ten-year asset test.** Before a large commitment: *what does this leave behind in ten years?* An asset — a credential, a published work, a system others use, capital — or an experience. Both are legitimate. The error is spending on one while believing you are buying the other.

**The trough rule.** No phase that reduces income is entered before it has been sized and funded.

**The floor rule.** Every stream has a minimum viable rhythm that is defended, and a maximum that is refused. This already exists in the vision documents for time. It applies identically to money: the buffer has a floor that is not spent, whatever the opportunity.

**The scarcity rule.** Price the intersection, never the component. The market rate for a learning designer is not the rate for this profile.

## 9. What this changes, and what it does not

It does not change the mission. Depression, access, scale, and the room are untouched, and the sequence's phase structure holds exactly as written.

It changes four things:

- **CPACC moves to Phase one**, resolving a drift where the sequence had it early and three other documents had it in Phase two.
- **Accessibility and learning design are reclassified** from supporting income to a load-bearing column, with its own trajectory, its own scarcity, and its own route to the lectern.
- **A financial architecture is stated** — three engines, five seasons, one named exposure — where previously there was a single line about a plan to be made later.
- **The destination is restated as optionality**, which is what the four modes at twenty-five years actually require.

And it adds one sentence that belongs at the front of every other document in this portfolio: *the stability is not a compromise with the mission. It is the part of the mission that makes the rest of it survivable.*

## 10. Verification note

Per `factchecking-accuracy-vision-documents.md` §4 — every claim below is marked fact, intention, or projection.

**Analytical (argument, not citation).** Sections 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, and 9 are reasoning. They stand or fall on the argument, and no verification applies. The rankings in §7 are judgements about leverage, not findings.

**Verified — 15 August 2026.** UBC appoints tenure-track and tenured faculty in an educational leadership stream with the ranks Assistant Professor of Teaching, Associate Professor of Teaching, and Professor of Teaching; appointment and promotion in that stream are assessed on teaching excellence, demonstrated educational leadership, and contributions to curriculum development and innovation. Source: UBC Human Resources and UBC Faculty Association materials on tenure-stream appointments.

**Unverified — check before acting. Ranked by cost if wrong.**

1. **Degree requirements for each teaching-stream appointment, at each of SFU, UBC, and UVic.** The general expectation is a terminal degree, but "terminal degree" is defined per unit and per posting. §6 is built on this. Confirm against current postings and each institution's collective agreement before any decision defers or accelerates a doctorate on these grounds.
2. **Whether SFU and UVic operate comparable teaching-focused streams, and under what titles.** Only UBC's stream has been checked. Do not assume symmetry between institutions.
3. **Sessional and clinical-supervision appointment requirements** in counselling psychology programmes at all three institutions, including whether registration is a precondition.
4. **Whether any BC counselling psychology master's permits genuinely part-time enrolment**, and how practicum hours are scheduled within it. §5's proposal to shallow the trough by extending it depends entirely on this and is currently a projection with an unchecked condition.
5. **Current tuition and practicum costs** for the programmes named in the vision documents. The trough cannot be sized without them, and fees are not carried forward from a previous year.
6. **Doctoral funding structures** — stipend levels, scholarship eligibility, and whether part-time or employer-supported routes exist in the programme types named in Phase four.

**Not verified because it is not verifiable.** Every figure this document does *not* contain — target income, buffer size, rates, savings rate — is absent deliberately. Those numbers depend on facts about the life being built that no external source holds. They should be worked out once, written down, and reviewed annually. The absence of numbers here is not an oversight; putting invented ones here would have been the error.

**Not financial advice.** §4 and §5 describe structure, not products. Any decision about accounts, investments, or tax treatment belongs with a qualified accountant or fee-only planner familiar with the BC context.
