Career Destination The path Held throughout What it is for Staying ready To confirm

Living document · Revised September 2026

Two registrations, one specialism, six stages.

Registered Clinical Counsellor and Registered Canadian Art Therapist in British Columbia, specialising in disability — working inside a university, holding a small private practice at Therapeutic Studio, and teaching art from a doctorate in contemporary art or art history.

This document exists so that the plan can be held in one hand. Every stage below states what it requires to enter, what I build while I am in it, and the single condition that closes it. Nothing here depends on knowing how long anything will take.

The destination

Five things that turn out to be one life.

The work I want is a counsellor's and an art therapist's work, aimed at disability. Students and adults whose depression is entangled with a body or a mind that institutions were not built for. A university as the daily setting, because that is where those people are and where the accommodation systems that shape their lives are administered. A small private practice alongside it. And an art department to teach in, because the studio is not the thing I do to survive the rest of this — it is the thing the rest of this exists to protect, and one of my mentors has shown that the two can be held in one working week.

Written as five ambitions, this is unmanageable. Written accurately, it is two registrations, one doctorate, and a specialism I already hold — carried the way Grace Wong Sneddon carries hers: a master's in counselling psychology and registration as a clinical counsellor, an interdisciplinary PhD, an adjunct appointment in art history, and a primary career in the university's equity and academic leadership work. Fractional by design. That is the model, and this document is the plan for reaching it here rather than in Victoria.

The clinical registrations

A counselling master's, then registration as a clinical counsellor; then the art therapy diploma, then registration as an art therapist. Not the protected psychologist title — a counsellor's scope, held in two rooms. That is what permits the private practice, the practicum supervision of counselling students, and the clinical standing the accessibility work needs. It is the floor the previous version of this plan described, chosen now as the path.

The doctorate

A PhD in Contemporary Arts or Interactive Arts and Technology at SFU, or in Art History at UBC. The terminal degree an art or design department's teaching stream requires, taken last, on a portfolio the studio-day rhythm builds from now. It replaces the counselling psychology doctorate that used to sit here, and it is chosen for the lectern I actually want to stand at.

The specialism

Disability, and I already hold it. Roughly a hundred mapped contributions across accessibility, universal design, and learning systems. The essential-requirements corpus work. Accommodation law and clinical placement access. This is not something to go and acquire — it is the thing that makes a counsellor, an art therapist, and an art instructor each rare rather than one of many.

What this gives up

The counselling psychology doctorate, the protected title, the psychologist tier at the campus counselling centres, the CPA-accredited internship, and the educational leadership stream in a counselling department. Real losses, named once. The global mental health track stays set aside as before. What is kept in exchange is the studio as a first-class stream rather than a floor, and a doctorate on a question that is mine.

The floor beneath it

If the art doctorate never gets funded or the timing never arrives, a counselling master's, two registrations, and the accessibility role still deliver the university post, the practice, and sessional teaching — including studio and continuing-studies teaching, which an art school hires on practice rather than on a PhD. What would be lost is the adjunct appointment and the faculty stream. Knowing this floor exists is what makes the full version reasonable to attempt.

The path

Six stages, each closing on a condition met.

Keyed to years, a plan produces a permanent low-grade sense of being behind. Keyed to conditions, it asks one answerable question at a time: is this stage finished, and am I ready for the next.

Stage one

Ground

Put the existing work on the record, and get inside a university.

Nothing in this stage requires enrolment, permission, or a change in circumstances. It is composed entirely of work already begun, and it makes every application that follows easier to write.

One item is new, and it is here for a reason beyond the application file. Everything downstream assumes that sitting with one person in acute distress, in real time, is work I want. Make Space is group facilitation; it does not answer that question. A supervised year on a distress line answers it directly, at the cost of one shift a week — which makes it the cheapest possible test of the single assumption the whole path rests on. The sequence documents left that question open until after the counselling master's. It can be answered years earlier, from experience rather than ambition.

Requires

Nothing. This stage is underway.

The work

  • CPACC. Self-paced, against a published body of knowledge. It renders fifteen years of applied accessibility work legible to a hiring panel in a single line.
  • A Visual Communication Design certificate. Part-time, continuing studies — Emily Carr, BCIT, or UBC Extended Learning, whichever is accessible-design literate. It pays immediately in three places: the Groundwork workbooks, accessible document design in the advisor role, and the studio's public face. It also starts a design portfolio, which is the record one of the three doctoral doors reads.
  • Publish the four accessibility resources. All written, none public. Revise the essential-requirements piece first — its scarcity claim predates the AFMC technical standards reform, and that is an hour's work, not a rewrite.
  • Move the role into a British Columbia university. Student services, accessibility services, or learning design at UBC, SFU, or UVic. This places me inside the institution the path ends in, among the people who will later write references, supervise practicum, and sit on hiring committees.
  • Outcome measurement in Make Space. A validated depression screen at the start and end of each cycle. The smallest change with the largest downstream effect: it turns a practice into a programme with a defensible claim.
  • Apply to the Crisis Centre of BC as a Crisis Services Responder. Distress line and text support, under staff supervision. The programme is accredited by the American Association of Suicidology and the Centre states plainly that it is recognised by universities across the province as relevant experience for counselling psychology admission. Enrolment is limited and applying does not guarantee a place, so it goes in early. The commitment is real: roughly 110 hours of basic training plus 32 supervised hours, then 250 credited service hours at a mandatory four hours a week, including overnight and fringe shifts, with skills reviews at three and nine months. A reference letter follows completion.
  • Submit the conceptual paper. No affiliation required, and it changes the tenor of every application and conversation after it.

Complete when

The CPACC is held, the four resources are live, the paper is submitted, one Make Space cohort has pre- and post-data, the working role is inside a post-secondary institution, and Crisis Centre training is complete with shifts underway. The 250 service hours continue into the next stage; the training is what closes this one.

Stage two

The disability master's

The only degree in this path that can be held alongside a salary.

MSc Disability Studies, Rights and Inclusion — University of Leeds, online, part-time over twenty-four months, with intakes in March and September 2027. Leeds has taught disability studies since 1990 and its Centre for Disability Studies was the first university centre in the UK dedicated to the field. The programme is delivered by the School of Sociology and Social Policy and informed by that centre.

Its placement here is structural rather than sentimental. Every later stage requires daytime hours: the counselling master's and the art therapy diploma both have practicum placements, and the doctorate may require full-time study. This is the only window in the whole path where a graduate degree and a full-time salary can be assumed to coexist. Taken later, it cannot be taken at all.

The crisis line runs through this stage. Four hours a week alongside twenty hours of module study is not sustainable at full pace, and it does not have to be: Leeds permits breaks between modules and allows up to four years to complete. Taking fewer modules during the year the 250 service hours are accumulating is a use of the programme's design, not a lapse. The reference letter and the completed hours are in hand well before the counselling master's application is written.

The undergraduate prerequisites for the counselling master's run in the same period. They are evening and online undergraduate courses, and Leeds permits breaks between modules, so the two fit inside one stage rather than consuming two.

Requires

A bachelor's degree with relevant experience — the stated route is a 2:1 in any subject plus relevant experience, or a 2:2 with at least three years of it. Fifteen years of applied accessibility practice clears this comfortably. Applications are individually assessed and may include an interview.

The work

  • Nine taught modules of fifteen credits, eight weeks each, covering disability theory, human rights and the CRPD, policy reform, global challenges, research methods, and change-making practice. Roughly ten hours of guided learning and ten hours of independent study per week while a module is running — the honest weekly cost, and the reason other streams drop to their minimum rhythms during module weeks.
  • The forty-five-credit applied project. It may address a specific challenge in partnership with an organisation — an employer counts — or be a research project making recommendations for policy or professional practice. This is where the essential-requirements corpus study gets written, supervised, credited, and finished — and it is Fieldwork's Investigation and Synthesis phases conducted under supervision and a research ethics board, which is what the public series cannot supply on its own. Doing it inside a programme with a deadline is the difference between a study that exists and one that is always about to begin.
  • The five undergraduate prerequisites, alongside: interviewing skills, counselling theories, learning or cognitive psychology, abnormal psychology, and statistics. Career counselling was removed from the requirement for the 2026 admission cycle onward. Each is checked against the department's published equivalency table before enrolling, since equivalency is no longer adjudicated in advance, and the earlier UBC coursework is inventoried first so nothing is repeated.
  • A Global Mental Health certificate. A certificate, not a master's — Harvard's Trauma and Recovery programme, or a King's or LSHTM short course — taken inside a Leeds module break, with one stated purpose: the task-shifting and treatment-gap evidence that the manualised Make Space training system is built on. The global mental health master's stays set aside; this is the part of that track that serves a closing condition, scoped to the size that fits.
  • The supervisor conversation. Admission to the research-thesis counselling master's depends on supervisory expertise being available in the declared research area, and disability is named among the department's core research foci. A completed disability studies MSc and a supervised applied project are precisely what make that conversation straightforward.

Fees are £12,750 in total and payable module by module — around £1,170 per taught module and £2,220 for the project. That structure matters more than the number: it turns a degree into a series of affordable decisions rather than one large one.

Complete when

The applied project is submitted, all five prerequisites are held with transcripts in hand, the counselling master's has been chosen on the basis of which programme the salary can survive, and — if it is the MA — at least one supervisor whose work touches disability has agreed to a conversation.

Stage three

The counselling master's

Clinical formation, and the registration that opens the room.

The MA or the MEd — and this is a change. When a counselling psychology doctorate sat downstream, the MA's thesis was what that application would be read for. It no longer is. Both degrees carry the same counselling core and the same route to registration as a clinical counsellor, and both satisfy the art therapy institute and the art doctorate that follow. The rule is now: choose the programme that lets the salary and the degree coexist, and take the thesis only if it does not cost a year.

UBC or SFU first, because a staff member studying at their own institution is the cheapest version of this stage there is — tuition benefit, flexible hours, and a practicum negotiable around a four-day week. If a full-time role and the programme cannot be held together at either, two alternates are already named: Adler University's Master of Counselling Psychology in Vancouver, built for working students and carrying an art therapy specialisation that could fold Stage four into this one; and Athabasca's online Master of Counselling. Neither is second-best. Both are checked before the UBC and SFU answer is assumed.

Requires

The five prerequisites, a B+ senior average, a criminal record clearance before the supervised clinical placement, and — for the thesis route — an available supervisor in the declared research area. The published sources disagree on required experience: the university calendar names three years of helping-relationship work, the department page states no minimum. Make Space and the distress line have been that experience for years either way.

The work

  • Forty-eight credits of coursework, with a six-credit thesis if the MA is chosen. Recent graduates average three years.
  • If there is a thesis, it treats the disability and depression intersection — the same question the whole path is organised around — and it doubles as the first research document an art doctorate committee will read.
  • Practicum placements requested in post-secondary counselling and disability services, so the clinical hours accrue in the setting the career stays in.
  • Registration as a clinical counsellor applied for on conferral. This is the registration the practice opens under.

One scheduling fact belongs in the plan rather than in the surprise: the supervised clinical course runs in the second year and requires one day a week away from work. It is planned for at the start of the stage, alongside the financial arrangement that carries it.

Complete when

The degree is conferred, the practicum hours are complete, and the RCC application is submitted.

Stage four

Art therapy

The BFA brought into clinical service, and the practice opened.

The Advanced Diploma in Art Therapy at the Vancouver Art Therapy Institute, distance stream: eighteen months from a July start, applications by 30 March, a 600-hour clinical placement, and a creative project as the culmination. The Institute now requires a completed master's in counselling or a related field, which is why this stage sits here and not earlier. It leads to registration as a Registered Canadian Art Therapist with the Canadian Art Therapy Association.

In the previous version of this plan this credential was optional and twenty years away, to be taken up "if it still calls." It is calling now, and the reason is not sentimental: a clinician whose other room is a studio, working with people whose distress is entangled with a body or a mind the institution was not built for, should be qualified to work through image and material as well as through words. This is the stage in which Therapeutic Studio opens as a practice.

Requires

The counselling master's conferred, work-related experience demonstrated, and an application in by 30 March of the year of entry. The placement needs daytime hours again, so the arrangement made for Stage three either continues or is renewed.

The work

  • The diploma coursework, blended online and in short residential intensives in Vancouver.
  • A 600-hour supervised clinical placement — requested, where possible, in post-secondary or community mental health with a disability population.
  • The creative project, designed so that it can also serve as the first piece of the doctoral portfolio.
  • Therapeutic Studio opened as a private practice at RCC scope, deliberately small, with art therapy added on registration.

Complete when

The diploma is held, the RCAT registration is granted, and the first clients have been seen at Therapeutic Studio.

Stage five

The doctorate

The terminal degree for the art lectern, on the studio's own terms.

Three programmes, and they differ in what the application reads and what is taught afterwards. SFU's PhD in Contemporary Arts reads the studio: research-intensive, scholarly or practice-based, twelve to fifteen semesters, a supervisor matched on admission, founded to give practising artists a doctoral credential where the MFA had been the ceiling. UBC's PhD in Art History reads the writing, in the department that also houses visual art, and likely wants an art history master's at entry. SFU's PhD in Interactive Arts and Technology admits on academic preparation, fit with a research faculty member, and a supervisory agreement made before applying — roughly one applicant in ten is offered a place — and is the one door where the accessibility specialism is the thesis rather than the frame — accessible learning systems, or Make Space as an interactive method. The choice is not made now. It is made at the close of Stage four, by whichever record — studio or design — is stronger by then, which is why both are kept from Stage one. The counselling psychology doctorate that used to occupy this stage is no longer on the plan; what it opened — the protected title, the psychologist tier, the CPA internship — is named plainly as given up in the destination above.

Requires

The master's, a supervisor who wants the work, a catalogued and shown body of studio work, a publication record — and funding, a condition of equal standing to the other four. Applications to SFU open on 1 October for the following September; UBC's entry requirement for a candidate without an art history MA is unverified and must be checked before this stage is counted on.

The work

  • At SFU: a minimum of thirty-five units of coursework, qualifying examinations, a thesis prospectus, and a practice-based thesis culminating in a major original artwork or public presentation alongside the written component.
  • The question is Dear Ordinary's — the ordinary body, grief, and the sacred in everyday life — held inside the scholarly frame the rest of the path built: disability, creative health, and what making does for a mind that is flattening.
  • Teaching assistantships from the first year, because they are both the funding and the teaching record the adjunct appointment is later assessed on.

Whether the programme can be held alongside a university role, or requires the salary to stop, is the first thing to establish. Every financial arrangement in this stage follows from the answer.

Complete when

The thesis is defended and the culminating work has been shown.

Stage six

The portfolio

Four seats in one university, held fractionally.

This is the shape my mentor holds and the reason her profile is linked from the destination: a primary university career, a clinical registration and a practice, an adjunct appointment in an art department, and public service — none of them full-time, all of them real. Nothing here requires a new post to be created. It requires four existing kinds of post to be held by one person.

Requires

The doctorate, both registrations, and a decade already spent inside the sector.

The work

  • The accessibility role. Still the salaried spine — by now senior, and the seat from which essential requirements get rewritten.
  • Therapeutic Studio. A small private practice at RCC and RCAT scope: depression, disability, and image. Sized by desire rather than need.
  • The lectern. Sessional, then adjunct, at SFU's School for the Contemporary Arts, SFU's School of Interactive Arts and Technology, or UBC's Art History, Visual Art and Theory — studio, design, or seminar teaching, and supervision of graduate students whose work sits where mine does.
  • The studio. Not held at a floor. Running as a practice, shown and collected, with the teaching module the studio-day site has waiting for 2028.

Complete when

It does not close. This is the practice.

Held throughout

Four practices that run at every stage.

These are not additions to the plan. They are the plan's raw material, and each one becomes something else by being kept. What changes across the stages is only their volume — each has a minimum rhythm that is defended and a maximum that is refused.

Make Space

A weekly community mental health programme in creative health, with original conceptual tools. Once the screening instrument is in place, the remaining discipline is documentation: a brief reflective note after each session becomes field notes, and three years of them become a qualitative paper that needs no doctoral affiliation. Minimum rhythm: one session weekly.

The public writing

Practitioner-facing work on accessibility, disability, and learning design — and, since April 2026, a research method conducted in public. Fieldwork, published through Accessible Learning Labs, is a 24-part inquiry into research methods for inclusive learning design, built on one working hypothesis: a learning environment needs accessibility for the floor, disability justice for whose knowledge shapes it, and emergent learning to keep it able to change; where policy and practice diverge, one of the three is missing. It is the method the Leeds applied project, the corpus study, and the doctoral research statement all rest on, and it is the reason the Accessible Learning Labs landscape reaches a depth the primary role does not. Minimum rhythm: one substantial piece monthly.

The consulting practice

Priced as a specialism rather than a service: the scarcity is the intersection of disability studies, accommodation law, essential requirements, and learning design, not any one of them. It holds existing clients during coursework and does not grow. It is the flexible income that carries the trough stages.

The studio

Dear Ordinary and the studies, on the standing order set out at the studio-day site: Sunday ten until six for making, Monday evening for documentation and correspondence, twenty minutes of drawing on the days between. It is the embodied knowing the analytical work cannot generate alone — and, from this revision, it is also the portfolio a practice-based doctorate is admitted on, which is why its floor has risen from one piece a quarter to one kept Sunday a week. Nothing here is protected in order to be expanded later. It is expanding now.

What it is for

The specific gap this closes.

Disabled students report depression at higher rates than their non-disabled peers, and then meet a support structure that treats access and mental health as two separate offices with two separate intake forms. The counselling centre addresses the distress. The accessibility office addresses the accommodation. Very few people sit in either room able to see that a student's flattening mood and their unmet access need are the same problem viewed from two angles.

That is the gap. It is not a gap in compassion or in funding — it is a gap in combined expertise, and it is unusually narrow. A registered clinical counsellor and art therapist who can hold a clinical hour, and also read a programme's essential requirements document and say precisely why it is indefensible, is close to unique in this province. The credential that used to sit in this sentence was a psychologist's. The gap does not care which registration holds the hour; it cares that the same person holds the hour and the document.

The reach is wider than a caseload. A student helped in the counselling room is one person. A professional programme's technical standards rewritten from method to construct changes who is able to enter that profession at all, for every cohort that follows. The same expertise does both, and the second is only available to someone the institution already recognises as a clinician.

The art stream is not a reward attached to this. It is the third place the same gap appears. Art schools admit and assess on essential requirements too — studio hours, physical making, critique in a room — and their students carry the same entanglement of mood and access as everyone else's, in a setting where the work itself is the body. A doctorate in contemporary art or art history, taught from, is how one person gets to stand at the front of that room with a clinical registration, a disability specialism, and a practice of their own. That is the seat this plan is built for, and it is the seat one of my mentors has held for years.

This is also the argument for the research. The corpus study, the master's thesis, and the practice-based dissertation are not academic exercises appended to a clinical career; they are the instruments by which one person's practice becomes something other institutions can adopt without me in the room.

Staying ready

How to spend attention while waiting.

The risk in a long path is not failing an examination. It is arriving at each stage having spent the interval on the wrong things, and treating the intervals themselves as dead time to be endured. Four working rules.

  • Prepare one stage ahead, never three. While in Ground, the only forward-looking tasks are the prerequisite inventory, the Leeds application, and finding out whether a UBC or SFU salary and a counselling master's can coexist. While at Leeds, it is the master's application. Anything further out — the art therapy diploma, the doctorate — is speculation, and holding it costs attention without producing readiness.
  • Nothing on this path is a hoop. Abnormal psychology, learning theory, and statistics are not obstacles placed before the interesting part — they are the interesting part, arriving early. Statistics in particular is what makes it possible to read a depression trial and judge it, which is the literacy the whole plan rests on. Studied as content rather than as a requirement, these courses are the first months of the work itself.
  • Let each stage produce something citable — and, now, something visible. A published resource, a submitted paper, a dataset, a thesis; and alongside them a catalogued body of studio work, shown quarterly. A practice-based doctorate reads the portfolio the way a counselling doctorate read the thesis. By the point of doctoral application there should already be several things in the world carrying my name — which matters enormously during the stretches when no institution has yet said anything encouraging.
  • Keep a decision log rather than a diary. One file, one line per decision, dated: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why. It becomes the methods section later, it prevents reconstructing reasoning after the fact, and on the days the work feels like nothing it is evidence that it is not.

One review, twice a year, with one question: is this stage finished, and is the sequence still right. Between reviews the plan is not reopened. That is what makes it possible to put the whole of a day's attention into the day's actual work.

To confirm

What is set by others, and what I have not yet verified.

Four things carry external dates. Everything else moves at whatever pace the work and the life allow. The verification items below are ordered by what it would cost to be wrong about them, and each is checked with the source institution rather than a secondary summary. The September 2026 revision added a second block: the art therapy and art doctorate items are new, and none of them has been checked at source yet.

Fixed date

31 December. Prerequisite courses must be complete by this date in the year of application to UBC's counselling psychology master's. Every enrolment decision in Stage two anchors here. Confirm whether SFU's programme carries the same anchor.

Fixed date

March and September 2027. The two Leeds intakes. Missing one means the next; the programme allows up to four years to complete once started.

Fixed date

29 November 2027. Psychotherapy becomes a regulated profession in British Columbia under the College of Health and Care Professionals. What is being regulated is the title. Qualifying-programme standards are still being set, and they bear directly on the master's choice — and, now, on whether an RCC and an RCAT are the registrations the plan ends on or an interim pair.

Fixed date

30 March, each year. The Vancouver Art Therapy Institute application deadline, for a July start. The Advanced Diploma runs eighteen months from that July. Stage four's entry is set by this date and by the master's being conferred before it.

Fixed date

1 October. SFU's PhD in Contemporary Arts opens applications on this date for the following September. Stage five's entry is set by whichever October falls after the art therapy diploma is in hand.

What Therapeutic Studio is. The property architecture had it as the peer-to-peer method, deliberately not clinical service, and warned that the two cannot share a name. The plan now puts the private practice at therapeuticstudio.ca. Either Make Space moves under another name, or the practice does. Decide once, before the first client and before the next public piece about Make Space.

Decide

March or September 2027 for the Leeds intake. The rule: whichever start allows Stage one to be genuinely finished first, which now includes having crisis line shifts established rather than just beginning. On current shape that points to September.

Crisis Centre application deadlines and training cohort dates, and the cost of basic training, which volunteers pay themselves. Cohorts are limited and intake windows are fixed.

If declined

Comparable routes, in order of fit. The Crisis Centre for Northern BC accepts remote responders, trains in roughly 70 hours at no cost, includes ASIST certification, and asks one four-hour shift a week for a year. Crisis Text Line is remote and asks 200 hours at four a week. Either produces supervised distress work and a reference.

Verify first

The Leeds fee for 2027 entry, and whether module-by-module payment is available for a self-funded international student. The published total applies to earlier starts and will not carry forward.

Source before use

The prevalence claim in What it is for. It is consistent with the literature but is stated here from memory, and it should carry a citation before it appears in any application, paper, or public piece.

The work has been underway for fifteen years. What remains is a sequence of stages, each with a known entrance and a single closing condition — and the pace at which they arrive changes very little about what this eventually amounts to.
