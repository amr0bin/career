# The bodies of knowledge

> **Purpose:** The ten foundations the whole arc draws on: why each matters, which stage it feeds, the test that says it is held, the anchor texts, and the gap still open. · **Governs:** what is read and cited, and how the overview PDFs are used (as maps, never as citations). · **Carried out in Notion:** {{notion:Reading}}

## The ceiling this list serves

The rarest capacity in this field is holding all of it at once: being in the room with someone who is depressed while thinking about the population evidence, the training system that makes the work repeatable, the disability-justice question of who is left out, and the research question that makes the knowledge travel. Very few people hold accommodation law, construct validity, clinical training and programme design together. The arc is built so that each stage adds one layer. The thesis question needs all of them: **does the accommodation process itself drive depression in disabled students?**

Each foundation has a **test**. A foundation is held when its test can be passed, not when a book is ticked off.

## Two lists, one set

Two lists existed:

- **education-path/knowledge.js** (ten areas, each with primary anchors, a test and a gap). This is the primary list and keeps its numbering, k1–k10.
- **"The 10 core foundations"** (a Project file), which drew ten foundations out of the project documents. It has a matching set of topic PDFs.

All the topic PDFs are machine-generated overviews from the Stanford STORM tool. Each one carries the tool's own warning that it "can make mistakes". They are useful as maps and should not be cited, which is how knowledge.js already treats its own overview articles. Where a PDF adds something real, the addition is folded into the matching foundation below and marked *added*.

## k1 · Disability studies and disability justice

**Why it matters to the arc.** It is the frame that the four-literature paper, the corpus study and the Leeds essays argue from: impairment and disability, disablism and ableism, and the social model's own critics. *Added:* disability justice (intersectionality, interdependence, collective access) asks who current depression care leaves out, which is the thesis question seen from the outside.

**Stage fed.** Stage 1. Phase 1 *Ground* reading, deepened across Leeds in Phase 2 *Frame* (2027–29). Leeds OSSP5101M Understanding Disability, the corpus study's theoretical framing, and an applied-project partnership with a disabled people's organisation.

**Self-test.** Explain in one page why a technical standard is a disablist artefact under the social model, and why Shakespeare would call that account incomplete.

**Anchors.** Oliver, *The Politics of Disablement* (1990). Barnes & Mercer (eds.), *Implementing the Social Model of Disability* (2004). Shakespeare, *Disability Rights and Wrongs Revisited* (2014). Kafer, *Feminist, Queer, Crip* (2013). Goodley, *Disability Studies* 2nd ed. (2017). Dolmage, *Academic Ableism* (2017). Lawson & Beckett, the complementarity thesis (2021). *Added:* Sins Invalid, *Skin, Tooth, and Bone* (2nd ed., 2019) and Piepzna-Samarasinha, *Care Work* (2018).

**The gap.** The Canadian voices that the Leeds frame lacks: the Canadian Disability Studies Association literature, and Indigenous disability scholarship through BCANDS. The two disability-justice PDFs are US-centred (ADA, the Capitol Crawl) and do not fill this gap.

## k2 · Accommodation law as applied practice in BC

**Why it matters to the arc.** This is most days in the advisor role: the duty to accommodate, undue hardship, the interactive process and documentation, all read against the BC Human Rights Code. It is also the legal leg of the essential-requirements argument (the bona fide requirement test).

**Stage fed.** Stage 1, from now, and daily in the advisor role. It feeds the advisor interview, casework, and accessibility-committee service.

**Self-test.** Given a denied accommodation, write the three-step *Meiorin* analysis from memory, including the evidentiary standard for undue hardship.

**Anchors.** *BCGSEU (Meiorin)*, [1999] 3 SCR 3. *Moore v. British Columbia (Education)*, 2012 SCC 61. BC *Human Rights Code*, RSBC 1996, c. 210. *Accessible British Columbia Act*, SBC 2021, c. 19. OHRC, *Policy on accessible education for students with disabilities* (2018), read comparatively. BC Human Rights Tribunal decisions on post-secondary accommodation, read regularly.

**The gap.** BC's own guidance, rather than Ontario's: the Tribunal's decisions, the Office of the Human Rights Commissioner, and the Accessible BC Act regulations as they are made.

## k3 · Validity theory and educational measurement

**Why it matters to the arc.** A requirement written from method rather than construct is a validity failure. Messick, Kane and the *Standards* give the vocabulary to say this to a professional college in its own terms. It drives code 1 of the corpus study, and it is one of the four literatures in the framework paper.

**Stage fed.** Stage 1, now through Leeds. Later it feeds standards-committee work in Stage 3.

**Self-test.** Rewrite one programme's technical standard from a method ("must be able to lift 20 kg") to its construct ("must ensure safe patient transfer"), and name the construct-irrelevant variance the original introduced.

**Anchors.** AERA/APA/NCME, *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* (2014). Messick, "Validity" (1989) and "Validity of psychological assessment" (1995). Kane, "Validating the interpretations and uses of test scores" (2013). Meeks, Jain & Laird (eds.), *Equal Access for Students with Disabilities* 2nd ed. (2020).

**The gap.** Nobody has yet joined validity theory to essential requirements in print, so this synthesis has to be written. That is the four-literature paper (see `research-designs.md`).

## k4 · Depression: psychopathology, evidence and context

**Why it matters to the arc.** Both counselling programmes and the PhD assume it. It is also what makes the Make Space outcome data interpretable: classification, the treatments with the strongest evidence, and measurement-based care. *Added from the PDF list:* depression across cultural and social contexts, social determinants, and how depression intersects with disability.

**Stage fed.** Stage 1 Phase 2 (the abnormal-psychology prerequisite). Then Stage 2 (MA thesis measures) and Stage 3 (PhD coursework).

**Self-test.** Read a depression trial, state its effect size, say whether the comparator was active, and explain what a change on the PHQ-9 means clinically.

**Anchors.** DSM-5-TR (2022). ICD-11 CDDR (2024). Kroenke, Spitzer & Williams, the PHQ-9 (2001). Cuijpers et al., the psychotherapies network meta-analysis (2021). Martell, Dimidjian & Herman-Dunn, *Behavioral Activation for Depression* 2nd ed. (2022). Kuyken et al., MBCT relapse IPD meta-analysis (2016). The current Cochrane reviews of psychological therapies for depression.

**The gap.** The literature on implementing measurement-based care, and the depression-in-disability literature that the general reviews leave out. The "across contexts" PDF ends in Wikipedia-maintenance boilerplate and has no effect sizes.

## k5 · Counselling psychology, the room, ethics and regulation

**Why it matters to the arc.** It covers common factors and the alliance, the Canadian definition of counselling psychology, social-justice competencies, and the regulatory landscape. *Added from the PDF list:* **therapeutic group facilitation** as a clinical craft: holding a group safely, with boundaries, and with therapeutic effect, which is different from community facilitation. This is the craft beneath Make Space and the future clinical groups.

**Stage fed.** Stage 1 Phase 2, before the admissions interview (application 1 Dec 2030). Stage 2: the MA ethics course (UBC CNPS 586, SFU EDUC 872) and RCC/CCC. Stage 3: psychologist registration (~2041).

**Self-test.** Explain why the alliance predicts outcome across modalities. Name the body that regulates each title (RCC, CCC, Psychologist) and say what BC's regulatory change does to the first two. *Added:* name the therapeutic factors in a group, and say what a facilitator does when one member's distress begins to set the room's agenda.

**Anchors.** Wampold & Imel, *The Great Psychotherapy Debate* 2nd ed. (2015). Norcross & Lambert (eds.), *Psychotherapy Relationships That Work* 3rd ed. (2019). Ratts et al., MSJCC (2016). CCPA *Code of Ethics* (2020). BCACC *Code of Ethical Conduct* (current). CPA *Canadian Code of Ethics for Psychologists* 4th ed. (2017). CHCPBC Psychologists' Code of Conduct (2024). *Added:* Yalom & Leszcz, *The Theory and Practice of Group Psychotherapy* 6th ed. (2020).

**The gap.** The BC counsellor landscape: the BCACC and CCPA codes, and how counsellor regulation changes under the Health Professions and Occupations Act. The group-facilitation PDF names Yalom and Tuckman only in passing.

## k6 · Global mental health, task-shifting, knowledge translation and implementation science

**Why it matters to the arc.** This is the substance of the GMH track, and it is the frame that turns Make Space from a practice into a programme: the treatment gap, the task-shifting trials, and the frameworks that carry a pilot to scale. *Added from the PDF list:* **knowledge translation** (closing the evidence-to-practice gap) and **systems thinking**, including learning design as infrastructure. The core-foundations list calls task-shifting "the systems-level model the whole career aims to extend through creative approaches". The full track is in `global-mental-health.md`.

**Stage fed.** Stage 1: reading now, then GHM202 in Phase 2. Stage 2: the manualised Make Space facilitator curriculum. Stage 3: Creative Health peer programme and leadership.

**Self-test.** Place Make Space on the IASC intervention pyramid, name its Proctor outcomes, and describe what a hybrid type-2 trial of it would look like. *Added:* walk Make Space through the Knowledge-to-Action cycle and name the step where it currently stalls.

**Anchors.** Patel & Hanlon, *Where There Is No Psychiatrist* 2nd ed. (2018). The Lancet Commission on global mental health (2018). WHO mhGAP-IG v2.0 (2016). Chibanda et al., Friendship Bench (2016). Patel et al., MANAS (2010). Damschroder et al., CFIR (2009). Proctor et al., implementation outcomes (2011). Glasgow et al., RE-AIM (1999). Curran et al., hybrid designs (2012). *Added:* Graham et al., "Lost in knowledge translation: time for a map?" (2006), the KTA framework.

**The gap.** The Canadian stepped-care literature (Stepped Care 2.0) and the cost-effectiveness comparisons of scalable interventions. The KT PDF is the only one with Canadian content (KTT, the K2A framework, the 17-year evidence-to-practice gap). The systems-thinking PDF is built on Philippine health devolution and does not transfer.

## k7 · Research methods, epidemiology and open science

**Why it matters to the arc.** The labs worth joining are defined by method, so methods are how fit is signalled. The PhD comprehensive exam is a systematic review or meta-analysis. *Added from the PDF list:* **epidemiology and research literacy** as the base layer, meaning study design, measures of association, bias, confounding and causation. The core-foundations list describes this as "the difference between engaging with evidence and simply deferring to it."

**Stage fed.** Stage 1: statistics prerequisite, TCPS 2 CORE, corpus-study preregistration, Leeds research methods. Stage 2: MA thesis (mixed methods). Stage 3: PhD comprehensive exam.

**Self-test.** Write a preregistration with hypotheses, a frozen rubric and an analysis plan. Defend the choice between reflexive thematic analysis and IPA for an interview study.

**Anchors.** Field, *Discovering Statistics Using IBM SPSS* 5th ed. (2018). Braun & Clarke, *Thematic Analysis* (2022). Smith, Flowers & Larkin, *IPA* 2nd ed. (2022). Creswell & Plano Clark, *Mixed Methods Research* 3rd ed. (2018). Nosek et al., TOP guidelines (2015). Page et al., PRISMA 2020. Borenstein et al., *Introduction to Meta-Analysis* 2nd ed. (2021). TCPS 2 (2022). *Added:* Rothman, *Epidemiology: An Introduction* 2nd ed. (2012). PRISMA-ScR, for the scoping review.

**The gap.** None beyond the anchors. The courses do the teaching here.

## k8 · Learning design: instructional systems design, the learning sciences, UDL

**Why it matters to the arc.** It lets the learning-design work of over the past decade count as programme-development expertise on a counselling-centre posting. It also keeps the Groundwork workbooks and the facilitator manual honest about what transfers. *Added from the PDF list:* **instructional systems design** (needs analysis, measurable objectives, sequenced practice and evaluation; ADDIE and its successors). The core-foundations list calls it the foundation "that makes everything else deployable and scalable".

**Stage fed.** Held at practitioner level now, with its scholarly basis named now. It feeds the advisor application (programme development), the learning or cognitive psychology prerequisite, the workbooks, and the facilitator curriculum.

**Self-test.** State what the evidence does and does not show for UDL, and design a training module that respects cognitive load while remaining accessible.

**Anchors.** CAST, *UDL Guidelines* 3.0 (2024). Meyer, Rose & Gordon, *Universal Design for Learning* (2014). Sweller, Ayres & Kalyuga, *Cognitive Load Theory* (2011). Mayer, *Multimedia Learning* 3rd ed. (2020). Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006). *Added:* Burgstahler (ed.), *Universal Design in Higher Education* 2nd ed. (2015). Dick, Carey & Carey, *The Systematic Design of Instruction* (current edition).

**The gap.** The Canadian accessibility-in-higher-education literature (NEADS, CACUSS) that connects UDL to the duty to accommodate. The UDL and accessibility PDFs are framed around the ADA, the EAA and WCAG, with no Canadian post-secondary examples.

## k9 · Student mental health and student services in higher education

**Why it matters to the arc.** It covers prevalence, help-seeking and campus models. This is the literature the thesis joins, the source of the citation the opening claim needs, and the vocabulary of the target postings. The governing question of the scoping review lives here.

**Stage fed.** Stage 1 through Stage 2. It is where the MA thesis lives, and it carries into the joined access-and-mental-health service in Stage 3.

**Self-test.** Cite the Canadian prevalence figure for depression among post-secondary students with and without disabilities, and sketch BC's stepped-care model.

**Anchors.** Lipson, Lattie & Eisenberg (2019). Healthy Minds Study data reports. ACHA NCHA III Canadian Reference Group. CSA Z2003:20. The Okanagan Charter (2015). Cornish et al., Stepped Care 2.0 (2017). AHEAD program standards.

**The gap.** The disability-specific depression prevalence claim still needs its own citation before it appears anywhere. The NCHA Canadian data by disability status is where to look. (A scale figure is already sourced: in the 2025 CUCS survey, 33% of first-year students reported a disability. See `10-stability-and-the-ceiling.md`.)

## k10 · Arts, creative health and research-creation

**Why it matters to the arc.** It keeps Make Space, the studio and the Creative Health peer programme from reading as personal projects: the evidence for arts engagement and mental health, and its limits. It is the bridge to art therapy (VATI) in Stage 2.

**Stage fed.** From now, through Make Space outcome measurement. Stage 2 Phase 4: VATI Advanced Diploma. Stage 3: Creative Health peer programme in Therapeutic Studio.

**Self-test.** State the strength of the arts-and-depression evidence without overclaiming, name its proposed mechanisms, and write a research-creation proposal in SSHRC's terms.

**Anchors.** Fancourt & Finn, WHO HEN synthesis 67 (2019). Fancourt, *Arts in Health* (2017). Fancourt, Warran & Aughterson, DCMS evidence summary (2020). SSHRC, research-creation definition. Chapman & Sawchuk (2012). Bungay & Clift, arts on prescription (2010).

**The gap.** Mechanism evidence. Fancourt establishes association more than mechanism, and the RCT base is thinner than for CBT or behavioural activation. That unsettled evidence is the opening for a registered clinician who runs a programme with outcome data. Research-creation is now a supporting literature rather than an admissions requirement (see *Resolved*).

## How the ten are used

- **Anchors are cited. Overview PDFs are not.** Several overviews are thin, single-sourced, or written for another jurisdiction.
- **One test per area** is the measure of holding it. A reading list is not.
- The three areas missing from the PDF list (k2, k3, k9) are where the specialism lives. Weight study there first.

**Fallback, once:** if a foundation cannot be reached through study at the pace planned, the matching degree stage teaches it formally (k4 and k5 in the MA, k7 in Leeds and the PhD). Starting early is about speed and confidence. Nothing in the arc depends on teaching it alone.
