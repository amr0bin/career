# Research designs

> **Purpose:** The research line: four pieces of work, what each one is, how each is designed, and how they sequence into one doctoral question. · **Governs:** research design choices, the order of the papers, and which container (independent, Leeds, MA, PhD) holds each study. · **Carried out in Notion:** [Gates](https://app.notion.com/p/d4969021f8da4529a41eafec0c323ca4)

## The ceiling

**The question:** *does the accommodation process itself drive depression in disabled students, and which parts of it are the levers?* The parts in view are documentation burden, wait times, denials, interim measures and unmet need.

It is the highest-leverage question available. Universities control the levers, and changing them reaches every disabled student at once. No local researcher holds the question. Very few people hold the accommodation expertise and the clinical training together. At full height, the line runs from an independent framework paper to a preregistered corpus study, then a supervised Leeds study, an empirical MA thesis, a PhD testing the intervention the thesis points to, and then tri-council grants, doctoral students, and the evidence base for a joined access-and-mental-health service.

Each piece is designed to be **the input to the next one**.

## The four pieces

### 1 · The four-literature framework paper (comes first)

**The argument, in one line:** a defensible essential requirement has to survive four tests: **construct validity** (does it measure the capability, or one bodily route to it?), the **duty to accommodate** (is it bona fide under *Meiorin*?), **disability studies** (*whose body* is the default?), and **programme design** (was it designed, reviewed, and revisable?). No institutional role is answerable for more than two of these. That is why requirements that fail go unexamined.

- **Why it comes first.** The reasoning of 7 September 2026: the corpus study's seven codes trace to these four literatures, so the framework has to be in print before the data is.
- **Venue.** *Canadian Journal of Disability Studies*: open access, no fee, welcomes independent scholars. Under 6,000 words and under 40 references. Always post a preprint as well (EdArXiv or SSRN). Verify author guidelines before writing to length.
- **Before claiming originality.** Read the UK competence-standards provision under the Equality Act 2010 (also CPACC week 10). If the framework already exists there, the claim becomes translation rather than origination. That is still worth making, and it has to be known.
- **Container.** Independent. No affiliation and no ethics review needed, because it is a conceptual paper.
- **Knowledge it rests on:** k1, k2, k3, k8 (`bodies-of-knowledge.md`).

### 2 · The essential-requirements corpus study (the flagship)

**Question.** Do Canadian professional programmes state their essential requirements as the *constructs* a standard measures, or as particular *bodily methods* of performing them? And what does the difference imply for who can enter the profession? Underneath that, a second question: what authority do these documents claim, and has anyone ever been asked to defend them?

**Corpus.** 40 to 60 published essential-requirements and technical-standards documents. Three to five professions are covered in depth rather than ten thinly. Provinces are stratified so that none dominates. English only, and stated as such.

**The seven codes**

| # | Code | What it asks of every requirement | Literature |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Construct or method | Does it state the capability, or one bodily way of performing it? The construct/method ratio is the headline finding | Construct validity |
| 2 | Locus of authority | Does it say where the requirement comes from (regulator, accreditor, or nothing)? | Duty to accommodate |
| 3 | Accommodation framing | Absent, available, limited, or framed as evidence of unsuitability. Does applicant self-assessment turn the document into a disclosure event? | Law; disclosure burden |
| 4 | Bodily specification | How many requirements are phrased in sensory, motor or stature terms? | Disability studies |
| 5 | Temporal assumptions | Continuous availability, shift length, attendance minima, fixed progression | Episodic disability |
| 6 | Revisability | Last reviewed when, by whom, through what process? | Programme design |
| 7 | Voice | Any evidence that disabled practitioners or disability-led organisations were consulted? | Disability justice |

A complementary rubric (from `my-vision`) codes **mechanism proximity** (remote, adjacent, constitutive). The prediction worth registering is that requirements closest to the mechanism are the least likely to carry a justification. If that holds, it is the paper. If it inverts, preregistration makes saying so credible.

**The eight stages** (stages, not dates; the order holds even if durations change)

1. **Sampling frame.** Professions, provinces, institution types; 40 to 60 documents. Recruit the second coder in principle.
2. **Collect and capture the first twenty.** Save a PDF of each, and submit each URL to the Internet Archive the same day. Admissions pages change silently.
3. **Pilot-code twenty, revise, and write the coding manual.** Expect code 1 to need subcategories and code 3 a fifth value.
4. **Preregister on OSF.** Question, sample, codes, analysis plan. This is the deadline an institution would otherwise supply, and it is what the Leeds application can honestly point to.
5. **Collect and code the rest.**
6. **Second coder.** A ten-document subsample coded independently, with agreement computed and reported.
7. **Analyse, draft, deposit.** Dataset and coding manual on OSF, preprint on EdArXiv, submission to the *Journal of Postsecondary Education and Disability* (AHEAD).
8. **One conference abstract.** CDSA or AHEAD.

**Ethics: none needed, with the reasoning stated.** TCPS 2 (2022), **Article 2.2**, exempts research that relies only on publicly available information where there is no reasonable expectation of privacy. Published admissions documents fit that exactly. No board certifies the exemption in advance for an unaffiliated researcher, so the methods section states the reasoning and claims no approval. **The moment interviews are added, the study needs an institution.** Leeds supplies that.

**The second coder is a co-author.** The second coder is paid and offered co-authorship, ideally a disabled practitioner. This raises the paper's standing at low cost and puts a disabled practitioner inside the analysis, in keeping with the Leeds tradition that disability research should answer to disabled people. Institutions are reported in aggregate, with unattributed quotations, so a programme can later invite the work in.

**How it could be wrong.** The knowledge may already exist unpublished in advisors' practice (ask three experienced advisors). The UK may have got there first. No competition may simply mean no market. A scholarly rather than commercial contribution is a fine answer, and it should be known.

**Container.** Stages 1 to 4 are independent (Phase 1 *Ground*). Stages 5 to 8 run alongside the first Leeds year. The corpus's sampling frame becomes the Leeds Applied Project's recruitment frame.

### 3 · The scoping review

**Question** (the practice's governing question): *who does campus stepped care for depression leave out, disabled students first among them, and what low-intensity, accessible provision would reach them without displacing the licensed room?*

- **Design.** A registered scoping review: protocol on OSF, then screening, extraction, synthesis, preprint, journal, conference. Reported to PRISMA-ScR (k7).
- **Why it matters.** It is the research experience the counselling MA names as its differentiator. It is an authored, citable object that almost no master's applicant brings. It also tests the neglectedness claim properly: the case for this path should be re-run when the review reports.
- **Pace.** Six stages over about four years. One stage a year is behind pace and two is on pace. During Leeds it moves in module breaks, not weekly.
- **Container.** Independent, and needs no ethics review because it uses published literature. Target: preprint before the 1 Dec 2030 application.

### 4 · The Make Space paper, and its affiliation problem

Make Space (the community creative-health practice) carries years of facilitation. Session-level reflective notes and, from Phase 1, a validated depression screen (PHQ-9) at the start and end of each cycle turn it from a practice into a programme with a defensible claim. The intended output is a qualitative paper: reflexive thematic analysis or IPA of the programme experience.

**The problem is affiliation, not only ethics.** TCPS 2 CORE teaches the Canadian standard but supplies no Research Ethics Board. Journals will ask which REB approved the study. Retrospective approval for data already collected is difficult everywhere and effectively unavailable without an institution.

**Three real exits. Choose one before a word is drafted, because the choice changes the design, the consent language and the target journal:**

1. **Carry it into Leeds**, under the university's ethics approval as part of the applied work. This is the safest exit, and later than first planned.
2. **Reframe the genre** as practice reflection, programme evaluation or autoethnography. These sit outside human-subjects review but land in different journals with different conventions.
3. **Pay an independent REB.** This is expensive, and awkward for unfunded community work.

What does not work is writing it as research and hoping the question is not asked. **Standing rule:** nothing client-derived, ever, de-identified or not, in any public piece. Data collected from this point on is collected under a consent design that matches the chosen exit.

## How they sequence

| Step | Container | Work | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 *Ground* (now → 2027) | Independent | Four-literature paper submitted. Corpus stages 1–4 (preregistered). Scoping-review protocol registered. Make Space ethics route decided and screening begun | The Leeds application reads as a practitioner-researcher's |
| Phase 2 *Frame* (Sept 2027 → ~Aug 2029) | **Leeds Applied Project** (45 credits, Leeds as ethics host and supervisor, a DPO partner) | Corpus stages 5–8 finished and credited. The interview study the corpus cannot do: how disabled adults describe the ways learning design and requirements decided whether they were included. The Make Space study if exit 1 is chosen | The MA application: a supervised study, an academic referee, a partner on record |
| Phase 2 (2029 → 2030) | Independent | Scoping review to preprint. RA or lab apprenticeship with a UBC/SFU counselling faculty member. Supervisor conversation | The 1 Dec 2030 application |
| Stage 2 *The Room* (Sept 2031 →) | **MA thesis** (UBC, thesis-based) | The thesis question, tested: a survey of accommodation experience against validated depression and help-seeking measures, with interviews to explain the pattern (mixed methods) | The doctoral file: the first empirical answer |
| Stage 3 *The Question* (~2035 →) | **UBC PhD Counselling Psychology** | A dissertation testing the intervention the thesis points to, such as a joined access-and-mental-health intake or accessible low-intensity provision (creative health among the candidates). Feasibility study first, then hybrid effectiveness-implementation designs | Grants (CIHR for the depression side, SSHRC for the standards side), the joined service, a research programme |

**Thesis data and the dual role (verify first).** Studying the accommodation process at one's own employer raises a dual-role and conflict-of-interest question for the ethics board. Routes to settle with the prospective supervisor before applying: a study at another institution; recruitment through a student union or disabled students' association; a supervisor's existing dataset; or a national survey with disability items (check whether the Canadian NCHA carries them).

## The research-load tension, stated honestly

Phase 1 holds **three independent research projects**: the framework paper, the corpus study and the scoping review, plus the Make Space decision. They sit beside a full-time role, CPACC, crisis-line training, the advisor applications and the studio. Each project is sound on its own, but together they compete for the same best hours of the week. During Leeds the honest expectation is that the review moves only in module breaks: *"Expect this and it is a season; discover it and it feels like failure."*

**What resolves it** is a set of tasks, not a reason to aim lower:

- **Order, not simultaneity.** The framework paper is first, because it is short and needs no data. The corpus is next, because Leeds will finish it. The review runs at a floor of one stage a year until Leeds closes, then speeds up for 2029–30.
- **Let containers carry the load.** Leeds takes the corpus's back half and possibly Make Space. The MA takes the thesis. Nothing waits on an institution that could hold it.
- **Multiply with people.** The second coder (co-author), a standing practitioner reader for the review, an RA supervisor, and the Leeds supervisor. Each one turns solo hours into shared ones.
- **Decide one thing now:** whether the scoping review and the corpus study both need to be before-the-application pieces, or whether one is enough in print and the other is enough at preprint or registered-protocol stage. Log the decision either way.

**Fallback, once:** if the load does not fit, the scoping review drops to a registered protocol plus preprint by 2030, and its journal stage moves into the MA. The corpus study and the framework paper hold, because they are the Leeds and advisor-role evidence.
