# Requirement Validity

*Working reference for the knowledge resources that sit under the four-lens combination — what to call the area, what to build in it, and in what order. Companion to `essential-requirements.html`, which sets out the four tracks of work available now; this document covers what those tracks are for and what they produce that nothing else does.*

*Status: proposals under consideration, not decisions taken. Everything here is derived from the scarcity claim in the essential requirements document and should be revised as the corpus, the reading, and the conversations settle it.*

---

## 1. The scarcity, restated

Four bodies of knowledge almost never sit in one person:

| Lens | What it contributes to a decision |
|---|---|
| **Construct validity** (educational measurement, Messick) | Whether a requirement states the capability being assessed or one bodily route to it; whether the inference an assessment licenses survives an alternative method |
| **Duty to accommodate** (human rights law) | Whether a requirement is a *bona fide* occupational or academic requirement; whether the reasoning behind it is demonstrable, which is the procedural duty, not only whether the outcome is defensible |
| **Disability studies** (as critique) | Whose body and whose temporality the standard silently assumes; who defined the construct and who was absent when they did; whether the resource being built serves the institution or the person it lands on |
| **Professional programme design** | What breaks downstream when a requirement is rewritten — curriculum maps, assessment blueprints, placement agreements, accreditation submissions, progression rules |

Each has a separate faculty, literature, credential, and career ladder. No institutional role requires more than two, which is why the problems needing all four go unaddressed. The mapped practice reaches three of the four; the disability studies sources are cited by a different set of contributions entirely.

**The combination exists in the practitioner and not yet in any artifact.**

---

## 2. The naming

Three layers, doing different work. They should not be collapsed into one term.

### The concept — **requirement validity**

The teachable core. It parallels *construct validity* deliberately, which gives measurement people an immediate handhold, and it names a property that can be assessed rather than a stance that can be held.

*Is this requirement valid?* has an answerable structure: does it state the capability or one bodily route to it, and does the evidence support the inference it licenses. This is the term for the glossary, the course, and the first line of an abstract.

### The practice — **standards defensibility review**

Taken from the line already in the essential requirements document: a programme asking *are our standards defensible?*

Defensibility is the only word that spans all four lenses without strain. A standard is defensible when it:

1. measures what it claims to measure — *construct validity*
2. rests on stated authority rather than inheritance — *law*
3. does not silently encode a default body and a default temporality — *disability studies*
4. is connected to a curriculum in a way that lets it be revised — *programme design*

Four lenses, four conditions of a single property. This is a tighter formulation than "a synthesis of four literatures," and it is what a dean can repeat in a meeting she called for another reason.

### The field — **competence standards**

Borrow the UK term deliberately. It is the only jurisdiction that has given this object its own legal category, so the phrase already denotes precisely the thing under study, and using it makes the work legible at Leeds from the first sentence.

*Competence standards studies* as the scholarly name. *Essential requirements* stays as the North American operational term the corpus is built from. Holding both is an asset rather than a muddle: the terminological split across jurisdictions is itself a finding, and being the person who works in both vocabularies is part of the position.

### The tension, and what to do with it

**Defensibility is an institution-protective word.** To a *Disability & Society* readership it will sound, on first hearing, like consulting that helps programmes insulate themselves against challenge. That reading is not paranoid — most of the resources below are institutional tools.

Two responses, and both are needed:

- **The criterion cuts hard in one direction.** The same test that protects a genuine safety requirement dismantles an inherited one, and the corpus is likely to show that most are inherited.
- **The critique-facing work carries a different name,** because it does different work. There the object is not defensibility but its absence — the standard as an unexamined artifact of who the profession assumed would arrive. *The default body in professional standards* is closer to that register. It is a paper title rather than a field name, which is fine: fields need one name, arguments can have several.

### The three-room test

Say each candidate aloud in three rooms before committing:

| Room | Sentence |
|---|---|
| A health professions dean | *I review whether your essential requirements are defensible.* |
| A journal editor | *This is a paper on requirement validity in Canadian professional programmes.* |
| A disabled applicant reading the public guide | *This explains what a technical standards document can and cannot ask of you.* |

*Requirement validity* survives the first two and is opaque in the third. *Standards defensibility* survives the first two and sounds like the institution's lawyer in the third — which is why the applicant-facing resource should not use the field's vocabulary at all.

### Names rejected, and why

- **The four-literature framework** — serviceable internally, fatal publicly. Fields named after their inputs never recruit; *biopsychosocial* is the standing warning. Name the object, not the synthesis.
- **Gatekeeping** — sociologically accurate, and it closes the door the essential requirements document is careful to keep open. Makes the first meeting adversarial.
- **Inclusive assessment / accessible assessment** — already occupied by adjacent literatures, and would bury the distinctive claim under work that is mostly about exam adjustments.
- **Critical [anything]** — the prefix signals allegiance to one of the four families and quietly tells the other three whose paper this is.

### The relocation that is the actual novelty

Everyone else studies the adjustment made for an individual. This studies **the standard the individual is being measured against**. The unit of analysis moves from the accommodation to the requirement. Any name that does not carry that relocation is the wrong name.

## 3. The organising principle: build instruments, not arguments

An argument can reach three-and-a-half of the four literatures without anyone noticing. A paper can gesture at disability studies in its framing and do its real work in measurement and law. **An instrument cannot** — a tool that has to produce a decision fails visibly when a literature is missing, because the missing one is where the decision quietly goes wrong.

This is probably the real reason nothing in the mapped practice reaches all four: the artifacts built so far are argumentative, and argumentative artifacts can be silently partial.

**The test for any candidate resource:** if one of the four lenses is deleted, does the *output* change, or only the framing? If only the framing changes, it is an argument wearing a tool's clothes.

## 4. The resources

Seven. Several are by-products of the corpus study rather than competing projects.

---

### 1. The reframing bank — a construct/method translation library

**What it is.** For each requirement that recurs across professions — auscultate breath sounds, lift 50 lbs unassisted, sufficient visual acuity, manual dexterity, tolerate a twelve-hour shift, communicate effectively in spoken English — five fields:

1. the requirement as commonly written
2. the construct it plausibly serves
3. whether the stated method is the only route to that construct
4. documented instances of practitioners performing it another way
5. a construct-level rewrite

**Why it needs all four.** Measurement supplies the construct/method distinction. Law supplies why an uncited blanket method is indefensible rather than merely inelegant. Disability studies and the disabled-practitioner literature supply the existence proofs that break the *but it is genuinely necessary* reflex. Programme design supplies a rewrite that survives contact with an assessment blueprint.

**How it is built.** It is the code-1 coding output re-sorted. Sorting time, not research time.

**What it feeds.** Infrastructure others cite and use without reading the papers — a faster route into a field than an article. The evidence base for every consulting conversation. Versioned, CC-BY, deposited on OSF.

---

### 2. The review protocol — the corpus frame turned prospective

**What it is.** The seven codes are descriptive: they characterise what programmes did. Inverted, the same codes become a staged review a programme runs on its own document, with decision rules rather than observations.

1. **Extract the construct** — what capability, at what level, evidenced how
2. **Trace the authority** — regulator, accreditor, or convention; does it meet the *bona fide* requirement test
3. **Audit the default body** — sensory, motor, and stature specifications; temporal assumptions; whose voice was in the room when it was drafted
4. **Trace the system consequence** — which curriculum maps, assessment blueprints, placement agreements, and progression rules move if this requirement is rewritten

**Why stage 4 matters.** It is where programme design becomes load-bearing rather than decorative. A framework that tells a faculty its standards are indefensible without telling it what breaks when they change is a framework it will thank you for and ignore.

**Output.** A redrafted document plus a defensibility record.

**What it feeds.** Simultaneously a methods paper, the consulting deliverable, and the spine of the free facilitated session in Track D.

---

### 3. The validity-argument decision tool for individual accommodations

**What it is.** One page. It reframes the accommodation question as a measurement question — *does this alternative method change the inference the assessment supports?* — with three outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Construct-irrelevant** | The objection has no measurement basis. Accommodate. |
| **Construct-relevant but alternately achievable** | The assessment needs redesign; the student does not need an exception. |
| **Genuinely construct-central** | Rare. When true, state it plainly with its evidence. |

**Why it needs all four.** Undue-hardship jurisprudence tells you *when* you must accommodate but is silent on whether the accommodation destroys the measurement. Measurement theory answers that but carries no obligation. Disability studies is what stops the tool from treating the construct as given — someone chose it, and their assumptions are in it. Programme design is what makes the middle outcome actionable rather than aspirational.

**Why it matters.** This is the narrow, high-frequency artifact, and the one that breaks the safety-versus-access standoff advisors actually live in. No one has written it.

---

### 4. The defensibility file — a records schema for revisable standards

**What it is.** Code 6 will find that almost nothing carries a revision record. The complementary artifact is the record format itself. Per requirement:

- the construct it states
- its authority
- the evidence behind it
- review date and reviewers, including disabled practitioners
- accommodations already granted against it, and what happened

**Why it matters more than it looks.** It satisfies the procedural duty to accommodate, which requires demonstrable reasoning and not merely a defensible outcome. It creates longitudinal data that currently exists nowhere, because every institution re-decides from scratch and keeps nothing. And it is the mechanism that makes a standard revisable rather than inherited — which is the whole argument, rendered as a file format.

---

### 5. The case library

**What it is.** Anonymised decisions in a fixed template: requirement, construct claim, proposed alternative, four-lens analysis, decision, outcome.

**Why it needs all four.** Each case requires all four to write, which is exactly what makes the library a demonstration that the combination is transmissible rather than resident in one person.

**What it feeds.** Teaching material for all four communities; the raw material for the course, the book, and conference sessions.

---

### 6. The short course

**What it is.** Training for accommodation advisors and programme directors in the four-way reasoning.

**Why it is the resource that closes the scarcity.** If no institutional role requires more than two of the four, the answer is not to become the rare person who holds all four — it is to build the training system that distributes the reasoning to people who hold two. This is precisely the Accessible Learning Labs thesis (designing the training system that delivers the thing matters as much as the thing) and precisely the task-shifting logic applied to your own expertise.

It is also the strongest evidence that the combination is real: **a body of knowledge you can teach is one you have actually formalised.**

---

### 7. The applicant-facing guide — the deliberate counterweight

**What it is.** How to read a technical standards document: what it can and cannot lawfully require, what the self-assessment request actually is, what to ask and of whom.

**Why it is not optional.** Every resource above serves institutions. A four-literature practice that produces only institutional tools has answered the critique's question in the critique's absence. Code 3 already identifies these documents as disclosure events; the disability studies half of the framework obliges building something for the people on whom that event lands.

**How.** Built with disabled applicants rather than for them. In plain language, not the field's vocabulary. It also supplies the answer to the question the CJDS and CDSA audiences will ask first.

## 5. Order of build

| When | What | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | The reframing bank and the defensibility schema | By-products of coding the corpus rather than additional projects. Sorting time, not research time. Either is publishable infrastructure before the paper clears review. |
| **Then** | The review protocol | Wait until the first twenty documents have shown which codes need sub-categories — code 1 will need them, code 3 will need a fifth value. |
| **Alongside** | The validity-argument decision tool | Pure desk work, no data dependency. Testable against real disputes immediately. |
| **Then** | The applicant-facing guide | Needs co-authors, so it needs the network to exist first. Do not defer it past that point. |
| **Once ten cases exist** | The case library, then the course | A course built before the cases teaches a framework rather than a practice. |

## 6. Relationship to the four tracks

| Track (`essential-requirements.html`) | What it produces here |
|---|---|
| **A — the corpus study** | Directly generates resources 1 and 4 as by-products; supplies the empirical basis for 2 |
| **B — writing with no data** | The framework piece is where *requirement validity* is named and defined; the three-jurisdiction comparison is where *competence standards* is claimed as the field |
| **C — publish what exists** | The four drafted contributions become the citable ground everything above stands on |
| **D — the four ladders** | Supplies the co-authors for resource 7 and the test site for resource 2 |

Nothing above replaces a track. The tracks are how the work gets done; these are what it accumulates into.

## 7. Cautions

**Instruments are easier to overclaim than arguments.** A protocol implies it has been tried; a decision tool implies its three outcomes carve the space correctly. Run each against real disputes — the free facilitated session is exactly this test — before publishing it as a tool rather than a proposal. Calling the first version *a proposed protocol, untested* costs nothing and protects the second version's standing.

**The institution-protective framing is a real risk, not a perceived one.** Resource 7 is the structural answer, not a disclaimer in the introduction.

**The UK may have got here first.** The competence standards carve-out has generated guidance and case law Canada has no equivalent of. If the framework already exists there, this becomes translation and adaptation rather than origination — still worth doing, a different claim, and a different name.

**The knowledge may exist unpublished.** Ask three experienced advisors how they handle an essential-requirements dispute and listen for whether the four literatures are already in the room.

## 8. Open questions

- Does *requirement validity* survive contact with a measurement specialist, or does it read as a category error to someone who holds construct validity precisely?
- Is the three-outcome structure of resource 3 exhaustive, or is there a fourth case — the requirement that is construct-central but whose construct is itself indefensible?
- Where does the applicant-facing guide live so that it is found by the people who need it rather than only by the field?
- Does the field name need to be claimed publicly at all before the corpus paper exists, or does claiming it early invite the objection that it is one person's coinage?
