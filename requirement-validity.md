# Requirement Validity

*Working reference for the knowledge resources that sit under the four-lens combination — what to call the area, what to build in it, and in what order.*

*Companion to `constitutive-and-customary.md` (the framework, impact, scope) and `essential-requirements.html` (the tracks of work available now).*

---

**First revision — August 2026 (the AFMC correction).** This document was first written against a premise of empty ground: that no artifact anywhere combined the four literatures. **That premise was wrong**, and the correction is recorded in section 1. The resource list was reranked accordingly, verification status is recorded per resource, and one resource was added that the original list could not have contained.

**Second revision — August 2026 (the landscape update).** The practice map was widened to its full scope: ninety-four individually sourced contributions across a trunk of shared craft and five branches. Three things follow. The four-lens claim **survives the larger test** and is now demonstrable rather than asserted (section 1). A **second residual gap** appears in the AFMC bibliography alongside the measurement one, and it is the more immediately actionable of the two (section 1). And the map supplies a **second tier of knowledge** that does not change what requirement validity *is*, but decides whether anything built here is ever used — which reranks the list again, moves one resource up, and surfaces a legal constraint on resource 2 that the original schema would have failed on (sections 3 and 4).

Nothing here is a decision taken. Five checks in section 7 should happen before any of it is built.

## 1. The scarcity, corrected — and now tested

Four bodies of knowledge rarely sit in one person:

| Lens | What it contributes to a decision |
|---|---|
| **Construct validity** (educational measurement, Messick) | Whether a requirement states the capability being assessed or one bodily route to it; whether the inference an assessment licenses survives an alternative method |
| **Duty to accommodate** (human rights law) | Whether a requirement is a *bona fide* requirement; whether the reasoning is demonstrable — the procedural duty, not only a defensible outcome |
| **Disability studies** (as critique) | Whose body and whose temporality the standard assumes; who defined the construct and who was absent; whether the resource serves the institution or the person it lands on |
| **Professional programme design** | What breaks downstream when a requirement is rewritten — curriculum maps, assessment blueprints, placement agreements, accreditation, progression rules |

### What was found to be false

The original version of this document claimed the combination existed in the practitioner and not yet in any artifact. In May 2025 the Association of Faculties of Medicine of Canada published *Re-envisioning Technical Standards in Undergraduate Medical Education in Canada*. It distinguishes **organic** standards (motor and sensory methods) from **functional** standards (the ability required, with or without accommodation) — the construct/method distinction under other names. It cites the *bona fide* requirement test, the Accessible Canada Act and BC case law; it draws on critical disability scholarship including Jain's capability imperative; it had legal counsel and disabled physicians on the working group.

Three and a half of the four literatures, in one artifact, with a date on it.

### What the widened map now establishes

The *practitioner* half of the original claim was never tested; it was illustrated against a small map and taken as confirmed. It can now be tested properly. Across ninety-four individually sourced contributions:

- The essential-requirements and clinical-placement contributions cite Messick, *Meiorin*, and Wiggins & McTighe. **Three lenses.**
- The disclosure-burden contribution cites Goffman and Dolmage. **Disability studies, no measurement.**
- The models-of-disability and disability-studies contributions cite Oliver, Titchkosky, Kafer and Shakespeare. **Critique, no law, no measurement.**
- **No contribution cites all four families.**

The combination is in the practitioner and in no artifact — and that is now a statement about ninety-four sourced contributions rather than an impression. Record the count and the date; it will change as contributions are published and merged.

### What the residual gap actually is

**First: the measurement apparatus.** The AFMC bibliography contains no validity theory — no Messick, no validity argument, no construct-irrelevant variance. The distinction is reached by analogy from competency-based education and held as a policy preference rather than as a property that can be tested.

Meanwhile the measurement literature that *does* formalise it — Sireci, Thurlow, the NCEO accommodations work — has never been pointed at professional entry standards and contains no disability studies. The two clusters do not cite each other.

**Second: the mechanism.** AFMC invites other health professions to adopt parallel approaches and supplies no means of doing so. The literature that would supply one — CFIR, EPIS, PARIHS — appears in that bibliography no more than Messick does. A reform document with no implementation theory produces a document other professions admire and do not copy.

**Two demonstrable absences from a single bibliography** is a smaller claim than the original and a far better one, because it is provable from two bibliographies rather than asserted from inside. It is also more collaborative: it names what an existing working group would need in order to finish something it started, rather than what it failed to think of.

### The second opening

That invitation, with no mechanism attached, is the strongest practical warrant for the whole programme of work. Every profession outside medicine is both un-reformed and un-analysed, and the body that could have reformed them has publicly asked them to move.

## 2. The naming

Unaffected by both corrections. Three layers, doing different work.

### The concept — **requirement validity**
The teachable core. Parallels *construct validity* deliberately, giving measurement people a handhold, and names a property that can be assessed rather than a stance that can be held. Glossary, course, first line of the abstract.

### The practice — **standards defensibility review**
A standard is defensible when it (1) measures what it claims, (2) rests on stated authority rather than inheritance, (3) does not silently encode a default body and temporality, and (4) is connected to a curriculum in a way that lets it be revised. Four lenses, four conditions of one property — repeatable by a dean in a meeting she called for another reason.

### The field — **competence standards**
Borrow the UK term deliberately: the only jurisdiction that has given this object its own legal category, which makes the work legible at Leeds immediately. *Essential requirements* stays as the North American operational term the corpus is built from. Holding both is an asset; the terminological split is itself a finding.

### The tension
Defensibility is an institution-protective word and will read that way to a *Disability & Society* audience. Two responses, both needed: the criterion cuts hard in one direction (the same test that protects a real safety requirement dismantles an inherited one), and the critique-facing work carries a different name — *the default body in professional standards* is a paper title, not a field name, which is fine.

### The three-room test
To a dean: *I review whether your essential requirements are defensible.* To an editor: *a paper on requirement validity in Canadian professional programmes.* To a disabled applicant: *this explains what a technical standards document can and cannot ask of you.* The third is why the applicant-facing resource must not use the field's vocabulary.

### Rejected
*The four-literature framework* (fields named after inputs never recruit; biopsychosocial is the warning). *Gatekeeping* (closes the door). *Inclusive/accessible assessment* (occupied by exam-adjustment literatures). *Critical [anything]* (signals allegiance to one of the four).

### The relocation that is the novelty
Everyone else studies the adjustment made for an individual. This studies **the standard the individual is measured against**.

## 3. The organising principle

An argument can reach three and a half of four without anyone noticing. **An instrument cannot** — a tool that must produce a decision fails visibly where a literature is missing.

**The first test:** delete one lens. Does the *output* change, or only the framing?

**The reranking principle, first revision.** Each resource drew value from one of two sources: *nobody has built anything like this*, or *nobody has the measurement lens*. The second is confirmed; the first is disproved. Resources resting on measurement strengthened. Resources resting on general absence shrank.

### The second tier, added in the landscape revision

The widened map shows the practice holds far more than four literatures: implementation science, knowledge mobilization, plain language, research ethics, privacy law, behaviour change, assistive technology frameworks, disability prevalence measurement, evaluation design. The temptation is to promote some of these to fifth and sixth lenses. **Resist it.** Run the first test on them and they fail: delete implementation science and the judgment about whether a requirement is defensible does not change at all.

They belong in a second tier, governing something different:

| Body | What it decides |
|---|---|
| **Implementation science** (CFIR · EPIS · PARIHS) | Whether a reformed standard is adopted, by whom, and what stalls it |
| **Knowledge mobilization** (Graham et al.; KMb evaluation) | Whether the instruments reach the people who could use them, and whether use can be measured |
| **Plain language** (ISO 24495-1) | Whether the applicant-facing work is readable by applicants, and whether a rewritten standard is readable by the person it governs |
| **Behaviour change** (COM-B) | Whether a training module changes practice or only awareness |
| **Privacy law** (FIPPA · PIPEDA · PHIPA) | What a records schema holding accommodation outcomes is lawfully allowed to contain |

**The second test, for the second tier:** delete one of these and the instrument still produces the right answer — but nobody uses it, nobody can read it, or it cannot lawfully be built. That is a different kind of failure and it needs naming separately, because the first tier is about being right and the second is about being used.

**The reranking that follows.** Resources whose constraint is *adoption* rather than *correctness* move up, because the second tier is exactly what the AFMC document lacks and what nothing else in this space supplies. Resource 4 is the main beneficiary. Resource 2 acquires a legal constraint it did not have. Resource 7 acquires a diagnosis.

### 1. The reframing bank — construct/method translation library
**STRENGTHENED · build first · one check outstanding**

For each requirement recurring across professions — auscultate breath sounds, lift 50 lbs unassisted, sufficient visual acuity, manual dexterity, tolerate a twelve-hour shift, communicate effectively in spoken English — six fields: the requirement as commonly written; the construct it plausibly serves; whether the stated method is the only route; documented instances of practitioners doing it another way; a construct-level rewrite; **and the rewrite in plain language.**

**Why all four:** measurement supplies the distinction; law supplies why an uncited blanket method is indefensible rather than inelegant; disability studies and the disabled-practitioner literature supply the existence proofs that break the *but it's genuinely necessary* reflex; programme design supplies a rewrite that survives an assessment blueprint.

**Why the sixth field, added this revision:** a construct-level rewrite that clears an assessment blueprint and cannot be read by the applicant it governs has moved the barrier rather than removed it. The trunk's plain-language contribution and ISO 24495-1 make this a checkable field rather than a good intention, and the corpus study's reading-load code produces the baseline it is measured against.

**Why it strengthened:** AFMC invited other professions to adopt functional standards and gave them no instrument. This is that instrument. Cross-profession, which nothing existing is.

**Build:** the code-1 output re-sorted, with the code-10 measure attached. Sorting time, not research time. Versioned, CC-BY, OSF.

> **Verification — outstanding.** Not checked against Meeks, Jain & Laird, *Equal Access for Students with Disabilities* (2nd ed., Springer, 2021), distributed free, which may contain a partial version. **Read before building.**

### 2. The defensibility file — records schema for revisable standards
**STRENGTHENED · build second · the sleeper · now with a legal constraint**

Per requirement: the construct it states, its authority, the evidence behind it, review date and reviewers including disabled practitioners, accommodations already granted and what happened.

**Why it strengthened:** professions following the AFMC invitation will have to show their reasoning — the procedural duty requires demonstrable reasoning, not merely a defensible outcome — and nobody has given them a format. The AFMC document is itself versioned and dated, which proves the concept once and no further.

**Why it matters more than it looks:** it creates longitudinal data existing nowhere, because every institution re-decides from scratch and keeps nothing. It is the mechanism that makes a standard revisable rather than inherited — the whole argument, rendered as a file format.

**Why the map already holds the practice:** two trunk contributions — designing for the update cycle, and quality assurance as a documented practice — are this schema's operating discipline in general form. Named owner, review trigger, version, recorded reviewer, criteria written down before the review rather than after. The schema is an instance of a practice already articulated, which is a considerably better position than inventing a records format from nothing.

**The constraint the original schema would have failed on.** *Accommodations already granted and what happened* is personal information about identifiable students. FIPPA, PIPEDA and PHIPA govern its collection, retention and disclosure, and an institution cannot lawfully keep an open-ended longitudinal file of accommodation outcomes attached to a public-facing standards document. The design has to separate two layers: a **requirement-level record**, which is institutional and publishable, and an **outcome record**, which is de-identified, aggregated, retention-limited, and probably belongs with the accommodation office rather than the programme. The map's contributions on learner data and analytics ethics and on designing out the disclosure requirement are the source for both halves. This is not a caveat to add in an appendix — it is a structural decision that has to be made before the first field is drawn, and getting it wrong turns an accountability instrument into a surveillance one.

> **Verification — outstanding.** No search run for existing accommodation-decision record schemas, and no privacy review run at all. **One search and one privacy read before building.**

### 3. The validity-argument decision tool
**PROBABLY INTACT · novelty claim was overstated · check before writing**

One page. Reframes the accommodation question as a measurement question — *does this alternative method change the inference the assessment supports?*

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Construct-irrelevant** | The objection has no measurement basis. Accommodate. |
| **Construct-relevant, alternately achievable** | The assessment needs redesign; the student does not need an exception. |
| **Genuinely construct-central** | Rare. When true, state it plainly with its evidence. |

**Why all four:** undue-hardship jurisprudence says *when* you must accommodate, not whether the accommodation destroys the measurement. Measurement answers that but carries no obligation. Disability studies stops the tool treating the construct as given. Programme design makes the middle outcome actionable.

**The correction:** the original text read *"one page, and no one has written it."* That was asserted without checking. The OIA publishes a competence standards checklist and the EHRC has technical guidance on adjusting how a standard is assessed. Those are legal-reasoning tools and the construct-keyed three-outcome structure still appears absent — but *appears absent* is the phrase that caused the error this document exists to correct.

**Unchanged by the landscape revision.** The tool is a judgment instrument, first tier only, and the second tier has nothing to add to it. That is itself mildly reassuring about the tier distinction.

> **Verification — outstanding and load-bearing.** Check the OIA competence standards checklist and Advance HE's *Disabled Student Commitment: Competence standards and reasonable adjustments* **before writing a line.** If it survives, this is the most distinctive item on the list.

### 4. The translation kit — how a non-medical profession runs the AFMC move
**NEW IN THE FIRST REVISION · STRENGTHENED AGAIN · now arguably the second build**

Profession-agnostic: what to extract, whom to consult, how to phrase functional standards, what changes downstream, what record to keep. Assembled from resources 1, 2 and 3 into one product.

**Why it exists:** AFMC issued an invitation and supplied no mechanism. This is the mechanism.

**Why the landscape revision moves it up.** The first revision justified this resource by pointing at a hole. The second identifies what fills it: adoption of a reformed standard is an implementation problem, and implementation science is a body of knowledge held in this practice, sourced, and absent from the AFMC bibliography. That changes the kit from *a sensible bundling of three other resources* into **the only item on this list whose core value comes from a literature nobody else in this space is carrying.** Resources 1 through 3 could each, in principle, be produced by a sufficiently motivated measurement person or accommodation professional. This one could not.

**What that means structurally:** stop treating it as an assembly of the three preceding resources and give it a spine of its own. EPIS is the obvious candidate — exploration, preparation, implementation, sustainment — because it maps directly onto what a faculty actually does: discovering the standard is indefensible, deciding to rewrite, rewriting, and keeping it revisable. Resources 1, 2 and 3 then slot in as the tools used at particular phases rather than as the kit's contents. CFIR supplies the barrier taxonomy for why it stalls. Sustainment is where resource 2 lives, and naming it that way is what stops the schema reading as bureaucracy.

**What it replaces:** the review protocol as the consulting deliverable. It is also the thing that gets you into a room with CASN or a nursing faculty.

**Framing, non-negotiable:** an extension of the AFMC approach into professions it invited, grounded in the measurement theory *and* the implementation theory it lacks. Cite AFMC and Meeks from the first draft.

> **Verification — new and outstanding.** Nobody has checked whether implementation guidance has been published since the AFMC document, by AFMC, the AAMC, or the Coalition for Disability Access. This is the same failure mode that produced the first revision, arriving in a new place. **One search before writing.**

### 5. The applicant-facing guide — the counterweight
**UNCHANGED IN STATUS · argument strengthened twice · craft now available**

How to read a technical standards document: what it can and cannot lawfully require, what the self-assessment request actually is, what to ask and of whom.

**Why unaffected by either revision:** never justified on novelty. Justified because a four-literature practice producing only institutional tools has answered the critique in the critique's absence.

**Why it strengthened, first revision:** AFMC recommends admissions websites let applicants see possible accommodations *without personal disclosure* — an institutional-side acknowledgement of exactly the disclosure-event problem the corpus study's disclosure code names. The guide has a citable warrant.

**Why it strengthened again:** the map holds a drafted contribution treating disclosure burden as a measurable design variable rather than an unfortunate necessity — why a flexible deadline requiring an email is a disclosure event and a built-in buffer is not. That is the guide's analytic spine. And the trunk's plain-language contribution is its craft: this is the one resource on the list where *reading load* is not a nice-to-have but the entire point, since its readers are applicants under time pressure making a decision about disclosure.

**How:** with disabled applicants, not for them. Plain language, not the field's vocabulary. Co-authorship means it waits on the network — but not past that point.

---

### 6. The case library
**SHRANK · moves later · constraint was never novelty**

Anonymised decisions in a fixed template: requirement, construct claim, proposed alternative, four-lens analysis, decision, outcome.

**Partial competition:** the Docs With Disabilities podcast functions as an informal case library; the Jarus group produces case-based educator resources including research-based theatre. Structured four-lens written cases with decisions and outcomes were not found.

**The real constraint:** access, not originality. You cannot write cases without real disputes, which means the free facilitated sessions happen first.

**Added this revision:** the same consent and anonymisation problem as resource 2, in a sharper form. A case is a real dispute involving a real identifiable person in a small population, and anonymisation that works in a large sample fails in a nursing cohort of sixty. The trunk's ethics-and-consent contribution is the governing standard, and consent for publication has to be obtained at the session rather than sought afterwards.

### 7. The short course
**DEFER SUBSTANTIALLY · build last · but the reframing is now sharper**

**Most threatened.** Meeks co-developed the AAMC disability webinar series; the Coalition for Disability Access trains accommodation professionals; in the adjacent workforce, WHO/UNICEF's EQUIP is a full competency-based training platform with assessment tools and a digital delivery system. *Training people in this* is occupied.

**What isn't:** training in the measurement reasoning specifically. That is a **module**, not a course.

**Added this revision.** The map holds a contribution on faculty development that changes accessible practice, built on COM-B: institutions have run accessibility workshops for years with limited change in daily teaching, and the question is whether the gap is capability, opportunity, or motivation. Applied here, that reframes the whole item. *Training people in essential requirements* is occupied; **diagnosing why the existing training has not changed how standards are written is not.** That is a smaller, more interesting, and considerably more defensible thing to build — and it is a paper before it is a module.

**The original argument still holds** — a body of knowledge you can teach is one you have formalised — but it is now an argument for building it last, not soon.

## 7. Five checks before building anything

Roughly three hours. Each bears on specific resources. Two are new in this revision.

1. **Meeks, Jain & Laird, *Equal Access* (2nd ed.) and the technical standards chapter.** Highest-value single read. Bears on resources 1, 4 and 7.
2. **OIA competence standards checklist; Advance HE competence standards guidance.** Bears on resource 3, which is the item most exposed to an unchecked novelty claim.
3. **One search for existing accommodation-decision record schemas.** Bears on resource 2.
4. **One search for implementation guidance published since the AFMC document** — by AFMC, the AAMC, or the Coalition for Disability Access. *New.* Bears on resource 4, which is now second in the build order on the strength of an unchecked absence.
5. **One read on personal-information retention in an accommodation context** — FIPPA and the relevant provincial health information act. *New.* Bears on resources 2 and 6, and is a design constraint rather than a compliance step.

If these come back clean, four resources are unaffected, one is new, and one has a structure it did not have before. If they don't, better to know before the sorting is done than after.

## 8. Cautions

**Instruments are easier to overclaim than arguments.** A protocol implies it has been tried; a decision tool implies its three outcomes carve the space correctly. Test against real disputes before publishing as a tool rather than a proposal. *A proposed protocol, untested* costs nothing and protects the second version.

**Every novelty claim needs its check attached.** The failure the first revision corrects was generated in a single conversation by an intelligent process reasoning from the inside. It will recur — and it did, in this revision, in the form of an unchecked claim that AFMC's invitation has still gone unanswered. Any resource added to this list in future arrives with a verification line or does not arrive.

**The institution-protective framing is a real risk.** Resource 5 is the structural answer, not a disclaimer in an introduction. An asymmetrically distributed instrument becomes an institutional weapon by default rather than by intent — so the protocol must *require* disabled reviewers rather than recommend them.

**A records schema is a surveillance instrument until it is designed not to be.** New in this revision, and the most consequential thing the landscape update surfaced. Resource 2 is the item most likely to be adopted enthusiastically by an institution for the wrong reason. Separate the requirement-level record from the outcome record before drawing a single field.

**The map is not the evidence.** The four-lens claim is now testable against ninety-four sourced contributions, which is a real improvement over an impression — but the map is a self-description, and a self-description showing that its author holds an unusual combination is exactly what an author would produce. It rules out the weak version of the claim (no artifact combines them) and it cannot rule in the strong one. Only the practitioner conversations in the falsification list can do that.

**Extension is not a lesser claim.** Positioning this as an extension of AFMC and Meeks rather than as origination is more accurate, more collaborative, more fundable, and more likely to be adopted. It is also simply true.

## 9. Open questions

- Does *requirement validity* survive contact with a measurement specialist, or read as a category error to someone who holds construct validity precisely?
- Is the three-outcome structure exhaustive, or is there a fourth case — the requirement that is construct-central but whose construct is itself indefensible?
- Which non-medical profession is the right first target for the translation kit? Ontario's shared nursing document is the strongest candidate: one artifact governing an entire province, with an explicit independence clause.
- Where does the applicant-facing guide live so the people who need it find it, rather than only the field?
- Should the field name be claimed publicly at all before the corpus exists?
- **Has any other profession already run its own version of the AFMC move?** This risk was unanticipated once and materialised. Check per profession, not once.
- **Is EPIS the right spine for the translation kit, or is CFIR?** EPIS is phase-based and matches what a faculty does; CFIR is domain-based and matches why it stalls. The kit probably needs one as structure and the other as diagnostic, and choosing wrongly makes it unusable rather than merely imperfect.
- **Is the real claim about combination, or about infrastructure?** *New.* A mapped, individually sourced practice of this size may be rarer than the four-lens combination it was built to demonstrate. If so, the method is the contribution and the corpus study is its first application — a larger claim, reached honestly. The test is in the falsification list in `essential-requirements.html`, and it is unrun.
